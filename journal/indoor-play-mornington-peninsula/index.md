# Indoor Play on the Mornington Peninsula: The Four Worth Driving To

When the weather turns and the kids need to run, there are four indoor play venues on the Peninsula that actually hold up. Here is what each one is good for, and the five things to check before you leave the house.

There is a particular kind of Peninsula weather - grey, wet, and not committed enough to be dramatic - that turns a normal Saturday into a problem. The beach is out. The garden is out. The kids have been circling the kitchen since eight.

Four indoor venues on the Peninsula solve that problem properly. They are not interchangeable, and choosing on distance alone is how you end up with a toddler in a room built for nine-year-olds.

## Kidz Shed, Hastings

An independent play centre and cafe, locally owned, running since 2018. The draw is the separation: younger children get their own space rather than being absorbed into the traffic of older kids on the climbing frame. There is a cafe on site with proper food, which matters more than it sounds - the difference between a good visit and a short one is usually whether a parent can sit down.

Best for: mixed-age families where the youngest needs somewhere safe to be left alone for a minute.

## Play at the Messy Shed, Mornington

Creative and sensory play rather than climbing. Paint, mess, materials, process over product - the point is what the child does, not what they take home. It relocated to Mornington from Tyabb and runs sessions alongside a play space.

Best for: preschoolers, and for any child who finds a big loud play centre overwhelming. Also for parents who would rather the mess happened somewhere other than their kitchen.

## Inflatable Land, Mornington

Housed inside Mornington Indoor Sports on Milgate Drive. Slides, obstacle courses, foam pits, and a soft zone for smaller kids, with a licensed cafe attached. This is the physical option - the one for children who need to be genuinely tired by lunchtime.

Best for: older kids, big groups, and birthday parties. It runs structured party packages, which book out.

## We Rock the Spectrum, Somerville

Purpose-built for children on the autism spectrum: suspended swings for vestibular input, crash mats, a zip line, and structured programs rather than open chaos. It is the only Peninsula venue designed around sensory needs from the outset instead of retrofitting for them, and it is open to all children.

Best for: sensory-sensitive kids, and families who have found that mainstream play centres do not work for them.

## Five things to check before you leave

Every one of these venues changes its hours between school term and holidays, and none of them appreciate a carload arriving to a locked door.

- **Hours, today.** Check the venue’s own website or Facebook page rather than a directory listing. Directory hours go stale faster than anything else on the internet.

- **Whether you need to book.** School holidays, weekends, and anything involving a party room generally require it.

- **Age zones.** Ask specifically whether there is a separated area for under-fives if you are bringing one.

- **Socks and food.** Most Peninsula venues require socks for everyone entering the play area, including adults. Outside food policies vary - some welcome it, some do not.

- **Access.** If you need step-free entry, a baby change table, or accessible parking, confirm it by phone. Do not assume.

Pricing at all four is tiered by age and changes without much notice. Treat any figure you find online - including on a directory site - as indicative, and confirm at the door or by phone.

## Making a day of it

Indoor play is a two-hour solution, not a day. It works best as the first move: burn the energy in the morning, eat somewhere that will tolerate a tired four-year-old, then let the afternoon be quiet. ## FAQ

What indoor play centres are on the Mornington Peninsula?

The four established options are Kidz Shed in Hastings, Play at the Messy Shed in Mornington, Inflatable Land at Mornington Indoor Sports, and We Rock the Spectrum in Somerville. Each covers a different age range and play style rather than competing directly.

Which Peninsula play centre is best for toddlers?

Kidz Shed and the Messy Shed both cater to younger children - Kidz Shed with a separated play area away from older climbers, the Messy Shed with creative and sensory play rather than climbing equipment. Inflatable Land suits bigger, more physical kids.

Is there a sensory-friendly play centre on the Peninsula?

We Rock the Spectrum in Somerville is purpose-built for children on the autism spectrum, with suspended and sensory equipment and structured sessions. It is the only venue on the Peninsula designed around that need rather than adapting to it.

When are Peninsula play centres quietest?

Mid-week mornings during school term. School holidays bring longer hours at some venues but significantly bigger crowds, and party rooms book out well ahead.
