# Portsea Hotel's beer garden, the Point Nepean orchid flush, and Red Hill Truffles' last hunts

The Portsea Hotel deck in first-spring warmth, the native orchids peaking along Point Nepean's Number 2 Rifle Range track, and the final truffle hunts of the season at Red Hill Truffles in Main Ridge.

**EAT / DRINK - Portsea Hotel, Portsea**

The front beer garden faces Port Phillip at the exact moment the water starts to look blue again rather than grey. September at the Portsea Hotel is the version of this pub that rewards timing: the summer crowds haven’t arrived, the deck furniture is out, and the kitchen is still running the fish and chips that make this place genuinely hard to argue with.

Order the battered flathead if it’s on the board. The portion is honest, the view does the rest of the work, and the whole experience has a directness that fancier rooms along the Peninsula struggle to replicate. The beer list is unremarkable, which is fine - you are here for Port Phillip through the railing, not for a tap handle.

Book a table or arrive before noon on Saturday. The Portsea Hotel is on Point Nepean Road, right on Portsea front beach. Allow two hours minimum; it’s easy to stay longer.

Pair it with a walk to the Portsea back beach through London Bridge before lunch - twenty minutes each way and worth every step.

**EXPERIENCE / WALK - Number 2 Rifle Range Track, Point Nepean National Park**

The native orchid flush along the Number 2 Rifle Range track inside Point Nepean National Park peaks in mid-to-late September, and this weekend sits inside that window. Donkey orchids, spider orchids, and sun orchids appear in the low scrub between the track and the old quarantine station boundary - easy to miss if you’re moving fast, obvious once you slow down.

The track runs from the Visitors Precinct toward the rifle range ruins, roughly 3km return on flat ground. Go in the morning, before the westerly picks up off Bass Strait. The light through the coastal scrub before 10am is worth the early start on its own. The orchids are gone within two weeks of the warmth arriving in October, so this weekend is the right weekend.

Enter via Point Nepean Road, Portsea. Park entry is free; the optional shuttle is ticketed. Allow two to three hours return including time to stop. No booking is required for walking.

Finish at the Portsea Hotel for lunch and the view earns its place twice in one morning.

**DISCOVERY - Red Hill Truffles, Last Hunts of the Season, Main Ridge**

The truffle hunt season at Red Hill Truffles closes 30 September. That is eleven days away, and the remaining sessions are booking out. If you’ve been meaning to do this since June and haven’t, this is the actual deadline.

The hunts run on the property at Mornington-Flinders Road, Main Ridge, using trained dogs working the rows of inoculated oak and hazelnut trees. The autumn and early-winter hunts get more attention, but the late-season hunts have their own logic: the ground is warming, the dogs work differently in the lighter air, and the guides are in a reflective mood at the end of a long season. You come back with truffles and a working understanding of why they cost what they cost.

This is not a locals-only secret in the strict sense - but most people treat it as a winter thing and miss the final weeks entirely. Book directly at redhilltruffles.com/hunts. Sessions are timed, group sizes are small, and bookings are essential. Red Hill Truffles is on Mornington-Flinders Road, Main Ridge.

Pair it with a stop at a Red Hill cellar door on the way back - the Pinot Noir made within a kilometre of the truffle ground is not a coincidence worth ignoring.

## FAQ

When does the Portsea Hotel open, and do I need to book?

The Portsea Hotel is on Point Nepean Road, Portsea, on the front beach. It opens daily for lunch and dinner. Deck tables fill fast on spring weekends - arrive early or check the website for bookings.

How do I book the Point Nepean Number 2 Rifle Range track?

Entry to Point Nepean National Park is free. No booking is needed for walking. Optional services such as the park shuttle are charged separately. Arrive early on spring weekends to reduce parking pressure.

Is the Red Hill Truffles season really finishing soon?

The truffle hunt season at Red Hill Truffles, Mornington-Flinders Road, Main Ridge, runs until 30 September 2026. Book directly at redhilltruffles.com/hunts - sessions book out, and almost nothing remains at this point in the season.
