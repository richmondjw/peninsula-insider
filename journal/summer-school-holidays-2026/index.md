## The short version

- A dated family guide to the 2026-27 Victorian summer school holidays (19 December 2026 to late January 2027) on the Mornington Peninsula.

- Recommends calm bay beaches (Safety Beach, Mount Martha, Mornington), morning outings before the heat, and a few anchor experiences: Sunny Ridge strawberries, Ashcombe Maze, Point Nepean by shuttle or bike, the Arthurs Seat Eagle.

- Practical advice on traffic, booking, sun and fire danger, and wet-day backups.

Six weeks is a long time on the Peninsula with children, and the best summer holidays here are not the busiest ones. They are the ones with a rhythm: water in the morning, something to do before lunch, a slow afternoon, and an evening on the sand.

This is the plan for the 2026-27 break. Victorian government school Term 4 ends on **Friday 18 December 2026**, and students return on **Thursday 28 January 2027**. The Shire’s peak foreshore camping season runs over almost exactly the same window, from 19 December to 30 January, so expect every bay-side town between Dromana and Sorrento to be at capacity for most of it.

## The beaches that work with kids

Stay on the bay. Port Phillip’s beaches are calm, shallow and warm by January, and they are where Peninsula families actually spend the summer.

- **Safety Beach.** The name is accurate. Long, shallow, gentle water and a foreshore that runs for kilometres. The first choice with toddlers and nervous swimmers.

- **Mount Martha and Mornington.** The bathing-box beaches. Mills Beach in Mornington is good for a paddleboard on a still morning, and the town is right there for ice cream and toilets.

- **Rosebud and Rye foreshores.** Wide, flat and busy, with the camping grounds behind them. Good for a whole day if you arrive early.

- **Portsea front beach.** Clear water and a pier to walk, on the bay side of the very tip.

The ocean back beaches at Gunnamatta, Rye, Sorrento and Portsea are for walking and looking with children, not for swimming, unless the beach is patrolled and you are between the red and yellow flags. Our [beach swimming guide](<https://peninsulainsider.com.au/journal/the-peninsula-beach-swimming-guide/>) has the detail.

## Outings worth the drive

Pick one a day, do it in the morning, and you will have a good holiday.

**Strawberry picking at Sunny Ridge.** The Peninsula’s classic summer outing. Pick-your-own runs through the warm months, roughly November to April; go early before the heat, and check picking conditions on the day.

**Ashcombe Maze.** The hedge maze and gardens in Shoreham are low-tempo in the best way. Children run the maze; adults sit with a tea. A good choice for a cooler or overcast day.

**Point Nepean National Park.** The fort tunnels, the quarantine station and the views across the heads are a genuine adventure for older children. Ride the main path or use the Parks Victoria shuttle, which adds services in peak periods such as Christmas and the summer school holidays. Leave the car at the entrance and treat it as a half day; our [Point Nepean half-day plan](<https://peninsulainsider.com.au/explore/plans/the-point-nepean-half-day/>) sets it out.

**Arthurs Seat.** The gondola over the treetops is a reliable hit with younger children. Our [Arthurs Seat Eagle guide](<https://peninsulainsider.com.au/journal/arthurs-seat-eagle-visitor-guide/>) covers tickets and timing.

**Under the water.** For confident older swimmers, snorkelling trips out of Sorrento run in summer to the seals and sponge gardens near the heads. Boats sell out in January, so book days ahead and check age and swimming requirements with the operator.

## Lunch that keeps everyone happy

Families do best at places with space to move and a menu that works for every age.

- **Red Gum BBQ, Red Hill South.** Big room, generous plates and table service. It opens for lunch Tuesday to Saturday, with dinner Thursday to Saturday; book ahead in the holidays.

- **La Baracca at T’Gallant, Main Ridge.** Wood-fired pizza, shaded outdoor tables and room for children to run. The Peninsula’s most reliable easy group lunch.

- **Main Ridge Dairy.** A working goat dairy with farm cheeses and goat-milk gelato. Children understand the whole operation in about thirty seconds.

- **The Rye Hotel.** A sprawling foreshore pub near the front beach, with a deck and a menu children recognise.

For more, see [casual lunch on the Peninsula](<https://peninsulainsider.com.au/journal/best-casual-lunch-mornington-peninsula/>).

## The timing that saves the holiday

**Beat the morning rush.** Bay car parks fill by mid-morning on warm days. Arrive by nine and the day is easy; arrive at eleven and it is a negotiation.

**Avoid Point Nepean Road at peak times.** The coast road through Rosebud and Rye is slow on any hot day in January. Base yourself near the beach you will use most and walk or cycle to it.

**Use the late afternoon.** Summer sunsets land around 8.30pm, and the bay beaches empty after five while the water is still warm. A dinner picnic on the sand is the cheapest good night on the Peninsula.

**Plan around the heat.** On hot days, do the outdoor outing first, then retreat indoors or into the shade from late morning. Pack hats, water and more sunscreen than seems sensible.

**Check fire danger.** On high fire danger days, skip remote bush walks, check the VicEmergency app and the day’s rating, and choose the beach.

## When it rains

Summer on the Peninsula still produces wet days. The [indoor play guide](<https://peninsulainsider.com.au/journal/indoor-play-mornington-peninsula/>) lists the wet-weather options, and the Mornington Peninsula Regional Gallery and Ashcombe Maze’s café are both good for a grey morning. A long lunch at La Baracca under cover is a fine way to wait out a shower.

## The short version

Bay beaches, early starts, one outing a day and a long evening on the sand. Book the things that sell out, a snorkel boat and a weekend lunch in particular, and leave the rest of the summer open.

## FAQ

When are the 2026-27 summer school holidays in Victoria?

Term 4 ends on Friday 18 December 2026. Term 1 2027 begins on Wednesday 27 January for staff, with government school students starting on Thursday 28 January. Check your own school's dates, as some schools differ.

What is the safest beach for young children on the Mornington Peninsula?

The bay side is the place for young children. Safety Beach is the Peninsula's most reliably calm stretch: shallow, gentle and long. Mount Martha, Mornington and Rosebud foreshores are also bay beaches. Avoid swimming at the ocean back beaches with children unless the beach is patrolled and you are between the red and yellow flags.

Where can you pick strawberries on the Mornington Peninsula?

Sunny Ridge Strawberry Farm in Main Ridge runs pick-your-own through the warmer months, roughly November to April. Pick in the morning before the heat, and check the farm's picking conditions on the day before you drive.

What can kids do on the Mornington Peninsula when it rains in summer?

Our indoor play guide covers the Peninsula's wet-weather options. Ashcombe Maze's café and gardens, the Mornington Peninsula Regional Gallery and a long lunch at a family-friendly winery such as La Baracca at T'Gallant also work in light rain.

### [Safety Beach](<https://peninsulainsider.com.au/explore/safety-beach-foreshore/>)

Safety Beach is the Peninsula's most reliably calm swimming beach - the name is genuinely accurate - and the right answer when you have a young family, a nervous swimmer, or simply want to walk along a long flat shoreline without the theatre of the back beaches. The water is shallow, warm even by April standards, and the bay rarely produces any real chop along this stretch. The foreshore runs for kilometres between Safety Beach and Mount Martha and is good for long early-morning walks or slow afternoon sits. Martha's Table sits a short stroll away for post-swim aperitivo, and the marina at the northern end is a quiet place to watch the boats at dusk. Best in the early morning or the hour before sunset. The middle of the day on a hot summer weekend can get busy, but the stretch is long enough to find your own patch.

### [Sunny Ridge Strawberry Farm](<https://peninsulainsider.com.au/explore/sunny-ridge-strawberry-farm/>)

Sunny Ridge has been running pick-your-own strawberry picking on the Red Hill plateau long enough to be the reference point for this kind of experience on the Peninsula. The season runs from November through April, outside that window there's nothing to pick, so check before you drive. During the season, the format is straightforward: collect a punnet, walk the rows, fill it, weigh it at the counter, and pay by weight. The simplicity is the point. The farm café serves strawberry ice cream and sundaes made from the estate's own fruit. It's not a gourmet café, it's a family destination with a very clear purpose, and it does that purpose well. Go in the morning on a clear day when the fruit is cool and the rows aren't crowded. Later in the afternoon on busy weekends, the most accessible rows can be well-picked-over. Wear clothes you don't mind staining. The juice is persistent, and small children will make the most of it.

### [Ashcombe Maze & Lavender Gardens](<https://peninsulainsider.com.au/explore/ashcombe-maze/>)

Ashcombe Maze holds Australia's oldest symmetrical hedge maze, a circular rose garden, a lavender labyrinth, and a small café that sells lavender everything, ice cream, scones, shortbread, tisane, from a room that smells exactly like the fields outside. The gardens are at their peak in spring when the lavender is up, but the maze works in any season and the café is reason enough in autumn. The place is deliberately low-tempo. You walk the maze, you sit in the lavender, you drink tea, and you leave feeling like you have stumbled into a slightly older, gentler version of the Peninsula. It is a good rainy-afternoon reset and an even better spring morning plan. Pair it with Flinders Sourdough on the way through or Flinders General Store afterwards. Bring the kids. Bring older visitors. Do not try to be cynical about it, the charm wins.

Bay Coast · town

### [Safety Beach](<https://peninsulainsider.com.au/explore/places/safety-beach/>)

Safety Beach is the long shallow crescent between Mount Martha and Dromana - the kind of bay beach where the water is calm enough for very small children and the ridge behind it is steep enough to put you among vineyards in under ten minutes. For most of its history it was a quiet caravan-and-holiday-house stretch; in the last decade it has slowly sharpened, with a marina at the western end, a handful of smarter houses lining the foreshore, and a couple of genuinely good food stops using the view properly. It is a useful place to stay for anyone who wants a base that works equally for beach mornings and hinterland afternoons.

Mornington · town

### [Mornington](<https://peninsulainsider.com.au/explore/places/mornington/>)

Mornington was the gateway town for a long time - the first real stop on the road south, somewhere to buy petrol before pressing on to beaches and vineyards. That undersells it now. A genuinely good main street, a Wednesday artisan market along Main Street worth making time for, a foreshore with bathing boxes and a saltwater pool - Mornington has quietly become a whole-day destination rather than a waypoint. It's also the most practical Peninsula base: north to Mount Martha's beaches, south to Red Hill's vineyards, fifteen minutes from coast in either direction.

Peninsula Tip · town

### [Sorrento](<https://peninsulainsider.com.au/explore/places/sorrento/>)

Sorrento is where the Peninsula narrows to a point and the social temperature rises. This is old-money beach culture done with increasing sophistication - limestone cliffs, a ferry to Queenscliff across the Heads, ocean baths carved from rock at low tide - alongside a main street that has quietly evolved into something genuinely worth driving for. The summer crowd is real and relentless, but the town earns it. Off-season Sorrento is a revelation: the light changes, the restaurants empty out to their best selves, and you can walk the ocean beach from one end to the other without encountering anyone but locals. The back beach at dusk is one of the best things on the Peninsula, full stop. Stay at least two nights.
