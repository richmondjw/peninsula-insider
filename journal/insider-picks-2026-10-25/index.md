# Jetty Road Brewery's seasonal taps, Arthurs Seat in spring bloom, and the National Works on Paper closing run

Jetty Road Brewery's rotating seasonals earn the afternoon, Arthurs Seat State Park is at peak wildflower density, and the National Works on Paper 2026 closes 22 November at Mornington Peninsula Regional Gallery.

**Jetty Road Brewery, Dromana - the rotating taps right now**

The seasonals list at Jetty Road is where the head brewer stops playing it safe. This spring the rotating taps have been turning over faster than winter, with short-run batches replacing each other roughly every three weeks. Worth asking specifically what is on the seasonal handle before ordering a pint of the year-round range - that is where the interesting work is happening. The taproom itself is the most considered space of any bayside brewery on the Peninsula: deliberate fit-out, a generous north-facing deck, and no sense of the usual warehouse compromise. It sits directly alongside the Dromana Drive-In, which gives the whole precinct an unhurried, slightly unexpected character that suits a long Sunday afternoon. Go mid-afternoon on a weekday if you want the deck without a wait.

**Practical note:** 57 Collins Road, Dromana. Open Wednesday to Sunday; check jettyroad.com.au for current hours. Walk-ins fine, but the deck fills on warm spring days.

Pair with a stop at Small Stone Pantry in Dromana for something to eat on the way through.

**Arthurs Seat State Park - wildflower density peaks this fortnight**

The heath scrub on the southern and eastern slopes of Arthurs Seat is at its best between mid-October and early November, and this week falls squarely in that window. Spreading guinea flower, pink heath, and common correa are all open simultaneously right now - that overlap closes by mid-November as the heath dries out. The Seawinds Gardens loop starting from the carpark off Arthurs Seat Road takes roughly ninety minutes at a relaxed pace and passes the best concentrations of flowering heath on the upper slope. Morning is better than afternoon: the flowers track the light, and the views back across Port Phillip are clearer before the coastal haze builds. Bring shoes you don’t mind getting damp; the lower section of the track stays wet longer than you’d expect this early in the season.

**Practical note:** Enter via Arthurs Seat Road, Arthurs Seat. Free access. Allow 1.5–2 hours for the Seawinds loop. No booking required.

Pair with lunch at the Dromana Hotel, fifteen minutes down the hill.

**National Works on Paper 2026, Mornington Peninsula Regional Gallery - closes 22 November**

NWOP is the longest-running acquisitive survey of works on paper in Australia, and the 2026 edition at Mornington Peninsula Regional Gallery has been up since early September. It closes 22 November, which means this is the final four weeks. The show brings together around eighty selected works across drawing, printmaking, watercolour, and mixed media from artists across the country - the acquisitive structure means the gallery actually buys from the selection, which keeps the standard honest. It is not a touring blockbuster; it is a working survey, and it reads that way. The gallery space at Civic Reserve handles it well, with enough room to spend time with individual works rather than shuffle past them. If you are already heading to the Mornington Farmers’ Market or the Main Street, the gallery is a short walk from both.

**Practical note:** Mornington Peninsula Regional Gallery, Civic Reserve, Dunns Road, Mornington. Open Tuesday to Sunday 11am–4pm. Free entry. Closes 22 November 2026.

Pair with coffee at Store Ten, a few minutes’ walk from the gallery.

When does the National Works on Paper 2026 close at Mornington Peninsula Regional Gallery?
