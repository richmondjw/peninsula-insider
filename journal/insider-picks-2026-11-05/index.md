# Green Olive in the grove, the coastal heath in full flower, and the Works on Paper closing window

Green Olive at Red Hill's wood-fired lamb under the olive grove, the Boneo heathland orchids at peak density, and the National Works on Paper show at MPRG closing 22 November.

Insider Picks  Spring

Mornington Peninsula Regional Gallery, Mornington Peninsula   Photo · Robert Blackburn, courtesy of Visit Victoria

**Green Olive at Red Hill** earns the spring lunch argument clearly: the olive grove is in full leaf, the kitchen gardens are supplying the kitchen right now, and the wood oven has been running since the estate planted its first trees.

Order the wood-fired lamb. It arrives with a credible claim on those words - the oils used in the kitchen come from the grove a few metres from your table, not from a wholesale supplier. The Mediterranean smallholding setting means the menu actually follows the season rather than performing it. A spring Sunday here is as close as the Peninsula gets to eating in the place that grew the food. The estate olive oils are available to buy on the way out, and the current harvest-pressed bottle is worth taking home.

Book ahead - Sunday lunches fill. Green Olive at Red Hill, 1180 Mornington–Flinders Road, Red Hill. Allow two hours minimum.

Pair this with a walk through the Red Hill Road orchards before lunch while the apple blossom is still out.

The heathland between the **Cape Schanck** car park and the lighthouse boardwalk junction is as good as it gets right now. Spider orchids, sun orchids, and coastal tea-tree are flowering simultaneously along the low scrub trail, and the November window for the ground orchids is tight - another warm week and the peak passes.

The specific stretch to walk is from the main car park south toward the boardwalk junction, keeping to the left-hand trail where the heath is lower and more open. That is where the orchid density concentrates. The cliff views are incidental. The real reason to go this weekend is ground level, not horizon level. Take your time on the first 300 metres of trail rather than pushing straight to the lighthouse.

No booking required. Cape Schanck Road, Fingal. Allow 45–60 minutes for the heath walk alone. Dogs on lead permitted on sections of the trail - check Parks Victoria for the current access rules.

Pair with breakfast at Flinders before you drive down, to arrive by 9am before the wind picks up.

**National Works on Paper 2026** at Mornington Peninsula Regional Gallery closes 22 November - seventeen days from today. This is the annual survey show that matters most to anyone who follows drawing and printmaking in Australia, and the MPRG has hosted it long enough that the hanging is confident rather than cautious.

The show runs across the full main gallery space. What makes it worth the drive specifically this week: the final-fortnight crowd is lighter than opening month, the work has settled on the walls, and you can move through it without being pushed. It is a national acquisitive prize show, so the range is deliberately wide - expect works you would not find at a commercial gallery and prices set during the prize process rather than after. The gallery does not rush you out.

Mornington Peninsula Regional Gallery, Civic Reserve, Dunns Road, Mornington. Open daily 11am–4pm. Free entry. No booking required.

Pair with a walk along the Mornington foreshore after, while the late-afternoon light is still long.

## FAQ

When is Green Olive at Red Hill open for lunch?

Green Olive at Red Hill operates Thursday to Sunday for lunch. Book ahead via their website. The venue is at 1180 Mornington–Flinders Road, Red Hill.

When does the National Works on Paper exhibition close at Mornington Peninsula Regional Gallery?

The National Works on Paper 2026 show closes Sunday 22 November. The gallery is at Civic Reserve, Dunns Road, Mornington, open daily 11am–4pm.

Where is the best spot to see coastal orchids and heathland wildflowers near Cape Schanck this spring?

The heathland trail between the Cape Schanck car park and the lighthouse boardwalk junction is the most reliable corridor for spider orchids and tea-tree in flower. Allow 45–60 minutes. The car park is off Cape Schanck Road, Fingal.
