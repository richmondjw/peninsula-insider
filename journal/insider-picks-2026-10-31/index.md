**Moke Dining, Flinders - the set menu that justifies the drive**

Michael Cole placed fourteenth in the world at Bocuse d’Or. His room on Cook Street holds twenty people. The menu changes weekly and runs to five courses. There is no à la carte, no flexibility required from you - just show up having booked, and eat what the kitchen has decided. Right now in late spring, that means local seafood, young brassicas, and stone fruit beginning to edge into dessert. The format rewards a table that is willing to hand over the decisions. Saturday lunch is the move: book it, then walk the cliff path from Flinders to West Head beforehand. You will have earned the meal by the time you sit down. Bookings are essential and the room fills well in advance for spring Saturdays - check the Moke website for availability before you plan the day around it. Cook Street, Flinders.

Pair this with a walk along the Flinders cliff path to West Head before your reservation.

**Balnarring horse trails - spring green at its best**

The horse trail network around Balnarring and Merricks reaches its most open condition in late October. Grass is long, wattlebirds are loud, and the paddock tracks that connect Merricks Road to the Balnarring back roads are uncrowded before summer beach traffic shifts attention to the coast. This is not a marked trail system - it is a network of public paths and back roads locals have used for decades on foot and on horseback. The junction of Merricks Road and Tucks Road gives you access north through open farmland with clear views to the ranges. Go in the morning before the nor’westerly picks up. Wear something you do not mind getting damp. Allow ninety minutes for the full Merricks Road loop and back through the reserve. No booking, no fee, no crowds on a Saturday morning at 8am. Balnarring.

Pair this with breakfast at the Emu Plains Market in Balnarring directly after.

**Emu Plains Market, Balnarring - the locals’ Saturday circuit**

Most Peninsula markets push a tourist demographic by mid-morning. Emu Plains Reserve in Balnarring does not. The stalls lean toward local producers - small-batch preserves, cut flowers grown on-property, ceramic seconds from potters who live within twenty kilometres. The market runs Saturdays from 9am to 2pm, and the sweet spot is 9.30am before the serious preserves and bread sell out. The reserve itself is worth the walk: open grass, old-growth trees, and a creek line that is running well after spring rain. There is no single standout vendor because the lineup rotates, so the discipline is to arrive early, walk the full circuit before you buy anything, and make decisions on the second pass. That is what the regulars do. Running through to 19 December 2026. Emu Plains Reserve, Balnarring. No booking required.

Pair this with the Balnarring horse trail loop directly before - both sit within ten minutes of each other.
