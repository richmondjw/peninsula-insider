# Insider Picks - Sunday 22 November 2026

**EAT - The Red Hill Baker, Balnarring**

The wood-fired oven at The Red Hill Baker has been running since the early hours. By the time you arrive, the sourdough loaves are out and the morning pastries are already moving fast.

This is a Balnarring institution in the most literal sense: the village’s rhythm on a Sunday morning organises itself around this bakery. The sourdough has a proper crust, the kind that needs both hands to break. The pies and pastries are made in-house, and the selection narrows quickly after nine. Spring is a good time to arrive with a plan - grab a loaf, something for the road, and head out from there. There is no indoor theatre here; it is a working bakery that gets on with it.

Go early. The bread and pastry selection is fullest before 9:30 am. Balnarring village, no booking required.

Pair a loaf with a stop at The Heritage Balnarring for a late morning drink on the veranda before the Sunday crowd fills it.

**EXPERIENCE - Bushrangers Bay Trail, Cape Schanck**

The Bushrangers Bay trail is at its most rewarding right now. The clifftop grasses are green from the September rains and the coastal scrub is flowering.

The trail drops from the Cape Schanck Lighthouse car park down to Bushrangers Bay, a beach that is closed in on three sides by basalt columns and almost impossible to reach any other way. In summer, the descent gets crowded and the return in heat is unpleasant. In spring, the air is still mild, the light lasts long into the evening, and the cliff face above the beach holds a consistent offshore breeze. The boardwalk sections are steep and exposed - wear shoes with grip. The return climb takes around 40 minutes and there is no shade on the upper section, so bring water regardless of the forecast.

Allow 90 minutes return from the car park. No booking. Park at Cape Schanck Lighthouse, Boneo Road.

The lighthouse precinct is worth ten minutes of your time before the descent - the views north to the bay are clearest on a clear spring morning.

**DISCOVERY - National Works on Paper 2026, Mornington Peninsula Regional Gallery (closes today)**

The National Works on Paper exhibition closes at 4:00 pm today, Sunday 22 November. If you have been meaning to get there, this morning is the last chance.

NWOP is a serious national survey of works made on paper - drawing, printmaking, watercolour, mixed media - that the Mornington Peninsula Regional Gallery has hosted for years. The 2026 edition opened in September and has drawn entries from across Australia. The works range from large-format ink drawings to intimate etchings, and the hang is dense in the best way: enough to slow you down, not so much that you lose focus. It is a free show in a good gallery that consistently punches above its regional size. The works are for sale through the gallery, so if something catches you, check the desk before you leave.

Open until 4:00 pm today only. Mornington Peninsula Regional Gallery, Dunns Road, Mornington. Free entry.

Commonfolk Coffee on Mornington’s main strip is a ten-minute walk and worth the detour for a post-gallery coffee.

## FAQ

What time does The Red Hill Baker open in Balnarring?

The Red Hill Baker is in Balnarring village. Go early - the wood-fired sourdough and morning pastries sell out before midmorning most weekends. Check their current hours at redhillbaker.com.au.

When does the National Works on Paper exhibition close at Mornington Peninsula Regional Gallery?

The National Works on Paper 2026 exhibition closes today, Sunday 22 November 2026, at 4:00 pm. The gallery is on Dunns Road, Mornington. Free entry. No booking required.

How do I get to the Bushrangers Bay trail at Cape Schanck?

Park at the Cape Schanck Lighthouse car park, Boneo Road, Cape Schanck. The Bushrangers Bay trail descends to the beach and back - allow around 90 minutes return. No booking needed. Wear shoes with grip; the boardwalk sections are steep.
