# Mornington Peninsula 3-Day Itinerary - The Best Weekend Plan

Three days is the sweet spot. Enough time to eat properly, drink without rushing, walk a stretch of coast, and actually relax - which is the whole point.

## The short version

This itinerary assumes you're driving from Melbourne (about 75 minutes to [Mornington](<https://peninsulainsider.com.au/explore/places/mornington/>), 90 to [Red Hill](<https://peninsulainsider.com.au/explore/places/red-hill/>)) and staying somewhere central on the ridge between Red Hill and [Main Ridge](<https://peninsulainsider.com.au/explore/places/main-ridge/>). If you only have two days, cut Day 3's morning and leave after lunch on Day 2.

## Day 1: Arrive, orient, eat well

Leave Melbourne after the morning traffic clears - 10am is fine. Drive straight to [Red Hill](<https://peninsulainsider.com.au/explore/places/red-hill/>) and stop at a currently operating village cafe for a pie and coffee. The Red Hill Baker no longer operates in Red Hill; its sole current shop is in Balnarring.

Check into your accommodation, then head to [Montalto](<https://peninsulainsider.com.au/wine/montalto/>) for a late lunch at the restaurant or a more casual plate at the piazza café. The grounds are worth a walk - the sculpture trail takes 30 minutes and earns you that second glass of pinot.

For dinner, book [Laura](<https://peninsulainsider.com.au/eat/laura-pt-leo/>) if you want the peninsula's best fine dining - it's a set menu, and worth it once. If that's not your speed, [Rare Hare](<https://peninsulainsider.com.au/eat/rare-hare/>) on the Willow Creek property does excellent wood-fired cooking in a room that feels like a wealthy friend's barn. Book either at least two weeks ahead for weekends.

## Day 2: Wine, food, and the ridge

This is the day people come here for. Hit cellar doors from 11am. Do three, maximum four - more than that and you stop tasting anything.

A good sequence: start at [Ten Minutes by Tractor](<https://peninsulainsider.com.au/wine/ten-minutes-by-tractor/>) for serious, elegant pinot noir and chardonnay. The wines are benchmark-level and the staff actually know what they're talking about. Next, drive five minutes to [Main Ridge Estate](<https://peninsulainsider.com.au/wine/main-ridge-estate/>) - the oldest vineyard on the peninsula and still one of the best. Small production, no pretension.

Lunch at Pt Leo Estate. Sit outside if the weather cooperates. The sculpture park here is the best on the peninsula - give it an hour after eating.

Afternoon: one more cellar door. [Stonier](<https://peninsulainsider.com.au/wine/stonier-wines/>) in [Merricks](<https://peninsulainsider.com.au/explore/places/merricks/>) does excellent chardonnay, or [Crittenden Estate](<https://peninsulainsider.com.au/wine/crittenden-estate/>) in [Dromana](<https://peninsulainsider.com.au/explore/places/dromana/>) if you want something more experimental.

Dinner: keep it simple. [Portsea Hotel](<https://peninsulainsider.com.au/eat/portsea-hotel/>) does good pub food with a view, or drive into [Mornington](<https://peninsulainsider.com.au/explore/places/mornington/>) for something with more ambition. You've had a big day of tasting - don't fight it with another degustation.

## Day 3: Coast, beach, then leave

Get up early. Drive to [Portsea](<https://peninsulainsider.com.au/explore/places/portsea/>) and walk the track from London Bridge to the ocean back beach. It takes about 40 minutes each way, the clifftop views are the best on the peninsula, and at 8am you'll have it almost to yourself.

Breakfast at Hotel Continental [Sorrento](<https://peninsulainsider.com.au/explore/places/sorrento/>) - sit on the terrace overlooking the bay. Then walk down to Sorrento main street.

Before you leave, stop at [Red Hill Cheese](<https://peninsulainsider.com.au/eat/red-hill-cheese/>). Buy cheese, olive oil, maybe some charcuterie. This is your take-home haul and it's better than anything you'll find at the airport.

Leave by 2pm to miss the worst of the return traffic. Sunday afternoons are brutal - if you're leaving Sunday, go by 1pm or wait until after 5pm.

## Worth knowing before you plan

The hedge maze at Arthur's Seat suits families better than adult-only weekends. The Eagle skylift is best treated as a views-first stop rather than the centrepiece of the day. And don't try to squeeze in hot springs on this itinerary unless you cut a cellar door from Day 2. Hot springs deserve their own half-day.

## Planning notes

**Book ahead:** Laura needs 2-4 weeks. Rare Hare and Montalto need 1-2 weeks for weekends. Cellar doors generally don't need bookings except Ten Minutes by Tractor (book online).

**Where to stay:** Anywhere between Red Hill and Main Ridge puts you central. Avoid staying in Dromana or Mornington if you want to feel like you've left Melbourne.

**Budget:** Accommodation is the largest cost for a comfortable three days; meals and tasting fees together usually come to less than the room. Set the accommodation tier first and the rest of the budget follows.

**Best time:** March through May is ideal. Summer weekends are overcrowded. Winter is underrated if you don't need beach weather.
