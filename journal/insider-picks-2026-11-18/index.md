## EAT - The Heritage Balnarring, Balnarring

The beer garden at The Heritage Balnarring earns its reputation in warmer months, when the 1930s pub shakes off its winter-fire identity and spreads out properly into the garden and deck.

Spring is the right season to test it. The kitchen runs pub classics alongside a changing specials board - the kind of operation that knows its crowd and doesn’t overcomplicate things. On a warm November afternoon the deck fills with people who have come from the Emu Plains Market down the road, or from a wander through the Balnarring Village Precinct. The beer garden has actual room to move, something that matters once the season turns and everyone wants to be outside at once. The specials board shifts with what’s available locally, so it’s worth asking what’s on before you order.

Allow two hours minimum. The Heritage Balnarring is on Balnarring Road, Balnarring. Walk-ins are welcome but the deck books out on Saturday afternoons - call ahead.

Pair with a stop at the Emu Plains Market on Saturdays at Emu Plains Reserve before lunch.

## EXPERIENCE - Peninsula VineHop Festival, Mount Martha

Six venues, one Saturday, and a route that covers the stretch between Mount Martha and the broader Mornington Peninsula wine corridor - the VineHop Festival on Saturday 21 November is the most logistically efficient way to cover this much ground in a day.

The hub sits at The Briars in Mount Martha, with the festival running 11am to 7pm. The format is a progressive tasting route: you move between linked cellar doors and venues at your own pace rather than standing in one field all afternoon. Spring is exactly when these grounds are at their best - new-growth vines, longer light, and producers pouring from the current vintage. The route rewards those who plan transport in advance; this is not a drive-yourself day.

Tickets via Humanitix at events.humanitix.com/peninsula-vinehop-festival-2026/tickets. Bookings essential. Allow a full day and arrange a designated driver or book accommodation nearby.

Pair with an overnight stay in Mount Martha or Mornington to avoid the drive back.

## DISCOVERY - National Works on Paper 2026, Mornington Peninsula Regional Gallery

The National Works on Paper closes this Sunday 22 November at 4pm at MPRG on Dunns Road, Mornington - after that it’s gone for another year.

This is the longest-running acquisitive works-on-paper prize in Australia, and the Mornington Peninsula Regional Gallery has hosted it since the program’s early years. The 2026 edition opened in September; by the final weekend the shortlisted and acquired works are all hung and the full selection is visible. Works on paper rewards close looking in a way that large-scale painting doesn’t always demand - you end up closer to the work, to the mark-making, than you expect. The gallery is free to enter and the exhibition reads well in a single visit of around 45 to 60 minutes. If you have been meaning to get there, Sunday is the last opportunity.

MPRG is at Civic Reserve, Dunns Road, Mornington. Open 11am–4pm. Free entry. No booking required.

Pair with lunch in Mornington township afterwards - the gallery is a short walk from the main strip.
