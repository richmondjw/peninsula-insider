# Two Bays on tap, waratahs on the Bushrangers Bay track, and the VineHop Festival this Saturday

## Drink

**Two Bays Brewing Co, Dromana - the tasting paddle right now**

Two Bays opened in Dromana as Australia’s first dedicated gluten-free brewery, and that credential still leads most introductions. Set the qualifier aside. The pale ale and session IPA are worth ordering on their own merits, and the unfussy taproom on Old Mornington Road is a better spring afternoon than most of the bayside pub circuit.

The spring food menu runs pizzas, burgers, and charcuterie boards - solid enough to justify staying through a second round. The tasting paddle is the right starting point: work through the current lineup, identify which beer you’d drink a full pour of, then order it. The IPA handles the brief well on a warm afternoon.

Allow 90 minutes. No bookings required for walk-ins, though groups should check ahead. Find them at 2/36 Old Mornington Road, Dromana; twobrewing.com.au.

Pair the afternoon with a walk along the Dromana foreshore before or after.

## Walk

**Bushrangers Bay Walk, Cape Schanck - waratah season**

The Bushrangers Bay Walk from Cape Schanck is good year-round, but the stretch of heathland between the car park and the bay cliffs earns a specific visit in November. Common waratahs are peaking now - large crimson flower heads against grey-green scrub, the kind of colour that stops people mid-stride. This is not a spectacle you can replicate in December.

The return loop is around 5 km and takes 1.5 to 2 hours at a comfortable pace. The final descent to Bushrangers Bay involves a steep section and some exposed rock platform; wear closed shoes. The ocean views from the cliff edge above the bay are the payoff, and in spring swell the wave energy on the basalt is worth timing your arrival around.

Start from the Cape Schanck car park on Cape Schanck Road. Parks Victoria entry fees apply; check parks.vic.gov.au for track conditions before you go.

Pair the walk with lunch at a Flinders café on the way back north.

## Discovery

**Peninsula VineHop Festival, Saturday 21 November - what to know before you go**

The Peninsula VineHop Festival is not a secret, but how it actually works is something most first-timers figure out too late. It runs this Saturday, 21 November, 11am to 7pm, with The Briars in Mount Martha as the festival hub and six linked venues spread across the Peninsula. You move between them. That is the structure - it is a progressive festival, not a single-site event.

The practical gap: the linked venues are geographically spread, so going in without a loose route wastes time. Check the venue list on the website before Saturday, pick two or three you actually want to reach, and work backwards from the 7pm close. The mid-afternoon window, roughly 2–4pm, is the least congested at most stops.

Tickets via Humanitix; book ahead, it has sold out in previous years. The Briars is at 450 Nepean Highway, Mount Martha. Full venue map at vinehopfestival.com.au.

Pair it with an overnight stay in Mount Martha rather than driving back after 7pm.
