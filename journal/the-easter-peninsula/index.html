The Easter Peninsula: How to Do the Long Weekend Without Losing It to the Crowds

Easter is the Peninsula's first big four-day stress test of the year - the cars from Toorak come down on Thursday afternoon, the cellar doors are full by Friday morning, and the bakeries run out of hot cross buns by Saturday lunch. Here is how to plan a long weekend that uses the holiday properly instead of fighting it.

By The Peninsula Insider Facts verified 22 April 2026

An Easter Sunday brunch table on a Peninsula garden terrace with hot cross buns and chocolate eggs

The Mornington Peninsula at Easter is one of the most predictable failure modes in Australian short-break travel.

The cars start arriving on Thursday afternoon. By Friday morning the cellar doors are running on the same staffing they use for a normal Saturday and have triple the bookings. By Saturday lunch the bakeries have run out of hot cross buns and the queue at Commonfolk reaches the kerb. By Sunday morning the back-beach car parks are full by 8.30am, and by Monday afternoon the freeway home is a parking lot. People arrive expecting the version of the weekend they had in October - quiet, generous, easy - and they find themselves shuffling through a sequence of busy rooms wondering why.

There is a better version, and it is not particularly complicated. You just have to accept that Easter is a different weekend than every other weekend on the Peninsula calendar, and plan accordingly.

Here is how.

The principle: invert everything

The single rule for an Easter weekend that works on the Peninsula is to invert the rhythm of the standard weekend. The normal Peninsula weekend goes: arrive Friday, big lunch Saturday, walk and producer trail Sunday, drive home Monday. At Easter, the lunch crowd at the cellar doors is enormous on Saturday and slightly less enormous on Sunday - so the right move is to do the booked, busy parts of the weekend on Sunday and Monday, and the quiet parts on Friday and Saturday.

Specifically:

  • Friday is the village morning. Bakeries, foreshore walks, slow coffee. Not booked anywhere.
  • Saturday is the landscape day. Walks, beaches, the parts of the Peninsula that the Toorak crowd does not bother with.
  • Sunday is the day for the booked lunch. By Sunday lunch, the ridge has thinned a bit. The bookings are still tight but the cellar doors are functional.
  • Monday is the leave-early morning. Coffee, one last small move, and on the road by 11am to beat the freeway.

Done this way, the long weekend has the same number of moves as the standard weekend but uses the calendar geometry to its advantage. You do the quiet moves when everyone else is booked, and the booked moves when everyone else is on the beach.

Where to stay at Easter

Easter accommodation on the Peninsula is the second most stressful booking exercise of the year after Christmas, and the rule is simple: book in February, not in March. By the middle of March, the good rooms are gone and the leftover stock is either overpriced or in the wrong town for what you want.

The stays that handle Easter best are the self-contained ones. Polperro Villas and Crittenden Villas are at the top of the list - both let you cook the Easter Sunday brunch in the room rather than fighting for a cafe table. Lindenderry is the formal alternative if you want a hotel with a dining room and the gardens come into their own at the Easter weekend. Hillview Cottages in Red Hill are the practical option for families. Peninsula Hot Springs Glamping is the most underrated Easter stay - the tented suites with private hot tubs and fire pits are ideally suited to a long-weekend reset.

Avoid: any hotel within walking distance of a cellar door. The car park noise from Friday afternoon onwards will undo whatever calm you came for.

The four-day plan that works

Good Friday - the village morning

Friday is the day everyone else uses to drive down. You should be either down already or arriving as early as possible. The freeway is busy from 10am Thursday and again from about 11am Friday - aim for an early Friday drive (out of Melbourne by 7.30am) or, better, arrive Thursday evening and use Friday morning as the actual start.

Most cellar doors are closed on Good Friday. Most bakeries and cafes are open with reduced hours. This is the day that punishes a packed schedule and rewards a slow one. Here is the shape:

8.30am - Bakery breakfast. Red Hill Bakery if you are based on the ridge, Sorrento Bakery if you are at the tip, Flinders Sourdough if you are south. Hot cross buns are the move - they are the day’s organising food and most of the bakeries make them properly. Buy more than you think you need; you will eat them across all four days.

10am - A village walk. Friday morning on the Peninsula is the right time to walk a village without anyone else there. The Mornington foreshore from the pier to Mills Beach. The Sorrento foreshore from the village to the front beach. The Flinders pier and back along the village street. None of these need a booking, all of them are pleasant on a Good Friday autumn morning.

12pm - Lunch at a pub. The pubs are open and they are the right answer on Good Friday. Balnarring Pub, Flinders Hotel, or Dromana Hotel. Casual, no-pressure, plenty of room. Avoid the cellar doors that do open - they are over-booked and under-staffed and you will get neither the room nor the kitchen at their best.

2.30pm - A garden, a maze, or a chocolate shop. Ashcombe Maze, Mornington Peninsula Chocolates, or Sunny Ridge Strawberry Farm for picking. All three are good on Good Friday because they are open, family-friendly, and not the kind of experience that benefits from a quiet weekend.

6pm - Dinner at the stay. The night you cook in. Bring something from the producer day or from the bakery. Open a bottle from the cellar door you bought from before driving down. This is the night the long weekend slows down.

Easter Saturday - the landscape day

Saturday is the day the cellar doors are busiest, and it is the right day to be nowhere near them. Use the day for the parts of the Peninsula that thin out under crowd pressure - the back-beach side, the National Park, the longer walks, the ocean coast.

8am - A walk you would not normally have time for. The Two Bays Walking Track or a long section of the Bushrangers Bay walk. The Cape Schanck Boardwalk if you want shorter and more dramatic. The Coastal Walk at Cape Schanck if you want the full coastal experience. Whatever you pick, go early - the back-beach car parks fill by 9.30am at Easter.

10.30am - Coffee somewhere quiet. Avoid Mornington and Sorrento village centres on Easter Saturday - they will be packed. Drive to one of the smaller villages - Somers, Balnarring, Main Ridge - for a coffee at a quiet cafe.

12pm - A beach picnic. This is the day the producer trail pays off. Cheese, bread, fruit, hot cross buns. Either at Mount Martha Beach (north end, away from the bathing-box crowd) or Balnarring Beach. The beaches at Easter are quieter than the cafes, which is the inverse of what most visitors expect.

2.30pm - A second short walk. Or a swim if the water is up to it (in late autumn most years it is at the very edge of swimmable). Or a short hinterland drive - Main Ridge to Merricks via the back roads - just to see the vines turning colour for the season.

6pm - A booked dinner. This is the night you book the dining room - but pick a smaller, quieter one rather than a destination room. Bistro Elba, Allis, or Pier Street Flinders are all the kind of room that handles a busy night without losing its temper. Save the destination dinner for Sunday.

Easter Sunday - the booked lunch and the egg hunt

Sunday is the day the rest of the weekend has been preparing for. The destination cellar-door lunch is on Sunday, the egg hunt is on Sunday morning, and the long-weekend rhythm hits its stride.

8am - Egg hunt at the stay. If kids are part of the weekend, build the morning around them. Easter Sunday brunch should be the centre of the day for families - eggs in the garden, hot cross buns toasted on the kitchen bench, scrambled eggs from a producer dozen.

10.30am - A short walk before lunch. Twenty minutes around the garden of the stay, or a foreshore stroll, or a short market run. Nothing too ambitious - the lunch is the event.

12.30pm - The booked lunch. Sunday lunch at one of the destination cellar doors is the moment Easter is for. Merricks General Wine Store, Polperro, Ten Minutes by Tractor, or Pt Leo Estate. Book in February. Budget two and a half hours. Order the local lamb if it is on the menu.

3.30pm - A walk after lunch. The post-lunch walk is the move. Cape Schanck Boardwalk, the sculpture grounds at Pt Leo, or a vineyard loop near the cellar door. Twenty minutes is enough.

6pm - An easy dinner. Cheese plate at the stay. Leftovers from the producer trail. Possibly nothing at all - Easter Sunday lunch is enough food for the day.

Easter Monday - the leave-early morning

Monday is the day everyone tries to drive home at 4pm and the freeway turns into a car park. Do not be that person.

8.30am - Coffee somewhere walkable. Whatever village your stay is in.

10am - One last small move. A bakery stop for the road. A producer farm shop pick-up. A short walk. Nothing booked.

11am - On the road. You are home by 12.30pm and the freeway is moving. The afternoon is yours.

How to make Easter weekend count

  • Driving down on Thursday afternoon between 4pm and 7pm. Two-and-a-half-hour traffic. Leave Friday morning early or Wednesday evening late.
  • A Saturday cellar-door lunch. Wait for Sunday. Saturday is for landscape.
  • Sorrento village from 10am to 2pm. The main street is unwalkable.
  • A back-beach swim on Easter Saturday afternoon. The car parks are full and the water is at the lower edge of comfortable in April. Walk it instead.
  • Trying to leave on Monday afternoon. The freeway crawls from about 1pm. Be on it by 11.30am or wait until 7pm.

What Easter is actually for

The reason Easter is worth coming for, despite all of the above, is that the Peninsula in late April is genuinely beautiful and the long weekend gives you four days instead of two. The vintage is finishing on the ridge. The light is at its best. The autumn vegetable harvest is filling the markets. And the long weekend lets the trip move at a pace that a normal two-night weekend never quite hits.

The trick is treating Easter as a different weekend than the standard one. Different rhythm, different bookings, different moves. Done that way, the four days feel like four days. Done the standard way, they feel like the same two days you would have had any other weekend, with twice the traffic and half the room at the table.

Which is, in this publication’s general philosophy, the same advice for almost every Peninsula visit: choose the version that fits the weekend you are actually in, not the one you wish it was.

Walk
Cape Schanck 90 min · Moderate

Bushrangers Bay Walk

Bushrangers Bay is the walk we send people to when they say they want to see the wilder side of the Peninsula without committing to a full-day hike. The track drops from the Cape Schanck lighthouse precinct through coastal scrub and opens onto a broad crescent of basalt and sand that feels much further from Melbourne than it is. The return climb is enough to justify lunch afterwards, but not so punishing that it tips into chore. Do it in the late afternoon when the light starts to flatten across the water and the whole coastline turns silver.

Lookout
Cape Schanck 35 min · Easy

Cape Schanck Boardwalk

The Cape Schanck boardwalk is short, obvious, and absolutely worth doing. A lot of Peninsula viewpoints require an act of faith before they pay off; this one gives you the basalt formations, the pounding water, and the lighthouse almost immediately. Think of it as the Southern Ocean aperitif before a longer walk or a late lunch inland. It is particularly good for visitors who want scale and coastline without needing to dedicate half the day to it.

Walk
Mornington 120 min · Easy

Mornington Peninsula Foreshore Walk

The foreshore walk between Mornington and Mount Martha is the Peninsula's most dog-friendly significant walk and one of its most reliably beautiful - seven kilometres one way along the coast, with sweeping Port Phillip Bay views, heritage bathing boxes, clifftop lookouts, and several quiet little beaches you can detour into along the way. Start at Mornington Pier and walk south toward Mount Martha, or reverse the direction for a later-afternoon light run. The track is well-formed throughout, accessible to most fitness levels, and supports everything from a fast morning run to a leisurely family stroll. It is the best long morning walk on the bayside, followed by coffee at either end. For the full seven kilometres, allow 90 minutes walking at a brisk pace or two hours with stops. For a shorter segment, the stretch between Mills Beach and Fossil Beach is particularly good.
