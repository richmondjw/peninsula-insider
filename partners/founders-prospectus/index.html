Peninsula Insider II.The publication
II

The publication

A modern newsroom for the Peninsula.

Local first. Editor-led. Published weekly. Every claim verified. Every commercial relationship disclosed. Built for digital, not converted to it.

The publication will cover the Peninsula across five pillars: Eat & Drink, Stay, Do, Plan, and the Insider's Journal. The Journal carries a weekly cover story, longform features, interviews with the chefs and winemakers shaping the region, and seasonal guides that go deeper than the destination-marketing copy currently on the internet for this place.

Two products carry the brand. The publication itself, at peninsulainsider.com.au. And The Insider's Letter, a weekly five-minute read written and signed by the editor.

The bet is simple. There is no category-leading editorial brand for the Mornington Peninsula. Visit Mornington Peninsula is a legacy directory. Broadsheet treats the region as a side note to Melbourne. Easy Weddings and the listicle players have no editorial voice. That position, the local editorial authority, is unowned. We intend to take it.

Peninsula Insider III.The firewall
III

The firewall

Three rules hold the line.

Peninsula Insider takes commercial partnerships. We are open about that. The publication is funded in part by the businesses who appear on it. What we do not do is let those businesses influence the editorial.

i.

Curated commercial

Only Peninsula businesses appear on PI. No programmatic, no off-region brands, no Booking.com retargeting filling our sidebar. If a reader sees a partner story or a sponsor placement here, the venue is one we know.

ii.

Editor-led

James and Emma sign the editorial. They pick the cover. They never appear at advertiser-only events. The masthead is theirs. The rate card is the publisher's. The two do not meet.

iii.

Disclosed by default

Every paid item carries a Partner Content eyebrow. Every founding partner is publicly listed on our about page. Every conflict is declared in the article that touches it. Once a year we publish a one-page firewall audit, naming what we ran, what we declined, and where the line strained.

The differentiator is not "we don't sell." Visit Mornington Peninsula sells. Broadsheet sells. The differentiator is we tell you who is paying, and the editorial does not bend.

Peninsula Insider IV.The newsroom & the cohort
IV

The newsroom & the cohort

Built as a publication, not a blog.

Editorial commissioning runs through a desk model: research, drafting, fact-checking, copy, scheduling, editorial sign-off. Each factual claim is logged with a primary source before draft. Every cover passes editor sign-off before publish. Every Founders' Circle piece is read in full by James or Emma before it goes out.

The result is a five-to-seven-day production cycle on a feature that would take most regional publishers five weeks, at the editorial standard of a national title.

Peninsula Insider V.What a founding partner gets
V

What you get, across 24 months

The work, made real.

Every deliverable below is real work, produced by our desk, signed off by the editor, photographed by our contracted photographer, hosted permanently on the publication.

Editorial

  • One hero feature. Two thousand to twenty-five hundred words, researched by our desk, drafted to PI standard, signed off by the editor. The most ambitious piece of writing your venue will appear in this year, anywhere.
  • One photo essay. Twelve to fifteen original images. Two days of shooting across two seasons. Partner receives a non-exclusive licence to reuse the imagery in their own marketing.
  • One short film. Sixty to ninety seconds, scripted and shot to the publication's house treatment. Lives on your venue page and rotates on the homepage cover for one season.

Placement

  • Year-round priority placement on the section hub most relevant to you. Capped at one founding partner per slot.
  • One quarter as the homepage cover story across the two-year window. Scheduled in agreement with you for the season that suits your business.
  • Two newsletter features, written by the desk, run in The Insider's Letter twice across the year. Editorial features, not banner ads.
  • Embedding in at least two pre-built itineraries and the Build-Your-Own planner, where your venue belongs in the recommendation.
