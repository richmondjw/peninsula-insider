# Product Marketing Context — Peninsula Insider

**Document version:** v1
**Last updated:** 2026-11-04

This is the foundational context document every marketing skill and loop reads
before doing PI work (SEO, content, newsletter, social, partner, paid). It
compiles what is already decided in the strategy and brand vault — it does not
introduce new positioning, pricing or research. Where a source document is
more detailed, this doc links to it rather than repeating it.

## Product Overview

**One-liner:** Peninsula Insider is the editorial guide to the Mornington
Peninsula — local, opinionated, and built to turn planning into a booked
weekend.

**What it does:** A content and discovery platform (astro site, Pagefind
search, Supabase-backed dynamic features, an "Ask The Insider" concierge) that
helps Melbourne-based planners and locals decide what to do, eat, drink, and
book on the Peninsula, and helps local operators get discovered by people
already planning a visit.

**Product category:** Local editorial guide / destination discovery platform
— not a tourism-board directory, not a generic travel marketplace.

**Product type:** Content publisher with a structured-data product layer
(venues, events, offers, itineraries) and a B2B partner-listing side.

**Business model:** Free for readers. Revenue (not yet activated at scale,
see Current Status below) is designed around: (1) paid partner/venue listings
on a tiered ladder, Basic through Premium (A$79–1,500/month per
[[tier-ladder]], pricing not published publicly), (2) affiliate/booking
referral links, (3) the Insider's Letter newsletter as an audience and trust
asset. **See Current Status — none of the partner or paid-newsletter revenue
paths are live as of 4 November 2026.**

**Positioning — James's call, not finalised here.** The master strategy
([[master-strategy]]) carries this recommended positioning statement, written
by Remy and not yet confirmed by James as the final public positioning:

> For visitors, locals, and Peninsula-based businesses who need the region to
> be easier to discover, plan, and book, Peninsula Insider is the trusted
> local discovery and demand-routing platform that combines editorial
> judgement, structured local data, and AI concierge support, because generic
> travel platforms do not know the Peninsula with enough taste, specificity,
> or commercial usefulness.

Brand promise (from [[master-strategy]]): **Decide well. Do more on the
Mornington Peninsula.** Brand essence: **Trusted local guidance that turns
intent into action.**

The commercial-voice calibration ([[commercial-voice]]) gives the one-line
version used on partner-facing surfaces specifically:

> Peninsula Insider is the editorial publication for the Mornington
> Peninsula. We work with a small number of Peninsula businesses each year,
> told properly, on the publication's terms.

## Target Audience

Two audiences, per [[master-strategy]] and [[icp-and-targets]]:

### 1. Readers (consumer side, free)

- **Melbourne-based weekend planners** — the primary persona. Visit the
  Peninsula 4–10 times a year, plan from Melbourne, decide Thursday–Friday
  where to eat, stay, and go.
- **Locals** — want current ideas (weekend, family, dog-friendly, rainy-day)
  without relying on scattered social posts.
- Secondary, underserved personas identified in [[2026-04-09-strategic-review]]
  but not yet built for: dreamers/inspiration browsers, day-trippers, and a
  family/kid-specific pathway.

**Jobs to be done:** decide what to do this weekend; find somewhere specific
worth the drive (not generic "best of" lists); plan a day or weekend around a
constraint (rain, kids, dog, budget, occasion); trust that a recommendation is
honest, including when it says skip something.

### 2. Venues and operators (B2B/partner side, paused — see Current Status)

Priority segments from [[icp-and-targets]], ranked P1: wineries/cellar doors,
restaurants, accommodation, wellness/spa, event organisers, attractions, tour
operators, breweries/distilleries. P2: wedding/corporate venues, cafes,
markets, visitor-relevant services. P3: galleries, retailers.

**Jobs to be done:** get discovered by people already planning a Peninsula
visit; convert attention into bookings, enquiries, or event attendance; prove
the value of a paid listing with simple reporting.

## Problems and Pain Points

**For readers:** generic travel content (Visit Victoria, TripAdvisor, Google
Maps, metro lifestyle sites) is broad, not locally specific, and does not
have an opinion. It tells you *what exists*, not *what's worth it this week*.

**For venues:** generic directories give visibility without curation or
proof; social reach is ephemeral and un-measurable; the Peninsula has no
category-leading local media brand to be discovered through (this is the gap
[[master-strategy]] and [[2026-04-09-strategic-review]] both identify).

## Competitive Landscape

From [[2026-04-09-strategic-review]], [[2026-05-24-strategy-review]] and
[[brand-strategy]]:

| Competitor | What they do well | Where they fall short | What PI should take / avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Broadsheet** (Mornington Peninsula pages) | Premium editorial curation, brand equity, town × category URL structure | Treats the Peninsula as a Melbourne sub-region, not a destination in its own right; limited regional depth | Take: editorial standards, clean URL/entity model. Avoid: becoming a generic metro-lifestyle clone |
| **Visit Mornington Peninsula** | Official, broad, trusted for basic facts | Legacy directory behaviour, no editorial point of view, no planning utility | Avoid competing on breadth; own curation and opinion instead |
| **Time Out / Urban List / Concrete Playground** | Event/lifestyle discovery, social reach, commercial packages | Generic at scale, thin regional nuance | Take: category landing pages, newsletter cadence. Avoid: shallow listicle format |
| **TripAdvisor / Google Reviews** | Crowd-sourced validation | No discovery or planning value, no curation | Avoid trying to out-review; use as a trust signal only, not a format to copy |
| **AGFG, The Ninch, The Travel Textbook** | Category-specific authority (dining guides) | Narrower scope than a full destination guide | Watch for content-gap and link-building opportunities (see [[2026-11-04-marketing-skills-implementation-review]]) |

PI's opportunity, per [[brand-strategy]]: combine editorial authority from
Broadsheet, event-utility discipline from Concrete Playground, and local
listing economics from comparable regional guides, without rebuilding
everything in-house — rather than out-breadthing Visit Mornington Peninsula or
out-reviewing TripAdvisor.

## Differentiation

- **Editorial judgement with an actual opinion.** PI names what's overrated
  and what to skip — the thing a generic directory or tourism board will not
  do. ("One Thing Not to Do" is the signature newsletter block; see
  [[newsletter-product]].)
- **Local specificity, not brochure language.** Named places, seasons, drive
  times, booking windows — not "world-class dining" or "hidden gem" (see
  [[voice-and-tone]] words-to-avoid list).
- **Structured local data underneath an editorial front end.** Positions PI
  for search, AI retrieval, and partner reporting in a way a card-grid
  directory cannot support (see [[master-strategy]] Product Strategy
  section).
- **A named editor, not an anonymous CMS.** The Insider's Letter is signed;
  the site voice is a local host, not brochure copy (see
  [[2026-04-09-strategic-review]] §2.2 on the missing "I").

## Objections and Anti-Persona

| Objection (heard from venues/partners) | Response |
|---|---|
| "You don't have the traffic/audience yet." | Acknowledged directly in [[2026-11-04-marketing-skills-implementation-review]] as the known "traffic objection" blocking the partner pitch. Partner selling is currently paused (see Current Status) rather than pitched against this objection. |
| "How is this different from just buying a Broadsheet or Visit Mornington Peninsula listing?" | PI's pitch is curation and category scarcity (a small number of businesses per category, told properly) rather than broad reach — see [[commercial-voice]]. |

**Anti-persona:** Operators looking for mass-reach advertising rather than
curated editorial placement; businesses outside the Peninsula; anyone wanting
content without editorial disclosure (PI's commercial-disclosure framework is
non-negotiable per [[commercial-voice]]).

## Customer Language

**Words to use** (from [[voice-and-tone]]): local, current, nearby, best for,
worth booking, plan, save, this weekend, family-friendly, dog-friendly, rainy
day, verified, Insider, seasonal, last verified, check before you go.

**Words to avoid:** tourism clichés — hidden gem, must-see, must-visit,
bucket-list, ultimate, amazing, stunning, epic, unforgettable,
picture-perfect, gateway to, nestled in, step back in time, something for
everyone, perfect for. Also avoid robotic/AI-sounding language: leverage,
seamless, elevate, curate experiences, immersive journey, crafted with care,
truly unique, world-class.

**Glossary:**

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| The Insider's Letter | The weekly (currently paused — see Current Status) newsletter product, every Thursday |
| Ask The Insider | The source-grounded concierge/RAG surface for planning questions |
| Founding Partner | The time-limited entry product for the first partner cohort, sits outside the standard tier ladder |
| This Weekend on the Peninsula | The recurring weekly recommendation habit/format |

## Brand Voice

Full canonical rules live in [[voice-and-tone]] (consumer/editorial surfaces)
and [[commercial-voice]] (partner/commercial surfaces) — do not duplicate
them here, read those files directly. Summary only:

| Attribute | Means | Doesn't mean |
|---|---|---|
| Local | Knows the Peninsula in practice | Brochure familiarity |
| Grounded | Specific, concrete detail (hours, drive time, season) | Vibes-only description |
| Editorially confident | One real opinion per recommendation | Hedge-everything neutrality |
| Warm but not gushy | Friendly, current, useful | Exclamation marks, superlatives |

**Hard rules carried over:** no em-dashes; active voice; disclose paid
placement clearly; one opinion per recommendation.

**Tone by surface** (full table in [[voice-and-tone]]): homepage confident and
lightly aspirational; article body knowledgeable and opinionated; vendor-facing
commercially credible and measured; newsletter friendly and useful.

## Proof Points

- **Editorial depth:** ~623 sitemap URLs, Eat and Wine categories with
  genuine curation density (per [[2026-04-09-strategic-review]]).
- **Regional market size:** Visit Mornington Peninsula reports 8.274M annual
  visitors, A$1.8B direct visitor expenditure, 3,903 tourism businesses
  ([[master-strategy]], cited from Visit Mornington Peninsula's own
  reporting).
- **What's not yet proof:** subscriber count, open/click rates, paid partner
  revenue, and organic traffic are all pre-scale as of 4 November 2026 (see
  Current Status). Do not cite audience-size claims externally until these
  move.

## Goals

**Primary business goal (per [[2026-11-04-marketing-skills-implementation-review]]):**
build the audience engine before the commercial engine — PI's three revenue
paths (partner listings, affiliate links, the Insider's Letter) are all
currently blocked by reach, not by product design.

**Key conversion actions:** newsletter signup, partner enquiry, outbound
booking/affiliate click (per the conversion-tracking definition added to the
measurement card, same source document).

**Current metrics (verified 4 November 2026, Beehiiv/GSC):** 36 active
newsletter subscribers, 1 new subscriber in 90 days, 0 opens/clicks in 90 days
on Beehiiv (send status unverified), 12 organic clicks and 1,587 impressions
at average position 65 over the 31 Aug–27 Sep window, $0 partner revenue, GA4
instrumented but not yet readable by any agent lane.

## Current Status — decisions in force (James, 4 November 2026)

These four pauses and permissions govern every skill and loop that reads this
document until James changes them. Source:
[[2026-11-04-marketing-skills-implementation-review]].

1. **The Insider's Letter is not currently sending.** Treat the newsletter as
   designed but dormant, not as a live weekly product, until restarted.
2. **Partner selling is not activated.** The Founding Partner programme and
   tier ladder are designed, not sold. Do not draft or send partner pitches,
   outreach, or sales sequences.
3. **Pitches and venue emails are paused.** PR and link-building work is
   research-only — prospect lists and linkable-asset ideas, with no drafted
   or sent outreach.
4. **AI crawling and training on public text is permitted.** `robots.txt`
   allows every crawler and stays as-is; the Terms
   (`next/src/lib/retrieval-permission.mjs`) permit training on public text.
   This excludes photos and third-party rights content.

Any skill output that would conflict with these four points (a pitch email, a
partner sales sequence, a newsletter send) stays a draft held for James, not
an action taken.
