# Editor's Letter — Week 21, 2026

**Published:** Fri 2026-05-22 08:00
**Drafted:** Sun 2026-05-17 (week prior)

The roadside sign at the cellar door said *Open* on Tuesday, but the gravel hadn't been raked in a fortnight and the woman behind the counter looked surprised to see anyone. She poured the tasting anyway, with the grace of someone who has done it a thousand times to half-empty rooms.

This is the Peninsula in late May. The flat, honest version.

There is a tendency, when an audience is paying attention, to keep pretending the weekend just gone is the weekend still happening. Cafés that have quietly gone to four days leave the website unchanged. The kitchen running a stripped-back menu calls it a "winter edit". The cellar door that closes Mondays and Tuesdays describes itself as open daily, with seasonal hours. Everyone is being slightly dishonest, in the cheerful way small businesses are when survival depends on it.

I don't blame them. I do think we owe readers better.

So a position, for the record. From this week, Peninsula Insider will write about the off-season the way it actually is. Which kitchens are open this Tuesday — not the ones that were open last summer. Which cellar doors take walk-ins between now and August, and which ones want a phone call first. Which weekends are quiet enough that you can have the bay road to yourself for an hour, and which ones have been quietly sold out for a month by the same people who come every May.

This is not a moral position. The Peninsula in May has its own grammar, and the readers who matter to us understand it. The mistake is to keep writing as if it were March. The longer fix is harder: we will note which operators are honest about their hours and which aren't, and write accordingly. Lucien is already working through it.

Expect the next few weeks to read a little colder. More fires than terraces. More small dining rooms with steamed-up windows than long lunches. A planner for the Queen's Birthday weekend lands in a fortnight; Iris is on it.

The cellar door on Tuesday, in case you were wondering, was excellent. She poured a 2019 chardonnay that hadn't quite gone where the winemaker meant it to, and was the better for it. The gravel will get raked in October.

— Tyler Lockhart, Editor
