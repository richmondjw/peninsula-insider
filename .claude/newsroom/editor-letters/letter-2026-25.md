# Editor's Letter — Week 25, 2026

**Published:** Fri 19 Jun 2026, 08:00 AEST
**Drafted:** Sunday 15 June 2026
**Length:** 392 / target 350–450
**Format:** A (seasonal framing)

---

The week before the solstice is the Peninsula's most honest argument for itself.

Everything else in the year depends, in some way, on what the weather is doing. November rides the anticipation of what is coming. January does not need to try. Even the shoulder seasons have their alibi — the grapes are just arriving, or just leaving; the visitors are tapering off, or building up again. But the third week of June has no such luck. It is winter because it is winter, and whatever the Peninsula is in this light, at this temperature, with this particular number of people in the room, is what the Peninsula actually is.

In practice, most of it holds up rather well. This is not faint praise.

The truffle season is now three weeks old, which means the initial frenzy has subsided and the more interesting question has arrived: what are people actually doing with them. Lucien has been asking that of kitchens across the region, and his report this week is less a directory of options than an argument about which approaches to the ingredient make sense here specifically — on a peninsula with a particular character and no especially convincing reason to pretend otherwise. The answer, as it often is with Lucien, is more specific and more opinionated than you might expect.

Margot has put together the mid-winter field guide we have been building toward since May. A considered account of what the Peninsula offers in this particular window, organised around the premise that some things are genuinely better now than at any other point in the year, rather than merely available in winter as a consolation. The distinction is worth making. We have tried to apply it honestly.

Iris has the weekend picks for 20 to 21 June, which fall on the solstice itself — the shortest weekend of the year, with the longest nights and, arguably, the clearest reasons to stay out here rather than retreat back to the city. She has found several.

The issue after this one arrives from the other side of the solstice. Technically, everything gets lighter from here. Whether you believe that in the third week of June in southern Victoria is a matter of temperament. I tend not to, but I have been wrong before.

— Tyler Lockhart, Editor
