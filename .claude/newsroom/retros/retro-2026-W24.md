## 3. What did we learn?

**Three things, and one structural truth.**

**First: the commissioning meeting is the load-bearing wall.** We do not have a commissioning meeting → we do not have a locked slate → we do not have a cover, a standing column, or an Editor's Letter → the site runs on Weekend Picks alone. That is what happened in W24. The causal chain is direct and total. Every upstream gap cascades. The ritual is not bureaucracy. It is the thing that makes the rest possible.

**Second: standing columns need a harder floor than "commissioned."** Lucien's column is the most distinctive editorial product Peninsula Insider has. Two consecutive misses means it is being treated like a commission with a soft deadline rather than a standing obligation with a firm one. The mechanism for catching this — the Wednesday checkpoint — requires a locked slate to operate properly. Without a slate, the checkpoint has no reference to check against. The fix for standing columns runs through the fix for the commissioning meeting.

**Third: Iris is carrying the site.** Iris has delivered consistently — ahead of schedule, at quality — across W23 and W24. She wrote the weekend picks in both weeks. She filed the only piece that shipped in W24. The site's published editorial content right now is essentially Iris. That is not a sustainable arrangement, and it understates her contribution while letting others drift. W25 commissioning must address the workload imbalance.

**Structural truth: the rhythm is broken, not thin.** This is not a capacity problem or a content problem. The editorial product exists — the ideas are there, the beats are there, the talent is there. The rhythm that makes it possible to convert ideas into published pieces is not running reliably. Commissioning meetings skipped, slates not written, retros not held. Three weeks between retros. This retro is the first artifact in the `retros/` folder since W21. The operational layer is mature. The editorial management layer is not keeping pace with it.
