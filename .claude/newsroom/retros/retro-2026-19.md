Bottom line: the service-journalism engine worked. We shipped useful local planning coverage and kept the site moving.

## 2) What slipped and why?

The main slip was not content volume. It was **editorial operating discipline**.

- **No locked weekly slate** was filed for Week 19.
- **No newsroom artifact trail** existed until the perf note; output lived in commits and built pages instead of in the newsroom system.
- Several improvements landed as reactive maintenance and autofix work rather than through a declared editorial plan.
- Ask PI demand remained weak after product work: usage reports show **2 reader queries on 2 May, then 0 queries on 3–6 May**.

Why it slipped:

1. **Publishing outran process** — the team kept shipping, but the newsroom scaffolding was not maintained.
2. **Too much work happened as infrastructure/repair cadence** — useful, but it blurs promised work versus opportunistic work.
3. **Distribution/product attention on Ask PI lagged behind build attention** — we improved the surface, but not enough people used it.

## 3) What did we learn?

- Peninsula Insider is strongest when it solves **one immediate planning job** for readers. The Mother's Day package is the clean example.
- Quick notes are valuable when they **feed a larger editorial package**; on their own, they risk feeling like drift.
- Shipping volume is not the same as editorial control. Without a locked slate, we cannot cleanly say what was promised, what changed, and what won.
- Ask PI's current problem is not just content supply. It looks more like a **distribution and product-discovery problem**.

## 4) One change for next week

**Change agreed:** Reinstate a locked weekly slate before publishing starts on Monday, and require every major package or maintenance burst to map to that slate.

That gives us one source of truth for what we intended to ship, what changed, and why.
