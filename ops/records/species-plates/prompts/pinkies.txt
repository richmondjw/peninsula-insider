A juvenile Australian snapper, Chrysophrys auratus, the fish anglers call a pinky, about a hand and a half long. The same species as an adult snapper but young: a shallower, more oval and less deep body, no hump at all on the snout or nape, and a smoothly rounded forehead. Bright clean rose pink over the back and flanks, silvery white below. Numerous small brilliant electric blue spots scattered boldly across the upper flanks and back, vivid and distinct, far more prominent than on an adult. Eye large in proportion to the small head. One continuous dorsal fin, forked tail.
