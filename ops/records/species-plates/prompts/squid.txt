A southern calamari squid, Sepioteuthis australis, in lateral view. This is a squid, not a cuttlefish: the mantle is a long slender torpedo cone that narrows to a point at the rear, not a broad flat oval, and there is no thick internal cuttlebone giving it a rounded humped back. Wide muscular fins run down both sides along almost the whole length of the mantle, from the pointed rear tip forward toward the head, meeting at the tip to form a long narrow spearhead outline. At the head, ten appendages: eight short stout arms of similar length held together in a tapering bundle pointing forward and slightly down, and two longer slender tentacles reaching further forward and ending in widened clubbed tips. One large round dark eye set on the side of the head. Mottled translucent reddish brown and golden speckles dusted over a pearly cream body.
