Something shifted this week. The 15-degree sunny run that made last week's Peninsula days feel worth a detour is done. What you get now is 12 degrees, overcast, and a different kind of quietness — the one that belongs to the Peninsula between late autumn and the first proper winter plan.

This edition picks for that register. Different walks, different rooms, a closing cultural window that won't be here in three weeks, and one booking that has a hard deadline.

## Lock in the final forage before Sunday

**Wild Mushroom Forage and Lunch with The Kitchen. Saturday 30 May, 11am–1:30pm. $85 per person. Red Hill and Main Ridge forests. Strictly capped at 15.**

The session this Saturday (May 23) is covered in the weekend dispatch. This pick is about the one after it.

Saturday 30 May is the final session of the season. The format is the same: a guide with fifteen-plus years behind them, a two-hour walk through the Red Hill and Main Ridge forests looking for the edible wild mushrooms that come up after the autumn rains, then the cook-up on the spot. Butter, herbs, warm bread, what the morning found. Fifteen people maximum, no exceptions.

The Kitchen takes no online bookings. Contact them directly. If this week passes without a booking, it becomes the question of what is left.

The timing note: there is one more session on the calendar after the forage closes. Red Hill Truffles season opens the same morning, Saturday 30 May. If you book the forage and the truffle hunt in the same week, you have earned a serious late-autumn weekend.

## The walk for this weather

**Greens Bush (Two Bays section). Greens Bush Carpark, off Baldrys Road, Merricks North. 4 hours, moderate.**

Last week's pick was about coastal light. This week's is about everything the Peninsula has when the light goes grey.

Greens Bush is 8.9 kilometres one-way through the largest remnant bushland on the Mornington Peninsula. The canopy closes overhead in places, old grasstrees — some over two hundred years old — stand through the fern gullies and tea-tree, and the track passes through a landscape that looks largely as it would have before European settlement. Birds work the understory constantly. You will hear more than you see.

The walk is autumn and winter best, not despite the overcast but partly because of it. The light is even, the colours are sharp, and the crowds that make Point Nepean and Cape Schanck feel compressed on a clear-sky Saturday are not here.

Walk it as a return circuit from the Greens Bush Carpark or do the full one-way with a car shuffle and someone else's opinion about where to eat at the other end. Merricks General Wine Store is the nearest reasonable stop.

## Two weeks left — Tall Poppy, Moorooduc

**Kelli Lundberg Art @ The Studio & Co., Moorooduc. Closes 7 June. Free entry, no booking.**

The claim with Tall Poppy is that it is the first time NGV's Melbourne Design Week has programmed an exhibition on the Mornington Peninsula. The Melbourne Design Week 2026 catalogue corroborates it. Whether or not the claim holds exactly, the show is worth the drive for independent reasons.

Fifteen Australian designers and makers: furniture, ceramics, textiles, design objects. Curated by Tiffany Jade, who works from inside the same Studio & Co. precinct — a converted 1920s grocery building in Moorooduc with twenty-plus maker tenants. The space is working creative infrastructure, not a white-cube gallery. You will likely find people actually making things while you look at the show.

The show closes 7 June. Two weekends of life remain. The natural Saturday: lunch on the Red Hill ridge, then down to Moorooduc for the exhibition in the early afternoon. Accessibility is notably well-considered for a regional venue: wheelchair access, accessible bathrooms, online viewing option.

Open most days 10am to 4pm. 10am to 3pm Sundays. Closed Tuesdays. Parking via Derril Road.

## The dinner for this moment

**Tedesca Osteria, 96 Junction Road, Red Hill.**

There are two versions of Tedesca. The terrace version, which earns its two hats in the warm months on a clear Peninsula afternoon. And the winter version, which earns them a different way: wood-oven heat, a set menu that runs on what the cold season actually offers, a room that is warm in the particular way that rooms with kitchens in them are warm.

This week is the transition point. The terrace is no longer the draw. The room is.

Brigitte Hafner's kitchen runs on produce and wood and the kind of cooking that does not announce itself. The set menu means you have given up control, which is the correct call. Book three to four weeks ahead for King's Birthday if that is the plan; for a quieter mid-week or non-occasion Saturday, earlier availability is usually there.

The booking: tedescasteria.com.au. The room is small. Give it the first call of the week.
