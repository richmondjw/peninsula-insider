**WINE - Crittenden Estate, Dromana**

Crittenden's second generation is doing things with Italian and Spanish varieties that nobody else on the Peninsula attempts. Right now the cellar door carries skin-contact and amphora-aged experiments from winemaker Rollo Crittenden that are worth tasting before the summer traffic makes the room loud.

The estate was founded in 1982 by Garry Crittenden, which makes it one of the originals. The Los Hermanos range - built around Arneis, Sangiovese, Tempranillo, and Garganega - is where the interesting work happens. The amphora-aged Arneis in particular reads completely differently from anything produced through conventional winemaking on the Peninsula: textured, slightly oxidative, and genuinely surprising. Ask the team at the counter to walk you through the current skin-contact releases rather than defaulting to the standard Pinot tasting.

The cellar door sits at 25 Harrisons Road, Dromana. Walk-ins are welcome daily; allow an hour to do the range justice. Check the website for current hours before you drive.

Pair the visit with a stop at Small Stone Pantry for lunch on the way back toward the highway.

**WALK - Cape Schanck Boardwalk, Cape Schanck**

Coastal teatree is flowering along the Cape Schanck headland right now - small white blooms banking up on the windward side of the track - and the boardwalk out to Pulpit Rock is at its best before the December school holiday crowds arrive.

The Parks Victoria boardwalk runs from the lighthouse car park to the Pulpit Rock viewing platform above Bass Strait. It is 1.2 km return and takes around 35 minutes at an unhurried pace. Bass Strait is visible the whole way out, and on a clear November morning the offshore light is flat and bright rather than the low winter glare that makes the same walk cold and squinting. The lighthouse itself dates to 1859 and is still operational; the heritage buildings are open for inspection on certain days. The clifftop has no shelter, so bring something for wind even in spring warmth.

Parks Victoria manages the site. A vehicle entry fee applies - check Parks Victoria's website for current rates. Arrive before 10am on Saturday or Sunday to get the track without families four abreast.

The Foreshore café at Flinders is a 12-minute drive east if you want coffee afterward.

**DISCOVERY - Emu Plains Market, Balnarring**

The Emu Plains Market at Balnarring Reserve runs every Saturday until 19 December, and November is the right month: summer produce is arriving but the stalls are not yet overrun by visitors who discovered the market on Instagram two weeks ago.

The market runs 9am to 2pm at Emu Plains Reserve in Balnarring - a paddock-edge setting that is genuinely different from the paved main-street formats elsewhere on the Peninsula. Growers bring early-season tomatoes, zucchinis starting to come through, cut flowers, eggs, and bread. The scale stays manageable: enough stalls to fill a basket, not so many that you lose an hour searching for the exit. The producers who trade here tend to be from the inland Balnarring and Merricks corridor, which is different growing country from the ridge, and the range reflects that.

Check emuplainsmarket.com.au before Saturday to confirm which growers are trading; the stall list shifts week to week. Allow 45 minutes. No booking required.

Hurley Vineyard in Balnarring is five minutes from the reserve, and the cellar door is worth the detour afterward.
