The winter program at Mornington Peninsula Regional Gallery is worth the drive. Four exhibitions and a children's space make it a full afternoon, and the programming across the rooms is more considered than you typically find in a regional winter season.

## What's on

All exhibitions run until Sunday 23 August 2026, and entry is free.

The anchor show is **Helen Britton: The Story So Far**, a touring exhibition from the Australian Design Centre's Living Treasures series. Britton is a jeweller, and the work deals with memory and creative legacy across a career. It is the kind of exhibition that rewards slow looking rather than a lap of the room, and the Living Treasures framing gives it a retrospective weight that earns the time you give it.

Alongside it, **Natalia Milosz-Piekarska: Sifted Light** takes peninsula textures as its raw material, translating them into sculptural jewellery work. If you spend time on this coastline, you will recognise something in it. The two jewellery shows sit well together without repeating each other.

**In the Company of Animals** draws from the MPRG's own collection and sits comfortably between the more concentrated craft-focused rooms. It functions as a natural breathing point in the sequence, which is useful when you are moving through four exhibitions in one visit.

The fourth show is the one to note for its context. **Synthetic Memories**, by Barcelona-based studio Domestic Data Streamers, presents AI visual reconstructions of remembered moments. It is an Australian-first presentation, funded through the Shire's Positive Ageing initiative. The pairing of AI reconstruction with a jewellery show about memory and legacy is not accidental. That thread runs across the whole program, and the gallery deserves credit for the coherence of it.

A children's activation space runs alongside the main exhibitions. It makes this a workable outing with kids rather than a negotiation, and it means the adults can take their time in the Britton room without the usual compromise.

## The rainy day circuit

The gallery is on Dunns Road, Mornington. Start the morning at **Commonfolk Coffee** on Progress Street, a short distance away. It is a roastery cafe on an industrial street, which is to say it is unpretentious and focused on what it does. Weekdays it opens at 6.30am, weekends at 8am, closing at 3.30pm and 3pm respectively. Arrive before the gallery opens at 11am, take a table, order something to eat if the morning calls for it, and treat the hour as the warm-up rather than the afterthought.

The gallery runs Tuesday to Sunday, 11am to 4pm. Allow two hours if you are moving through all four exhibitions properly. The Britton and Milosz-Piekarska shows in particular are not ones to rush, and the programming logic across the rooms is worth sitting with rather than skimming past.

For lunch, the sequence works best if you head to **Rare Hare**, the vineyard restaurant in the vines beside Jackalope hotel. Lunch runs from 12pm every day. Book ahead: walk-ins are limited. A walk-ins-only wine lounge is available if you arrive without a reservation and want somewhere to sit with a glass while you reconsider the plan.

If the rain is still coming down by mid-afternoon and you have no particular reason to be anywhere, **Alba Thermal Springs** is open every day from 7am to 10pm, excluding Christmas Day. It is a logical extension of a day already organised around being indoors and warm. Check availability before you go, particularly if it is a weekend or school holiday period.
