**Avani Wines - 165 Shands Road, Red Hill South**

Avani is the Peninsula's most committed Syrah producer, and spring is the right time to visit. The vines are in full canopy push now, the cellar door is open before the December crush arrives, and the Amrit single-vineyard range is newly bottled from the 2025 vintage. The biodynamic farming shows most clearly at this time of year - walk the vineyard before you taste and the connection between what's growing and what's in the glass makes more sense. The skin-contact white is the conversation starter, but the Syrah is why you drove here. It has that cool-climate restraint that makes Peninsula Syrah different from anywhere warmer: pepper, iron, and patience. Buy at least one bottle for cellaring. Book ahead via the website; walk-ins are possible but the tasting experience is better when they're expecting you. Allow ninety minutes.

Pair the afternoon with a cheese plate from Red Hill Cheese, ten minutes up the ridge.

---

**Seawinds Garden - Arthurs Seat Road, Arthurs Seat**

The Arthur's Seat State Park gets most of the attention, but the Seawinds Garden on the same ridge is doing something more specific right now. The formal garden gives way to a native plant collection that peaks through November and into early December - grevillea, correa, and several hakea species in full flower at once. The loop track through the native section takes around 45 minutes at an unhurried pace. The view north across Port Phillip opens up at the garden's high point, which is worth pausing for. Come before 10am on a Saturday and you will likely have the garden almost to yourself; after noon it fills with Arthurs Seat Eagle visitors cutting through. No booking required. No entry fee. Dogs are welcome on lead. Wear shoes with grip - the path gets slippery after rain, and November delivers both rain and shine without warning.

Combine it with a coffee at Mornington on the way back down toward the freeway.

---

**Pub Carols at The Pig & Whistle - Main Ridge, from Thursday 3 December**

Pub carols done well is a specific thing: low production, high participation, a beer garden that smells of eucalyptus and grass, and a crowd that actually knows the words. The Pig & Whistle Tavern in Main Ridge has been running this format long enough that the Thursday night sessions have their own rhythm. The first night is Thursday 3 December, running through to 17 December, 6pm to 10pm each week. It is not a ticketed concert. It is a pub, with carols, in a shed in the middle of the Peninsula's farming ridge, which is precisely what makes it work. Bookings are available through Humanitix and worth securing for a table if you're coming as a group - it fills. The venue sits on a country road with limited parking, so coordinate arrivals. Children are welcome early in the evening.

Arrive hungry - the kitchen is open and the meals are straightforward and good.
