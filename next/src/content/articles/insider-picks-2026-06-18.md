The solstice falls on Saturday, and the Peninsula's editorial calendar is dominated by one big night on the Sorrento foreshore. This edition picks around it. Three things that feel strongest right now: one closing cultural window, one walk that winter actually improves, and one dinner that only makes sense in the cold months.

## See it before it closes - MPRG Autumn Exhibition

**Mornington Peninsula Regional Gallery, Mornington. Closes Monday 30 June. Free entry, no booking.**

The Autumn Exhibition at MPRG closes in twelve days. The show covers contemporary Australian painting, a rotating photography wall, and a Peninsula-focused historical exhibition in the side gallery. Entry is free, the gallery sits five minutes from Main Street, and the whole thing takes under an hour.

This is the week to go. The solstice crowd will be on the foreshore on Saturday; the gallery will be at its quietest. The rooms are warm, the lighting is proper, and on a grey mid-June afternoon the gallery fills the post-lunch hour better than any cafe on the Peninsula.

Allow 45 to 60 minutes. Pair it with lunch on Main Street either side, or walk down to the pier afterwards if the rain holds off. The family activity space at MPRG is genuinely useful if you are bringing children who have hit their limit of winter indoor time.

Open most days. Check mprg.mornpen.vic.gov.au for hours. Closed some public holidays.

## The walk for this weather

**Cape Schanck Lighthouse Walk. Cape Schanck Road, Cape Schanck. 90 minutes return, easy difficulty.**

The Cape Schanck Lighthouse walk is one of the most dramatic short coastal walks in Victoria, and mid-winter is when it is at its most theatrical. The 4-kilometre return track runs from the 1859 working lighthouse down to Pulpit Rock via a boardwalk that sits above sheer rock with ocean on both sides. In grey light and wind, the Bass Strait side of the Peninsula feels wilder and more remote than it does in any other season.

The boardwalk out to the headland is genuinely vertiginous. You are walking on a platform above rock with water on both sides, and in winter the swell is high enough that you can feel the scale of the place properly. The lighthouse itself runs tours on selected days; the view from the tower is among the best on the entire Peninsula.

Go early morning for the light, or late afternoon if you want the walk to end as the dark comes in. Check wind conditions before the boardwalk; the cape is exposed and the track is no place to be in a gale. Wear layers: the temperature drops noticeably on the headland even when Mornington is mild.

Pair with lunch in Flinders, or a warm drink at the Flinders Hotel. The drive from Cape Schanck to Flinders village takes ten minutes.

## The dinner for this moment

**Barragunda Dining, 165 Boneo Road, Cape Schanck.**

Barragunda is the Peninsula's most genuinely farm-to-table dining experience, and winter is when chef Simone Watts's kitchen makes the most sense. The 1000-acre regenerative estate at Cape Schanck supplies most of what appears on the plate. The menu changes weekly based on what the farm has produced that week. The room is quiet, intimate, and deliberately under-publicised in a way that feels almost rare in the modern Peninsula food scene.

The cooking is confident and seasonal in the most literal sense. Most courses are pulled from within walking distance of the dining room. The wine list leans into lower-intervention producers. The pace is slow. This is not a restaurant you book for a quick dinner; it is a restaurant you book for a winter evening when you have given up control and handed yourself over to the chef.

Book well in advance. The seats are limited, there are no walk-ins, and word has been spreading. For anyone who has already done the headline Peninsula restaurants and wants the one that locals talk about in winter, this is the call.

If you are staying overnight, RACV Cape Schanck Resort is the natural pairing. One Spa at the resort runs treatments that pair cleanly with a long dinner. The alternative is to drive home satisfied through the dark, which is its own kind of winter pleasure.
