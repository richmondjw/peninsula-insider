The Mornington Peninsula now has two serious geothermal bathhouses within fifteen minutes of each other, and deciding between them has become the single most common wellness question Peninsula visitors ask. Both are good. They are not interchangeable.

Peninsula Hot Springs opened in 2005 and invented the category in Australia. Alba Thermal Springs opened in 2022 and brought a different sensibility to it. On the right day in the right session, either can be the anchor of a Peninsula weekend.

Here is the side-by-side: what each does well, and which one fits which kind of day.

## Peninsula Hot Springs: what it is

Peninsula Hot Springs was the original. It opened in 2005, at a time when no Australian had a reference point for this kind of wellness category, and it has spent two decades expanding into one of the largest geothermal bathhouse complexes in the southern hemisphere.

The scale is the point. The property now contains:

- **The Bath House**: the original public-access bathing area, with more than twenty pools, a Turkish steam bath, cave pool, and reflexology walk
- **The Spa Dreaming Centre**: an adults-only premium bathing area with a smaller pool count but better pacing, plus treatment rooms
- **The Amphitheatre Hill Pool**: the newer 2020 addition, built up a slope with dramatic views, often the most photographed
- **Fire & Ice**: a dedicated hot-and-cold circuit with sauna, ice cave, and plunge pools

The scale is also the trade-off. Weekend daytime sessions can feel crowded in a way that undermines the relaxation. The Bath House welcomes all ages, with children 15 and under required to be accompanied by an adult; the Spa Dreaming Centre is reserved for guests 16 and over, and Moonlit Bathing (Friday and Saturday evenings) is 18 and over. Photography is hard to avoid in the Bath House. And the sheer size means first-timers often miss the best pools by wandering rather than following a ritual.

Bath House entry (Revitalise) sits in the mid tier depending on session and concession status. The all-of-site pass, Bath House and Spa Dreaming Centre together (Reset), is the premium tier, with a discounted Reset Twilight rate for Sunday to Thursday evening entry after 6pm. Same-day bookings attract a premium and public holidays a further surcharge, so booking ahead is worth doing for both the price and the guaranteed arrival time.

**To get the most out of Peninsula Hot Springs:** book a midweek morning session if you can, or a Twilight session (late afternoon through sunset, or the discounted Reset Twilight rate) on any day. The Reset all-of-site pass or the Spa Dreaming Centre alone gives quieter pacing than the Bath House on a weekend. Move through the Hilltop Pool, the Reflexology Walk, and the cave pool properly. Budget three hours minimum.

## Alba Thermal Springs: what it is

  <img src="/images/sourced/spa-alba-thermal-springs-01.webp" alt="Alba Thermal Springs geothermal pools and bathhouse architecture at Fingal, Mornington Peninsula" loading="lazy" />
  <figcaption>Alba Thermal Springs, Fingal - one architectural move, more than twenty-five pools. Photo: Alba Thermal Springs.</figcaption>
</figure>

Alba opened in 2022 on a quieter site a few minutes inland from Peninsula Hot Springs, built by a different operator with a different sensibility.

Where Peninsula Hot Springs has been expanded across two decades, Alba was designed in one move. The architecture by Hayball is confident and sculptural rather than organic. The more than twenty-five geothermal pools, including a rain pool, cold plunge pools, botanical and forest pools, and a sunset pool, are arranged through the landscape with proper pacing (hot, warm, plunge, vitality) and the pathways are adult-scaled. There is a well-resolved wet-area spa building. There is a restaurant, Thyme, serving locally sourced produce all day. And the ticketing caps bather numbers so the experience holds together on a Saturday at 2pm.

Alba's general springs are open to all ages: guests under 16 must be accompanied by an adult and infants need a swim nappy, so this is not the strict adults-only retreat it is sometimes assumed to be. It is the private springs experiences and the spa that are reserved for guests 16 and over, with 16-18 year olds needing an adult present. Springs hours run 7am to 10pm daily (last arrival 8.30pm); private springs run 9am to 9pm (last arrival 8pm).

For winter, Alba runs its Winter Warmers season: guests arriving after 6pm are offered a complimentary glass of mulled wine by the firepit, weather permitting, and the geothermal water still runs 37-43 degrees regardless of the air temperature outside. It is worth timing a winter visit for late afternoon into evening for that reason - the cold-air, hot-water contrast is the whole appeal of a Peninsula soak in July or August.

What Alba does well that Peninsula Hot Springs does not match:

- **Crowd control**: caps on session numbers make the whole experience feel like a European bath day rather than a tourist destination
- **Design coherence**: one architectural voice, one material palette, one spatial logic
- **Quiet pacing**: booking in advance and arriving on time matters here more than at Peninsula Hot Springs, and the payoff is a calmer, more contained day
- **Spa building**: the treatment area is significantly better resolved than Peninsula Hot Springs' Spa Dreaming Centre

What Peninsula Hot Springs still does better than Alba:

- **Scale of bathing options**: if you want seventy-plus pools to move through, Alba's twenty-five-plus still feel smaller
- **Programming and events**: PHS runs sound baths, breathwork sessions, and wellness events Alba does not currently replicate
- **Price flexibility**: PHS has entry tiers from Bath House standard up to the all-of-site Reset pass; Alba's springs pricing sits in a single premium band

> Prices and hours change seasonally at both venues, and Alba adds a 15% surcharge for guests who arrive without a booking. Confirm current rates and availability directly with the venue before booking.

**To get the most out of Alba:** book a morning session on any day. Do the full thermal circuit properly rather than hopping. Have lunch on the Red Hill ridge afterwards at <a href="/wine/ten-minutes-by-tractor/">Ten Minutes by Tractor</a> or <a href="/wine/port-phillip-estate/">Port Phillip Estate</a> and you have one of the most complete Peninsula days available.

## Pacing them across a weekend

Both are three-to-four hour commitments done properly, so a single day rarely holds both well: back-to-back bathing tends to blur, the skin complains, and you miss the best pools at each. The better model is Alba on the Saturday of one Peninsula weekend and Peninsula Hot Springs on the Saturday of another. Or Alba on arrival day and Peninsula Hot Springs the following morning if you are staying two nights, with a real break and a meal in between.

## Before you book

- **Session timing matters more than venue choice.** A Tuesday morning at Peninsula Hot Springs beats a Saturday 2pm at Alba for pure relaxation. Book off-peak if the calendar allows, and book ahead: Peninsula Hot Springs adds a 10% same-day premium, and Alba adds a 15% surcharge for guests without a booking.
- **Check the age rules for your session.** Peninsula Hot Springs' Bath House and Alba's general springs both welcome under-16s with an adult; the Spa Dreaming Centre and Alba's private springs and spa are 16+; Moonlit Bathing at Peninsula Hot Springs is 18+.
- **Check seasonal closures and cancellation windows.** Both venues run occasional maintenance closures, and both charge the full amount for late cancellations (24 hours' notice at Peninsula Hot Springs, 48 hours at Alba). Call or check the website before booking a long-distance trip around a specific session.
- **Bring layers.** Peninsula winters are cold on the walk between pools. Both venues provide towels and robes, but you want warm clothing for the car and the cafe.
- **Phones are discouraged in both bathhouses.** Respect that: the experience is designed for it.
