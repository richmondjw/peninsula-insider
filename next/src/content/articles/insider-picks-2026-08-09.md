**Stonier Fire & Wine Winter Lunch, Merricks - today only**

Stonier runs these fire-side winter lunches selectively, and today's is the one on the calendar. The format centres on Stonier's Merricks estate, where the cellar door dining space gets a proper working fire and the kitchen builds a menu around the current-vintage Pinot Noirs and the Chardonnay that keeps appearing on serious restaurant lists. Winter is genuinely the right time to drink Stonier's Merricks Pinot - the tannin structure makes sense when it's cold outside and something is burning inside. This is not a casual drop-in situation. Bookings are required and the event is today, 9 August. If you don't have a reservation, call the winery directly on the off-chance of a cancellation - it's worth trying. Allow three hours minimum. Stonier Wines, 2 Thompsons Lane, Merricks; stonier.com.au/visit.

Pair this with an afternoon walk down to Merricks Beach while the light holds.

---

**Whale watching from the Cape Schanck boardwalk - August window**

August is the peak month for humpbacks and southern right whales moving east through Bass Strait on the winter migration corridor. Cape Schanck's boardwalk puts you roughly forty metres above the water at a point that juts far enough into the strait to give proper sightlines both directions along the coast. Arrive before eleven on a clear, low-wind morning - afternoon sea haze cuts visibility fast. Bring binoculars. The whales move in groups of two to five, breaching less frequently than the Instagram highlights suggest, but the blows are visible at distance on a calm day and that's enough. The lighthouse circuit is forty minutes return; the full boardwalk descent to the beach adds another twenty. No booking, no fee for the boardwalk itself - Parks Victoria manages the site. Cape Schanck Road, Cape Schanck; parks.vic.gov.au.

Follow this with coffee and something warm at Georgie Bass in Flinders, fifteen minutes back up the road.
