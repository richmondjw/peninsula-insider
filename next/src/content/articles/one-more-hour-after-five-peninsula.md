October changes the useful part of the Peninsula day.

At 2am on Sunday 4 October 2026, Victoria moves clocks forward one hour. Victorian government schools begin Term 4 the next morning. The practical shift is simple: routine returns, but the evening now holds more usable light.

Do not spend that hour building a longer itinerary. Choose the shape before the place. The right question after five is not *what can we fit in?* It is *what do the time, weather and energy support tonight?*

## Before you leave

Make four checks:

1. **Time:** decide when you will turn back, not only when you will start.
2. **Weather:** read the current Bureau of Meteorology forecast and warnings, including wind and rain.
3. **Access:** check the current notice from the land manager responsible for the route. Do not use an old article as proof that a track, gate or car park is open.
4. **Finish:** keep dinner or the final stop near the route, so the extra light is not lost to another long drive.

If one check fails, use the simpler plan. Changing the plan is the useful decision, not a compromised version of the original one.

## Plan one: a little movement

**Use it when:** you have 45 to 60 minutes, enough energy for a walk, and conditions suit an outdoor route.

Choose a short out-and-back section of a familiar foreshore rather than promising yourself the full trail. Mornington and Safety Beach both let you control the distance: set the turn-back time before leaving and return by the same route.

Keep the first outing deliberately small. The win is finishing with enough light and energy left to make dinner easy.

**Weather alternative:** if the coast is wet or windy, replace the walk with a short town-centre loop and an earlier meal. Do not move an exposed walk into poor conditions just because the clock allows it.

## Plan two: a water-edge pause

**Use it when:** energy is low, the sky is doing something worth watching, and nobody needs a full activity.

Pick one bay-side foreshore and stay there. Bring the layer you will want after the sun drops, carry out what you bring in, and treat the water as a view unless current conditions and your ability make entering it clearly safe.

Safety Beach and the Mornington foreshore work because the plan can be as short as it needs to be. A bench, a short walk and twenty unhurried minutes by the water are enough.

**Weather alternative:** keep the pause but move it under cover. A sheltered window seat or takeaway collected before the weather arrives preserves the shape of the evening without pretending the conditions are better than they are.

## Plan three: a table-led finish

**Use it when:** the group needs food more than exercise, or the weather has removed the coast from the decision.

Choose the table first, verify the venue's current service hours, then add only the walk that fits around the booking. Mornington, Dromana and Sorrento all allow a short waterfront segment without turning dinner into a cross-Peninsula transfer.

This order matters. A walk followed by a hopeful search for an open kitchen is not a plan. A confirmed table with an optional twenty-minute loop is.

**Weather alternative:** remove the walk and arrive early. The evening still works.

## What not to do after five

- Do not start a remote or unfamiliar track on the strength of an old duration estimate.
- Do not make an exposed ocean-coast route the only plan when strong wind or rain is possible.
- Do not assume a venue is serving because its dining room appears in an article.
- Do not add a second town because the first plan felt too simple.
- Do not enter the water to rescue an otherwise quiet evening.

## The first long evening

The clocks change on Sunday 4 October. Use the extra light once before trying to optimise it: one short route, one current check, one weather alternative and one easy finish.

That is enough for the first long evening.
