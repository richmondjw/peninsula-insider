## EAT - Rare Hare at Willow Creek, Merricks North

The cured kingfish at Rare Hare has been good all year. In spring it gets better company: the Pinot Row terrace is at the point where afternoon light stays long enough to justify a second glass of Willow Creek Chardonnay.

This is Jackalope's relaxed lunch room, which means the produce and wine pedigree without the occasion pressure. The wood-fired grill drives the main course list - order whatever has come off the farm that week, and ask the floor team what's drinking well on the estate by-the-glass list. They know the current vintage better than any list description will. The room is narrow and unhurried; the terrace is where you want to be on a warm October afternoon.

Bookings are recommended for weekend sittings. Check rareharewinery.com.au for the current menu and available times. Allow two to three hours. Willow Creek Vineyard, 166 Balnarring Road, Merricks North.

Pair it with a walk through the Pt. Leo Estate Sculpture Park before you eat - it is ten minutes down Merricks Beach Road.

## EXPERIENCE - Arthurs Seat State Park, Dromana

*Wait - per rotation rules, Dromana is an avoided locality this week. Substituting: Greens Bush in Merricks.*

The Greens Bush trail network in Merricks is at its spring best right now. The stringybark forest floor carries native orchids through October and into November, and the canopy is thin enough that morning light reaches the understorey.

The Greens Bush circuit from the Tucks Road carpark runs roughly five kilometres through the largest remnant bushland on the Peninsula. Native helmet orchids and spider orchids peak through late October. Take the inner loop first, where the track follows a seasonal creek that is still running from the August rains. Carry water - there are no facilities on track. The whole circuit takes ninety minutes at a relaxed pace, longer if you stop for orchids, which you will.

Park at the Tucks Road carpark, Merricks. No booking required. Trail maps at parks.vic.gov.au. Start before 10am to get the best light through the canopy.

For the afternoon, Rare Hare is fifteen minutes north on Balnarring Road.

## DISCOVERY - Emu Plains Market, Balnarring

Emu Plains Market runs every Saturday at Balnarring Reserve through 19 December 2026, 9am to 2pm. It is the Peninsula's most low-key weekly market, which is exactly why it is useful.

The stall mix runs to locally grown cut flowers, seasonal vegetables, eggs, honey, and small-batch preserves. In late October the flower stalls shift from winter foliage to spring blooms - dahlias, ranunculus, and sweet peas are usually there by the last Saturday of the month. The reserve site is flat and shaded, easy with young children or dogs. It does not take two hours; forty-five minutes is enough to do a full circuit, buy cut flowers and a bag of new-season greens, and be back in the car before the crowd builds.

No booking required. Emu Plains Reserve, Balnarring. Gates open 9am. See emuplainsmarket.com.au for current stallholder list.

Pair it with the Greens Bush trail - the trailhead is twelve minutes south on Merricks Road.
