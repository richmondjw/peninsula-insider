The Wine Festival and Stonier are in the weekend dispatch. This edition is for what surrounds them.

King's Birthday is the Peninsula's biggest winter weekend. It earns it. But the long weekend is four days, and most of the editorial coverage stops at Saturday afternoon. These picks are for the rest of it: the music running four days across Mornington, the truffle hunt to book before spots go, the midweek recovery plan when everything is quiet again, and the next date on the winter calendar worth marking.

Rain is forecast across the weekend. That is not a caveat. Everything here works in 12-degree weather with a hat on.

## The festival running all four days

**Mornington Winter Music Festival. Fri 5 – Mon 8 June, Main Street Mornington and 20-plus venues. Free to $25 per gig.**

Twelve years running. Twenty-plus venues across Mornington town, a three-day sweep of jazz, blues, soul, and R&B that fills the pubs, halls, and street-level rooms of Main Street from Friday evening to Monday close. The street entertainment is free and mostly walk-in. The gig tickets top out at $25.

The dispatch covers the Showgrounds on Saturday. The Music Festival is what you do on Sunday, or after the wine fair, or on Monday when the Peninsula quiets and you find yourself wanting somewhere to be at 7pm that costs almost nothing.

The full schedule is at morningtonmusicfestival.com.au. Pick the room that suits; most don't require a booking.

## The truffle hunt to book while spots exist

**Red Hill Truffles. 1180 Mornington-Flinders Road, Main Ridge. From $189 per person. Season open.**

Red Hill Truffles opened its Perigord black truffle season on 30 May. The hunts are guided, with dogs working the rows and the earthy shock of a fresh truffle coming up from the ground the whole point of the morning. The standard format ends with a meal at Red Hill Estate Restaurant or pizza on the farm. Multiple sessions run daily through August.

Early season is the right time to book. The truffle yield is high, the crowds haven't settled into a rhythm yet, and the King's Birthday weekend slots will be the first to go. If the long weekend itself is already committed, book for the one after: a Saturday at Red Hill Truffles followed by a cellar door on the ridge is the Peninsula's most complete winter half-day.

The peninsula-tip alternative: Flinders Truffles, at 1900 Mornington-Flinders Road, runs farmgate days without booking through winter. If you're driving south anyway, it is worth stopping.

## The recovery plan mid-week

**Peninsula Hot Springs, Bathe-in Cinema. Thursdays to 31 July, Fingal (near Rye). Bathing session required.**

The long weekend clears by Monday evening. Tuesday and Wednesday are yours. Thursday, the amphitheatre at Peninsula Hot Springs hosts a film, and the thermal pools are quieter than they will be any Friday or Saturday until July.

The format is simple: book a bathing session, arrive at dusk, move between the pools as the temperature drops, and at some point find yourself watching a film in warm water with cold air around you. The film lineup posts weekly. Bathing sessions book through the Peninsula Hot Springs website; confirm the night before what's screening.

This works in June in a way it doesn't in warmer months. The cold outside is part of the point.

## The next date to mark on the winter calendar

**Sorrento Solstice Festival. 20–21 June, Sorrento Foreshore. Free.**

King's Birthday is the first winter festival on the Peninsula. Solstice is the second, two weekends later.

Sorrento Solstice runs on the foreshore at Ocean Beach Road: music stages, fire performers, a 6-metre effigy called Bernie that burns on the bay at 6:30pm Saturday, lanterns, food trucks, and a sunrise swim on Sunday. Everything is free; free Humanitix ticketing is recommended. It runs 2pm to 9pm Saturday, with the burn at dusk.

Nothing requires booking now. But the calendar is the point: winter on the Peninsula has a shape this year, and Solstice is the next mark on it after this weekend.
