**EAT - Polperro Winery, Red Hill South**

Polperro sits on the northern slope of the Red Hill escarpment, and on a clear winter Thursday the views across Port Phillip Bay are exactly the reason the Mornington Peninsula has a winter reputation at all. The kitchen runs a seasonal menu that changes fortnightly through the colder months, drawing from Peninsula growers and making no apology for the kind of food that belongs with a glass of the estate Pinot Gris - slow braises, aged cheeses, and the occasional produce-led dish that arrives looking simpler than it is.

The cellar door experience at Polperro rewards a longer visit than most. The winemaking style here is restrained and site-driven, with the Pinot Noir built for aging rather than immediate approval. If you are serious about the wines, ask for a vertical tasting - the team will oblige on quieter weekdays.

Polperro Winery, 150 Red Hill Road, Red Hill South. Open Thursday through Sunday. Book lunch ahead; the dining room is small and winter sittings are popular. polperro.com.au.

**EXPERIENCE - Greens Bush Walk, Merricks North**

The Greens Bush section of Mornington Peninsula National Park is the closest thing the Peninsula has to old-growth bushland, and winter is when it is most compelling. The understorey drips after rain, the wallabies are visible at track edges in the morning, and the canopy thins just enough to let the light through in horizontal bars. The shorter loops from Baldry Crossing run two to three kilometres and take about an hour at a moderate pace. The longer circuits extend to six kilometres and get properly quiet once you are past the first junction - no vehicle noise, no café sounds, just the creek and the birds.

Go early on a Thursday morning when the school groups are not yet out and the light is still raking across the fern gullies. Wear waterproof shoes; the track is well-maintained but the puddles after rain are genuine. Dogs are not permitted.

Greens Bush, accessed via Baldry Crossing on Browns Road, Merricks North. No booking required. Free entry. Allow 1–2 hours depending on route.

**DISCOVERY - Living Sea, MPRG**

Living Sea closes this Sunday at Mornington Peninsula Regional Gallery, which makes this the last working-week opportunity to see it. The exhibition brings together photographs, video works, and scientific imagery from the bay and the strait, presented at a scale that earns the label immersive without the usual overreach. The visual centrepiece is a large-format photographic sequence from the Port Phillip Bay seagrass beds taken over twelve months - the kind of systematic image-making that accumulates meaning the longer you stay with it.

There is a companion program of short films in the education wing running Thursday to Sunday afternoons. Both are free. If you are coming from the north, the gallery is a twenty-minute drive from the freeway and makes sense as a first stop before lunch in Mornington or a drive south.

Mornington Peninsula Regional Gallery, Civic Reserve, Dunns Road, Mornington. Open 11am–4pm Wednesday to Sunday. Free entry. Living Sea closes Sunday 24 August.
