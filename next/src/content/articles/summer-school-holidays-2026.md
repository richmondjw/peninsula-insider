Six weeks is a long time on the Peninsula with children, and the best summer holidays here are not the busiest ones. They are the ones with a rhythm: water in the morning, something to do before lunch, a slow afternoon, and an evening on the sand.

This is the plan for the 2026-27 break. Victorian government school Term 4 ends on **Friday 18 December 2026**, and students return on **Thursday 28 January 2027**. The Shire's peak foreshore camping season runs over almost exactly the same window, from 19 December to 30 January, so expect every bay-side town between Dromana and Sorrento to be at capacity for most of it.

## The beaches that work with kids

Stay on the bay. Port Phillip's beaches are calm, shallow and warm by January, and they are where Peninsula families actually spend the summer.

- **Safety Beach.** The name is accurate. Long, shallow, gentle water and a foreshore that runs for kilometres. The first choice with toddlers and nervous swimmers.
- **Mount Martha and Mornington.** The bathing-box beaches. Mills Beach in Mornington is good for a paddleboard on a still morning, and the town is right there for ice cream and toilets.
- **Rosebud and Rye foreshores.** Wide, flat and busy, with the camping grounds behind them. Good for a whole day if you arrive early.
- **Portsea front beach.** Clear water and a pier to walk, on the bay side of the very tip.

The ocean back beaches at Gunnamatta, Rye, Sorrento and Portsea are for walking and looking with children, not for swimming, unless the beach is patrolled and you are between the red and yellow flags. Our [beach swimming guide](/journal/the-peninsula-beach-swimming-guide/) has the detail.

## Outings worth the drive

Pick one a day, do it in the morning, and you will have a good holiday.

**Strawberry picking at Sunny Ridge.** The Peninsula's classic summer outing. Pick-your-own runs through the warm months, roughly November to April; go early before the heat, and check picking conditions on the day.

**Ashcombe Maze.** The hedge maze and gardens in Shoreham are low-tempo in the best way. Children run the maze; adults sit with a tea. A good choice for a cooler or overcast day.

**Point Nepean National Park.** The fort tunnels, the quarantine station and the views across the heads are a genuine adventure for older children. Ride the main path or use the Parks Victoria shuttle, which adds services in peak periods such as Christmas and the summer school holidays. Leave the car at the entrance and treat it as a half day; our [Point Nepean half-day plan](/explore/plans/the-point-nepean-half-day/) sets it out.

**Arthurs Seat.** The gondola over the treetops is a reliable hit with younger children. Our [Arthurs Seat Eagle guide](/journal/arthurs-seat-eagle-visitor-guide/) covers tickets and timing.

**Under the water.** For confident older swimmers, snorkelling trips out of Sorrento run in summer to the seals and sponge gardens near the heads. Boats sell out in January, so book days ahead and check age and swimming requirements with the operator.

## Lunch that keeps everyone happy

Families do best at places with space to move and a menu that works for every age.

- **Red Gum BBQ, Red Hill South.** Big room, generous plates and table service. It opens for lunch Tuesday to Saturday, with dinner Thursday to Saturday; book ahead in the holidays.
- **La Baracca at T'Gallant, Main Ridge.** Wood-fired pizza, shaded outdoor tables and room for children to run. The Peninsula's most reliable easy group lunch.
- **Main Ridge Dairy.** A working goat dairy with farm cheeses and goat-milk gelato. Children understand the whole operation in about thirty seconds.
- **The Rye Hotel.** A sprawling foreshore pub near the front beach, with a deck and a menu children recognise.

For more, see [casual lunch on the Peninsula](/journal/best-casual-lunch-mornington-peninsula/).

## The timing that saves the holiday

**Beat the morning rush.** Bay car parks fill by mid-morning on warm days. Arrive by nine and the day is easy; arrive at eleven and it is a negotiation.

**Avoid Point Nepean Road at peak times.** The coast road through Rosebud and Rye is slow on any hot day in January. Base yourself near the beach you will use most and walk or cycle to it.

**Use the late afternoon.** Summer sunsets land around 8.30pm, and the bay beaches empty after five while the water is still warm. A dinner picnic on the sand is the cheapest good night on the Peninsula.

**Plan around the heat.** On hot days, do the outdoor outing first, then retreat indoors or into the shade from late morning. Pack hats, water and more sunscreen than seems sensible.

**Check fire danger.** On high fire danger days, skip remote bush walks, check the VicEmergency app and the day's rating, and choose the beach.

## When it rains

Summer on the Peninsula still produces wet days. The [indoor play guide](/journal/indoor-play-mornington-peninsula/) lists the wet-weather options, and the Mornington Peninsula Regional Gallery and Ashcombe Maze's café are both good for a grey morning. A long lunch at La Baracca under cover is a fine way to wait out a shower.

## The short version

Bay beaches, early starts, one outing a day and a long evening on the sand. Book the things that sell out, a snorkel boat and a weekend lunch in particular, and leave the rest of the summer open.
