**EAT - Rye Hotel, Rye**

The Rye Hotel's enormous deck is the argument for spring on the back-beach side of the Peninsula. When the nor'westerlies ease mid-morning, the foreshore tables get full afternoon sun and stay that way until close. The kitchen runs pub classics alongside a specials board that moves with the season - ask what's current when you arrive rather than assuming the menu you saw online is today's.

Order something simple and cold to drink. The deck absorbs families and long-weekend arrivals without anyone noticing the place has filled up, which is its particular skill. Settle in for the afternoon rather than treating it as a quick lunch stop; the scale of the place rewards that approach.

Point Nepean Road, Rye. No booking required for the deck; check the website for kitchen hours if you're arriving after 2pm on a weekday. Allow two hours minimum.

Pair it with a run to St Andrews Beach Brewery on the way home - both are on the back-beach side.

**EXPERIENCE - Cape Schanck Heathland Boardwalk**

The boardwalk loop at Cape Schanck is doing exactly what it should be doing in early November: the coastal heathland either side of the timber walkway is in full spring flower. Honey-scented guinea flowers and pink swamp heath are peaking now, concentrated most densely in the section between the carpark and the first lighthouse platform.

The boardwalk itself protects the vegetation, so you walk through it rather than around it - the density of colour at eye and knee level is the point. Go before 10am to avoid the school-holiday surge, or after 4pm when the light is low and direct onto the cliff face. The walk to the lighthouse lookout and back takes around 45 minutes at an unhurried pace.

Cape Schanck Road off Boneo Road, Cape Schanck. Parks Victoria entry fee applies at the gate. Wear shoes with grip - the boardwalk can be slick after overnight dew.

Pair it with coffee at Flinders on the drive back north.

**DISCOVERY - Elan Vineyard & Gallery, Balnarring**

Elan Vineyard & Gallery on Turners Road, Balnarring, runs a working gallery alongside its cellar door - not as a side project, but as a proper program of changing shows. The current exhibition is on now; call ahead or check the website for the specific title and closing date before making the trip, as the showing schedule rotates and it would be a shame to arrive between exhibitions.

The combination of tasting and looking is Elan's particular quality. You can pour a glass at the cellar door and carry it through to the gallery without any awkwardness about it. The vineyard's reds have always suited this part of the Peninsula - the Balnarring soils give the shiraz in particular a savoury quality that most visitors don't expect from a Mornington wine.

Turners Road, Balnarring. Open Thursday to Sunday; confirm hours before visiting. No booking required for cellar door; call ahead for the gallery program.

Pair it with the Emu Plains Market at Balnarring Reserve on a Saturday morning - both are within five minutes of each other.
