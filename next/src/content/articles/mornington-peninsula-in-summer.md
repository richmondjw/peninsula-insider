Summer on the Mornington Peninsula is two different places sharing one coastline.

There is the summer most people experience: Point Nepean Road at a crawl through Rosebud, every car park full by ten, a forty-minute wait for fish and chips, and a drive home in the dark wondering why it felt like work. And there is the summer locals keep for themselves: a swim before breakfast, the middle of the day spent somewhere shaded and inland, and an evening on the bay that runs until nearly nine.

The difference is not luck. It is timing, direction and one or two bookings made in advance. This is how to have the second summer.

## The shape of the season

The Peninsula's summer has a clear peak, and planning around it changes everything.

**December, before Christmas.** The best-kept part of the season. The water is warming, the evenings are long, and the holiday crowds have not arrived. If you can choose any summer weekend, choose one in early to mid December.

**Christmas to the end of January.** Peak season. Victorian government schools finish on 18 December 2026 and return in late January 2027, and the Shire's peak foreshore camping season runs from 19 December 2026 to 30 January 2027. The bay-side foreshore campgrounds at McCrae, Rosebud, Rye and Sorrento fill, the towns between Dromana and Sorrento swell, and weekends become a test of patience. It is still wonderful. It just needs a plan.

**February.** The secret month. Schools are back, the bay is at its warmest, the evenings are still long and the roads empty out. February weekdays are as close to the perfect Peninsula summer as you will find.

## Rule one: swim the bay, respect the ocean

The Peninsula has two coasts and they behave very differently.

The **bay side**, Port Phillip, is where summer swimming happens. The beaches from Mornington and Mount Martha down through Safety Beach, Dromana, Rosebud, Rye, Sorrento and Portsea front beach are generally calm, shallow and family-friendly. The [bathing boxes at Mount Martha and Mornington](/journal/mornington-peninsula-beach-guide/) are the postcard, but the long flat foreshores at Safety Beach and Rosebud are where families actually spend the day.

The **ocean side**, Bass Strait, is the dramatic one: Gunnamatta, Rye and Sorrento back beaches, Portsea back beach. These are beautiful to walk and dangerous to swim. Surf, rips and rock shelves catch visitors every summer. Swim on the ocean coast only at a patrolled beach, between the red and yellow flags, and check the day's patrol times before you go. If the flags are not up, the beach is for walking.

For calm water on the back beaches, the rock pools and ocean baths at Sorrento are the exception that suits children at low tide. Our [beach swimming guide](/journal/the-peninsula-beach-swimming-guide/) covers each beach in detail.

## Rule two: move early, rest in the middle, go out again late

The single most useful habit on a Peninsula summer day is to do your driving and your beach before about eleven.

Early mornings are cool, the car parks have space, and the bay is often glassy. By midday the heat, the glare and the traffic all peak together. That is the time to be inland and in the shade: a cellar door lunch on the Red Hill ridge, a long table under a pergola, or simply back at your accommodation.

Then go out again. Summer sunsets on the Peninsula land around 8.30pm to 8.45pm through late December and January. From five o'clock the bay beaches empty, the water is still warm, and the light turns gold. A late swim followed by a sunset drink is the best two hours of a summer day, and almost nobody plans for it. [The Sunset Hour](/journal/the-sunset-hour/) has where to be.

## Rule three: know the roads

Point Nepean Road between Dromana and Sorrento is the Peninsula's summer bottleneck. On peak weekends, and on any warm day in January, it can take far longer than the map suggests to cover a short distance through Rosebud, Rye and Blairgowrie.

Three ways to beat it:

- **Base yourself where you want to be.** If your summer is about Sorrento and Portsea, stay there and walk. If it is about wineries and lunches, stay on the ridge or around Red Hill and Main Ridge. The worst summer plan is a daily commute along the bay.
- **Use the inland roads.** Moving between the bay and Western Port, or up to the hinterland, is often faster through Main Ridge and Red Hill than along the coast.
- **Leave the car at Point Nepean.** The Parks Victoria shuttle runs between the park entrance, the Quarantine Station and Fort Nepean, with extra services in peak periods such as Christmas and the summer school holidays. Check the current timetable before you go.

## Rule four: book the one thing that matters

You cannot book a summer day on the Peninsula end to end, and you should not try. But you should book the one thing you would be disappointed to miss.

That is usually a meal. Weekend lunches at the winery restaurants and sunset tables on the bay go early in summer. Some venues change their policy in peak season: our records show the [Portsea Hotel](/journal/waterfront-restaurants-mornington-peninsula/) takes walk-ins only from 23 December to 31 January. Book your anchor meal before you arrive, then leave everything around it loose.

For relaxed eating that does not need a booking, see our guide to [where to eat without a booking](/journal/where-to-eat-without-a-booking/), and for the middle of the day, [casual lunch on the Peninsula](/journal/best-casual-lunch-mornington-peninsula/).

## A summer day, done properly

A shape that works for most visitors on a hot day:

- **7.30am:** Swim at Mount Martha, Safety Beach or Portsea front beach while the bay is calm and the car parks are empty.
- **9am:** Breakfast or coffee in town. In Flinders, the [Flinders General Store](/explore/places/flinders/) is the village's morning stop.
- **11am:** Head inland before the heat. A walk in the shade, a cellar door, or a farm gate on the ridge.
- **1pm:** A long, shaded lunch somewhere you booked. Stay longer than you planned.
- **3pm:** Rest. Seriously. This is the hour that ruins a summer day when people try to fill it.
- **6pm:** Back to the bay for a late swim while the water is still warm.
- **8pm:** A sunset drink facing west, then dinner, ideally somewhere you can reach without driving.

## Crossing the bay

The Searoad ferry between Sorrento and Queenscliff runs every day and is one of the Peninsula's great summer outings: a crossing of the bay's entrance with the heads in view, and a day on the Bellarine at the other end. Services run through daylight hours, with more sailings around the holiday season. Check the timetable and, in peak season, book vehicle space well ahead. Walking on is the easier summer option.

## Hot days and fire days

Summer on the Peninsula includes genuinely hot days and, on some of them, high fire danger. On those days, choose the bay and the air-conditioned lunch over a bush walk on the ridge, carry water, and check the VicEmergency app and the day's fire danger rating before you head into the national parks. Plans built around a beach and a long lunch adapt easily; plans built around a remote walk should be changed.

## If you only remember one thing

Summer here rewards the people who start early and finish late. Swim before the crowds, shelter through the middle of the day, and give the long evening the attention it deserves. That is the Peninsula summer worth driving for.
