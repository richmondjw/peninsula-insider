**EAT - Somers General, Somers**

Somers General runs a weekend brunch menu that punches well above what a village this size should produce. The store stocks a thoughtful Peninsula wine shelf alongside pantry goods you'll actually use, but the reason to come this weekend is the food. Spring morning light through those front windows is worth the drive alone.

The brunch menu changes, so there's no point naming a dish that may have rotated off - but the kitchen leans seasonal and the coffee is consistently good. The pantry section makes an easy second act: olive oils, local preserves, and wines from producers you won't find at a bottleshop. The crowd is locals on Sunday morning, not day-trippers, and the pace reflects that.

Allow 45 minutes minimum. Somers Village, Somers VIC 3927. No bookings for brunch walk-ins - arrive by 10am on Sunday to avoid the wait.

Pair with a drive to Balnarring Reserve afterward if the stone fruit on the roadside stalls is running.

---

**WALK - Balcombe Creek Estuary, Safety Beach to Mornington**

The Balcombe Creek estuary earns its best conditions right now. Spring high tides push wading birds - spoonbills, herons, the occasional pied oystercatcher - right to the edge of the trail, close enough to watch without binoculars.

The walk runs from the reserve car park near Safety Beach toward the Mornington harbour end. The vegetation changes noticeably in spring: the she-oaks along the lower stretch are releasing pollen, the saltmarsh fringe is at its greenest, and the light off Western Port at mid-morning is flat and clear. This is not a dramatic headland walk. It is a quiet estuary walk, and the difference matters. You come here for birds and tidal light, not views.

Wear shoes that can handle soft ground near the water's edge. Allow 45–60 minutes one way, or turn back whenever. No facilities at the trailhead. Start early on weekends before the shared path gets cyclist traffic.

Pair with a coffee at Somers General on the way back through.

**DISCOVERY - Acoustic Saturdays at the Spike Bar, Peppers Moonah Links, Fingal**

This one genuinely flies under the radar. Acoustic Saturdays at the Spike Bar runs every Saturday from 5pm to 8pm, and the Fingal address keeps the tourist traffic thin. The deck at Moonah Links faces west over the golf course and the bay beyond it, which in November means you're watching the last of the light while a local act plays three metres away.

The format is straightforward: live acoustic sets, the full bar, no ticketing fuss. It has been running since June and continues through late December, which means this is one of the last genuinely low-key weeks before the Peninsula's summer crowd discovers it. By mid-December the deck fills. Right now it doesn't. That gap is the reason to go this Saturday specifically.

Doors from 5pm, Spike Bar at Peppers Moonah Links, Peter Thomson Drive, Fingal VIC 3939. Check the Moonah Links website for the current artist. No bookings required for the bar; book ahead if you want dinner at the restaurant.

Pair with a walk along the Fingal coastal track before the set starts - the light on Bass Strait from that stretch of coast at 4:30pm in spring is worth the extra thirty minutes.
