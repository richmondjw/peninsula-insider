**EAT - Flinders Sourdough, Flinders**

The Beach Bun is not a marketing concept. It is a brioche-adjacent, wood-fired bun that justifies arriving at Cook Street before 10am, because they are gone by then. Flinders Sourdough bakes in a small shopfront off Flinders village's main strip, using a wood-fired oven that has been running long enough to earn an opinion. In spring the loaves are lighter - less the dense winter crumb, more the open structure that works with good butter and new-season tomatoes from a farm stall two doors down. The pastry cabinet fills up fast and clears out faster. If the loaves are sold, the coffee from Little Rebel is worth staying for anyway. Take a sourdough loaf if one remains; it travels well and improves through the afternoon.

Flinders Sourdough, 37 Cook Street, Flinders. Weekends from 9am. No booking. Pair the loaf with something from Flinders General Store next door for a full Flinders morning.

**EXPERIENCE - Serafino Track, Arthurs Seat State Park**

The Serafino Track on the upper slope of Arthurs Seat runs through a corridor of native bush that is mid-spring right now: wattles finished, bush peas and guinea flowers well into flower, grass trees with spikes up. The track is approximately 3km of loop walking, mostly through open ironbark and stringybark canopy, with one section of exposed ridge that looks west across Port Phillip in the kind of November afternoon light that makes the bay read silver. The elevation here is modest - 305 metres - but it separates the experience from anything on the flat. Walk it late morning before the easterly builds. Footwear matters; the trail surface is loose shale on the descent. Parks Victoria has the current track map online and flags any closures after spring burns.

Arthurs Seat State Park, Arthurs Seat Road, Arthurs Seat. Allow 1.5 hours. No booking. Pair with a stop at Flinders Sourdough on the way back through.

**DISCOVERY - National Works on Paper 2026, MPRG, closes Sunday 22 November**

The National Works on Paper exhibition closes this Sunday at 4pm, and that is a hard deadline. MPRG's annual survey of Australian works on paper has run since 1986; this iteration features around 50 works selected from a national field, with a spread from graphite-on-rag to screen-printed textile paper hybrids. This year's selection skews toward works that earn the paper itself a role - surface texture and absorbency visible in the finished piece, not just substrate. The gallery is free to enter. Allow forty-five minutes; the works are hung with space between them and reward close reading. The 2026 acquisitions are listed on the gallery website if you want to know what the selection committee bought before you go.

Mornington Peninsula Regional Gallery, Civic Reserve, Dunns Road, Mornington. Open Tuesday–Sunday 11am–4pm. Final day Sunday 22 November. Free entry. No booking required. Pair with coffee at a Mornington main-street café before or after.
