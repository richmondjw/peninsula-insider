## EAT & DRINK - Eldridge Estate, Red Hill

Eldridge Estate is a shed, a small counter, and David Lloyd. That is the whole operation, and it is exactly enough.

Spring is when the MV6 Pinot Noir earns its reputation all over again. Lloyd's MV6 - propagated from Burgundy's Dijon clone 6 - is the kind of wine that makes other Peninsula Pinots feel slightly rushed. It reads tighter and darker than you expect from a cool October, with the tannic grip that tells you it needs time in the bottle but rewards you anyway. Tasting it here, in the place where it was made, against Lloyd's other single-clone pours, is a materially different experience from reading about it. The lineup lets you understand what the clone selection actually does.

The cellar door is tiny and the sessions are not formal, but the conversation is. Lloyd talks about his vines the way a farmer talks about weather: plainly and specifically.

Eldridge Estate is on Arthurs Seat Road, Red Hill. Hours are limited; phone ahead before you drive out. Allow ninety minutes minimum.

Pair the visit with lunch at Port Phillip Estate restaurant on the way through.

## EXPERIENCE - Greens Bush Loop, Mornington Peninsula National Park

The Greens Bush section of Mornington Peninsula National Park is one of the most under-walked circuits on the Peninsula, and right now it is at the peak of what it does best.

The 9.5-kilometre loop passes through intact lowland heath and tea-tree scrub. By late October, the heath is dense with pink boronias, yellow guinea flowers, and the papery white of leptospermum in full flush. This is not a manicured wildflower display - it is a functioning coastal scrub ecosystem that happens to be spectacular for about six weeks each spring. The track is well-marked but quiet; on a weekday you may share it only with grey fantails and the occasional echidna working through the leaf litter at the base of the banksias.

The going is flat to gently rolling. Morning light comes in low through the sheoak canopy along the western leg. Start before 9am to get it to yourself.

Access is from the Greens Bush car park off Boneo Road near Rosebud. Allow two to three hours for the full circuit. No booking required. Wear closed shoes - the track edges are scrubby.

Finish at Rosebud for coffee before driving back.

## DISCOVERY - National Works on Paper 2026, Mornington

**National Works on Paper 2026, Mornington Peninsula Regional Gallery. Open now, closes 22 November 2026.**

NWOP is the exhibition Peninsula locals reliably mention and then miss until the final week. Don't do that this year - four weeks remain and the show is worth two visits.

The annual survey brings together work in drawing, printmaking, watercolour, gouache, and collage from artists across the country. It is deliberately medium-specific, which gives the hang a coherence that mixed-media surveys often lose. The 2026 selection includes work that reads as both technically rigorous and genuinely alive on the wall. There is no single dominant style; the curatorial decision to show the range of what paper can do - from dense intaglio to spare graphite - makes for a show where your attention keeps resetting.

MPRG is at Civic Reserve, Dunns Road, Mornington. Open Tuesday to Sunday, 11am–4pm. Entry is free. Allow forty-five minutes, more if you want to sit with the shortlisted works.

Pair with a walk along Mornington Pier afterwards, then coffee at a Main Street cafe before the drive home.
