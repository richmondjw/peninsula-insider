Three days of the same question produced three days of the same answer. Sunday, Monday, and Tuesday each asked what the Peninsula was worth when it was raining, and the answer each time was: pick one indoor thing and make it deliberate.

Wednesday asks something else.

The mainly clear skies at Main Ridge this morning mark the first time since the weekend that the outdoor Peninsula has been genuinely available. No school holiday crowd - that finished last week. No weekend pressure on bookings or roads. The cellar doors with their fires lit will have tables that are actually quiet. The coastal walks that looked uninviting under persistent drizzle are now the version of themselves that mid-winter sometimes produces: cold, still, cleared of the week's visitors.

The forecast through to Sunday carries zero rain probability from Friday onwards, and Thursday holds only a thirty-three percent chance. If this week is a Peninsula week, the remaining days have earned themselves. Wednesday mid-week is often the Peninsula at its most useful - the infrastructure is available, the pace is unhurried, and the argument for being here now rather than waiting for the weekend is simply that you can.

What you do with it is the question. The options are open in a way they were not three days ago.
