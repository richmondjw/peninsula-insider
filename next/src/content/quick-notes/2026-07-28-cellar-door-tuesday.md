The Peninsula's cellar doors do not close for rain. Tuesday is a thunderstorm forecast, one hundred percent probability, six point one millimetres through the day, with a maximum wind speed of forty-one point two kilometres per hour. The cellar door argument for Tuesday is not about comfortable conditions. It is about who is not there.

A working-week visit during a storm sequence is quiet in a way that a wet Saturday afternoon is not. The weekend visitors who book tables at Pt. Leo Estate or Ten Minutes by Tractor are not driving down on a Tuesday in a thunderstorm. The cellar doors of the Red Hill, Main Ridge, and Merricks North precinct are staffed, the wines being poured are the same, and the rooms carry a different character when the tables around you are empty.

The argument is not weather-proof in the comfortable sense. Rain at forty-one kilometres per hour is noticeable between buildings and across car parks. The argument is weather-proof in the sense of being indoors with a tasting flight in a room that is lit by grey storm light coming through vineyard windows, with no competition for the table or the attention of whoever is pouring.

Some of the Peninsula's smaller producers reduce mid-week hours in winter, and the storm context makes confirming opening hours directly with any venue worth doing before the drive. Larger operations including Pt. Leo Estate and Ten Minutes by Tractor operate standard tasting room hours through working days, with bookings recommended.

Wednesday continues the wet sequence at ninety-eight percent. Thursday begins to ease. If Tuesday does not work, the same working-week quiet carries into Thursday, with less rain.
