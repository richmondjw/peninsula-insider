Monday's editorial called the week wet. That remains broadly true — Monday delivered its rain, the forecast showed Tuesday at 71% probability, Wednesday was uncertain. The indoor Peninsula programme was the correct frame.

Tuesday has changed. The rain did not follow Monday across the week; instead the fog arrived. The current morning is cold, still, and dense with it. But fog has a different relationship to a Peninsula day than rain does. It lifts. It usually lifts by 9am to 10am on the ridgeline, and what it leaves behind — on a dry-air autumn morning — is a day that runs clear from mid-morning through the afternoon.

The practical revision: if Tuesday was written off as a wet day based on Monday's forecast, that forecast did not hold. Today runs at 13°C with no rain probability in the current data. The cellar door circuit is not a Tuesday proposition for most producers (the majority run Wednesday through Sunday), but the Hot Springs thermal circuit, the Mornington dining corridor, and the coastal walks are viable from roughly 10am.

What holds from the Monday position: the week is still settling, Wednesday's improvement is early modelling, and Chloe Gill's Showcase at the Springs this Friday 23 May is the week's specific booking reason. That event is four days out and the wet-week momentum tends to concentrate bookings toward end-of-week events. The time to act on that is now, not Friday morning.

The Peninsula's autumn fog is not a weather problem. It is a 90-minute waiting condition.
