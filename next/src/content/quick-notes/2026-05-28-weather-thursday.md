The Bureau's Main Ridge forecast gives Thursday as the better end of a difficult week. A medium chance of showers, 40% probability, becoming less likely through the late afternoon and into the evening. Rainfall is minimal at zero to one millimetre. Maximum of 16 degrees. The westerly that ran at 15 to 20 kilometres per hour eases to light winds in the early morning, which is the calmest part of the day.

The shape of the week matters more than any single day. Thursday at 40% and Friday at 20% are the two workable windows. Both are partly cloudy rather than clear, but both are genuinely usable for outdoor plans, and Friday is the drier of the pair. From Saturday the pattern turns and does not let up: Saturday 80% with showers building through the afternoon and evening, Sunday 95% with 6 to 10 millimetres and northwesterly winds at 20 to 30 kilometres per hour, Monday 1 June carrying 90% and up to 15 millimetres.

For planning on the Peninsula today: the morning is the calmest stretch, light winds before the westerly returns. Anyone with coastal or walking plans should take the first half of the day. The exposed headlands at Cape Schanck and Portsea will carry the westerly edge once it builds. The inland valleys around Red Hill, Main Ridge, and Merricks sit more sheltered.

If outdoor Peninsula business is still unscheduled this week, Thursday and Friday are the days to use it. The weekend belongs to the covered and indoor version.
