The conditions this morning - six point nine degrees, north-north-westerly at six point five kilometres per hour, partly cloudy at half past six and overcast through the day - are not what most visitors associate with a cellar door visit. They are, however, what the Peninsula's wine country looks like at its quieter best.

Mid-winter Saturdays on the Peninsula run differently from the warm-season version. The visitors are here but not in the numbers of December or January. The cellar doors are open. The wines being poured are the same. The overcast lid softens the light in a way specific to the region in July, and the wind at eleven and a half kilometres per hour maximum makes the vineyard setting more workable than any gusty spring afternoon.

The Mornington Peninsula's cellar door precinct is concentrated around Red Hill, Main Ridge, and Merricks North. Most operations open from eleven in the morning through to five in the afternoon on Saturdays; hours should be confirmed directly with individual producers, as smaller estates in particular vary their Saturday opening schedule. Seated tastings at most venues require a booking.

The five-day dry block running from Friday through Tuesday means the vineyard approach roads - some of which are unsealed - will be in good condition across the weekend. Saturday and Sunday hold the same forecast. This is the middle of the window.
