Monday operates differently on the Mornington Peninsula. The weekend visitors have cleared. The car parks on the Red Hill ridge are not full. A cellar door that on Saturday requires a booking two weeks out will today have room for a walk-in. That is the structural truth of Monday, and it applies whether the weather cooperates or not.

This Monday, the weather cooperates completely. Six days of rain ended overnight. The sky above Red Hill is clear at 6°C and climbing toward a 16°C afternoon — full sunshine, 0% rain probability, a light E breeze. The autumn light that arrives after a wet spell on the Peninsula has a particular quality: the ranges hold the green that the rain saturated into them, the air has no haze, and the coastal horizon at Cape Schanck or the ridge views from Main Ridge show the kind of resolution that summer heat suppresses.

Peninsula Hot Springs is open on Mondays. The weekday programme runs hot springs yoga at 9:15am, fire and ice workshop at 10:30am (then 4pm and 5pm), body clay ritual at noon and 2pm, sauna infusion at 5pm. Monday bookings at the Springs are typically more available than the weekend crush — though still worth confirming before the drive.

A practical note on cellar doors: many Peninsula wineries run seven days during autumn, though Monday is the day to call ahead for smaller producers on the Merricks and Balnarring arc. The main Red Hill and Main Ridge cluster is well-served for Monday visitors. The drive down is worth it before the clear window ends — the forecast holds through Wednesday.
