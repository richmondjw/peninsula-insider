Sunday is the day to take the Peninsula on foot. The forecast is dry from the start, with a maximum of fourteen point three degrees and no rainfall expected. The northerly will be noticeable on exposed coastal sections, especially early, but it is a layer-and-go condition rather than a reason to abandon the plan.

The better rhythm is to start after the coldest first hour, keep the exposed part of the route for late morning, and leave enough time for a warm stop afterwards. A dry winter walk has a particular clarity here: the ground is less forgiving than in summer, the air is clean, and the coast is doing more of the work than any itinerary needs to.

There is no need to force the same plan into the early week. Monday has a seventy-three percent rain probability and Tuesday carries the heavier system, with twelve point four millimetres forecast, a ten point six degree maximum, and a thunderstorm signal. Those are days for a different Peninsula.

Today is the gap between systems. Take it while it holds.
