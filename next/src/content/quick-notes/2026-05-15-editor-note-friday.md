There is something particular about a Friday on the Peninsula at 20°C. It is the last day of the week before the visitor volume shifts, the roads fill, and the cellar door queues form. Today that gap opens at precisely the warmest point of the autumn run.

The high-pressure system has now held for five days. This morning the temperature sits at 9°C with clear skies; by 9am it clears to 15°C, by noon to 19°C, and by 3pm it reaches 20°C under full sun. The rain probability is 0% across every hour of the day. Sunset is at 5:18pm, giving just over ten hours of light from a low-angle autumn sun that makes every terrace on the Peninsula look exactly as it should.

Saturday holds at 20°C but cloud builds through the afternoon, and Sunday brings genuine rain — 100% probability in the morning, returning by evening. The cellar door circuit — the majority of producers operating Wednesday through Sunday — has been open since Wednesday. Today is the second full day of the Friday-to-Sunday window, and the quietest of the three.

If a day trip is possible this week, the case for doing it today is straightforward: the temperature and the weather are at their best, and the Peninsula is at its least crowded. The weekend begins tomorrow.
