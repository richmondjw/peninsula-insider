Wednesday delivered what it promised: persistent cloud, patchy rain through the middle hours, the Peninsula largely interior-facing. Thursday carries the tail of that, but something has shifted. The wet sequence — the low that ran Monday through Wednesday — has moved on. What remains is the overcast without the rain.

There is a qualitative difference. Twenty percent shower probability is weather you dress for and mostly escape. It is not 100% rain probability at noon. The BOM forecast issued at 6:39 AM AEST confirms it: Thursday, slight chance of a shower, light winds, 9 to 16 degrees. The standard mid-autumn cold day without its worst element.

The useful frame is what Thursday becomes: a planning day, a positioning day. Friday is mostly sunny. BOM has it at 5% rain, clear conditions, 6 to 16 degrees. That is the week's clear interval, and it corresponds to the Peninsula Hot Springs Showcase with Chloe Gill at 7pm. A dry, cold, clear Friday evening into a warm thermal circuit. That is the Mornington Peninsula autumn at its functional best.

The week, as a shape, makes sense now. A rough start, two wet mid-week days, a transition day, and a clean end. Saturday extends the fine run at 18 degrees; Sunday closes it with afternoon showers.

If there is one thing to do on Thursday: book Friday. The Showcase window is closing — see the note on the Springs if you have not read it. And if you are on the Peninsula today, the same indoor programme that held Wednesday holds Thursday. The overcast is not punishing. It is simply not the day to lead with the outdoors.

The week resolves well. Thursday is the hinge.
