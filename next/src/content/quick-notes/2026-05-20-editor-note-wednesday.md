The wet week that Monday forecast and Tuesday sidestepped has arrived. Wednesday is the Peninsula under cloud, with rain probability climbing through the morning and holding through the evening. It is not a punishing day — amounts are trace, temperature reaches 16°C, there is no storm in it. But it is the kind of day where the outdoor plan does not hold.

The appropriate reframe is not to abandon the Peninsula. It is to change the register. The thermal circuit works in any weather; the protected courtyard at a good cellar door is comfortable at 15°C; the midweek dining window on the Mornington corridor is open and uncrowded. Wednesday is not the day for the Arthurs Seat ridgeline or the Blairgowrie back-beach. It is the day for the inside, the covered, the warm.

The week's resolution is worth holding. Thursday runs the same wet pattern but clears in the evening. Friday arrives clean: the current forecast has a sunny morning, no rain probability across the day, and the weekend ahead opens with the peninsula's characteristic post-cold-snap clarity. Friday evening at the Peninsula Hot Springs — which coincides with the Chloe Gill Showcase at 7pm — will have had a cold, clear day behind it.

The shape of the week: one properly wet day (today), then a gradual clearing. That is not a bad week. It is an autumn week that earns its end.

The correct response to Wednesday on the Peninsula is: get to the indoor programme, book Friday before mid-week inertia kills the plan, and leave the Arthurs Seat walk for the weekend.
