The wind at Main Ridge this morning is north-north-easterly at five point seven kilometres per hour. By the standards of a mid-winter Peninsula morning, this is as still as the coastline gets. The partly cloudy read at half past six gives way to overcast through the day, but the rain probability is zero and the maximum wind speed on the daily forecast is under eleven kilometres per hour.

The Peninsula's coastal tracks read differently in this kind of weather than they do in summer. The overcast lid flattens the light. There is no glare off the water. The school holiday visitors who were on the Peninsula last week have gone back. What you get in exchange is the coast in its actual mid-winter form: cold, calm, and clear of the season's crowd.

Bushrangers Bay at Cape Schanck is the Peninsula's best-known coastal circuit - approximately six kilometres return with cliff-edge sections and a descent to the beach. The Department of Environment manages access and track status should be confirmed before heading out, as cliff erosion closures can apply at short notice. The foreshore paths around Portsea and Sorrento offer an easier alternative when a gentler hour on the coast is the intention.

Morning is the better time on any of the coastal sections: the wind is at its calmest and the quality of light under cloud, even in July, has more character than the flat afternoon. The four-day dry block running through Sunday means the conditions for this kind of morning repeat across the weekend. Today is the first of them.
