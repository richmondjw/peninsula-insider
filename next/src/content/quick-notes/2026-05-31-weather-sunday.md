The first proper Sunday of winter on the Peninsula. Eighty percent chance, showers most likely from the afternoon and through the evening, zero to four millimetres on the model band. Fourteen degrees at the top, eight at the bottom. The wind sits west to northwesterly at twenty-five to forty kilometres per hour, harder than yesterday and louder on the exposed headlands.

The early morning is the calmest stretch. Anyone with a short walk, a market pickup, a coffee on the way to a booking, has a workable window before noon. After that the cloud closes and the front edges of Monday's heavier system begin to show. Cape Schanck and the open coast carry the wind first; Red Hill, Main Ridge, and Merricks sit a little more sheltered.

Monday opens June at one hundred percent with six to fifteen millimetres, the wettest day of the run and the day the new month declares itself in earnest. Tuesday and Wednesday stay near a hundred percent. The week does not lift until later, and even then only into a damp interval rather than anything clear.

The shape from here is the covered Peninsula. Cellar door tasting rooms with the fire on, winery restaurants that hold their warmth across a long lunch, and Peninsula Hot Springs, where weather is genuinely a non-factor. The Sunday Sessions anchor at noon, with Paul Dillon at the Bath House Café, is confirmed and complimentary with bathing.
