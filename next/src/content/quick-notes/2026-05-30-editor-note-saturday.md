The week's read held. The two open windows on Thursday and Friday were the only workable days, and the weekend has arrived exactly as the models said it would. Saturday at ninety percent with showers building into the late afternoon. Sunday at eighty with the west-northwesterly pushing harder. Monday opens June at one hundred percent and as much as fifteen millimetres. The pattern does not lift until later in the new week.

There is nothing surprising in this. It is the first proper weekend of winter on the Peninsula, and it is doing what winter does. What matters editorially is that the Peninsula has a settled answer for it, and the answer is not to press a coastal walk against ninety-five percent rain. The answer is the covered Peninsula.

That means the cellar doors with proper indoor tasting rooms around Red Hill, Main Ridge, and Merricks, where the fire is on and the conversation is unhurried. It means the winery restaurants that hold their warmth, where lunch sits properly inside the afternoon. And it means Peninsula Hot Springs, where weather is genuinely a non-factor, and where Sunday brings Paul Dillon to the Bath House Café at noon, confirmed and complimentary with bathing.

A wet weekend is not a lost one here. It is simply a different kind of Peninsula day, slower, warmer indoors than out, and a reminder that this place has more than one season worth turning up for.
