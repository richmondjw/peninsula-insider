The north-westerly this morning is running at fourteen point four kilometres per hour. That is up from Friday's five point seven and Sunday's six - but it is within the range for a workable coastal walk on the Peninsula. Monday's maximum wind speed is seventeen point five kilometres per hour. Track conditions following five dry days will be firm and clear.

Monday and Tuesday represent the quietest possible window for the Peninsula's coastal sections. The working week has started. The dry window that began Friday is still holding. Two days remain before rain arrives Wednesday.

Bushrangers Bay at Cape Schanck is the Peninsula's best-known coastal circuit - approximately six kilometres return with cliff-edge sections above the basalt coast and a descent to the beach. Track and access conditions should be confirmed with the Department of Environment before heading out, as cliff-erosion closures can apply at short notice. The foreshore paths around Portsea and Sorrento remain accessible at all standard tides and read well under the overcast conditions that have characterised this dry block.

The north-westerly direction means the Cape Schanck headland, which faces south and south-west, will carry some wind exposure at the cliff edge. The Bushrangers Bay descent to beach level provides more shelter. The Portsea and Sorrento foreshore tracks run roughly east-west and are more sheltered from a north-westerly than the southern headlands.

Rain arrives Wednesday. These are the last two mornings of the dry block, and on a Monday and Tuesday the Peninsula's tracks will be close to empty.
