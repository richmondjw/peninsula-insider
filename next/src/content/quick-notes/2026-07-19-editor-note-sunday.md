Saturday's note reported a dry window running from Friday through Tuesday, with Tuesday sitting at two percent rain probability. Sunday's retrieval confirms all three remaining days - Sunday, Monday, and Tuesday - at zero percent. The window has not narrowed. It has held.

Five consecutive dry days is not the standard mid-winter condition on the Peninsula. The sequence began with a wet week - Sunday the twelfth through Tuesday the fourteenth ran rain - and the window that opened behind it has proven more durable than most. Sunday is the last morning of the weekend inside it. Monday and Tuesday follow, and they are clean.

Rain arrives Wednesday at sixty-nine percent probability and two point two millimetres. Thursday follows at seventy-five percent and nine point five millimetres - heavier than Wednesday. The wet week that comes next is sharper than what opened last week. The Peninsula's mid-winter pattern reasserts after the break.

The question for Sunday is not whether the day will hold. It will. The question is whether you are using the last morning of the clear run or saving it for the week ahead.
