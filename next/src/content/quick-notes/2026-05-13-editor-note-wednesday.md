There is a specific version of the Peninsula that only exists Wednesday through Friday: the one where the cellar doors are all open and the weekend visitors have not yet arrived.

Monday and Tuesday close most of the Red Hill and Merricks North producers. Some of the larger operations — Pt Leo Estate, Ten Minutes by Tractor, Yabby Lake, Eldridge Estate — maintain tasting by appointment across the full week, but the walk-in cellar door experience that defines the circuit reopens on Wednesday. This morning it resumes.

The conditions are right for it. The clear autumn window that opened after last weekend's rain has now held for three days. The track from the freeway to Red Hill is dry, the light over the ridge by mid-morning is the particular flat gold that the Peninsula does in April and May, and the temperature peaks at 17°C. One degree cooler than Tuesday, but the same quality of day.

Thursday extends the window and gets warmer — 19°C forecast, still no rain. If a midweek Peninsula day is possible this week, Thursday looks like the peak of it. But today is the first day the full circuit is running. The gallery at MPRG in Mornington has two exhibitions open (New Wave 26 and Michael Vale, free entry, 11am–4pm) and combines cleanly with a Red Hill afternoon.

The weekend will be busier. Wednesday is the version of the Peninsula that has no queue at the cellar door.
