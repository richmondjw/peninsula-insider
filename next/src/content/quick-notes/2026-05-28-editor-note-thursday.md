The week has a clear shape now, and it is worth reading before making plans. Thursday and Friday are the two open windows. Thursday sits at 40% and eases through the day. Friday is the drier of the two at 20% with light winds. Neither is a guaranteed clear sky, but both are materially workable, and they are the only days this week that are.

From Saturday the pattern turns without relief. Saturday carries 80% with showers building through the afternoon. Sunday reaches 95% with 6 to 10 millimetres and a northwesterly at 20 to 30 kilometres per hour. Monday 1 June holds 90% and as much as 15 millimetres. The Peninsula moves into its proper winter rhythm, and it stays there.

The practical read is simple. If there is anything outdoor still to do on the Peninsula this week, a coastal walk, a cellar door visit with garden time, a morning at the market, the next two days are the window. After that the plan should change rather than press on, because pressing on against 95% is how a good day becomes a wet one.

The covered Peninsula carries the weekend. Cellar doors with indoor tasting rooms around Red Hill and Merricks. Winery restaurants that hold their warmth. And Peninsula Hot Springs, where weather is a non-factor and Sunday brings Paul Dillon for Sunday Sessions at noon. A wet weekend is not a lost one here. It is simply a different kind of Peninsula day, and this is the season for it.
