Friday's note opened with a four-day dry block through Monday. The Saturday morning retrieval has revised that upward: Tuesday now reads at two percent rain probability and zero millimetres expected. The window has added a day.

For a mid-winter Saturday on the Peninsula, the extension matters because the question shifts from the weekend to the week ahead. Saturday and Sunday are confirmed. Monday is confirmed. Tuesday is effectively dry. Wednesday arrives at seventy-three percent probability and two point eight millimetres. Thursday follows at forty-one percent and eight point nine millimetres. The dry window is as clean and as confirmed as any five-day forecast this winter.

The wet spell that opened last week - Sunday through Tuesday ran wet - has earned the space that followed it. Five consecutive dry days in mid-July is not the usual condition. The Peninsula tends to deliver its good winter weather in shorter runs. This one is longer.

Saturday is the first morning of the weekend inside it. The question this week is not whether the conditions will hold. They will. The question is what to do with them.
