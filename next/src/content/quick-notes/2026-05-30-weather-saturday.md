The week's two dry windows have closed. Saturday arrives at 90% with showers most likely from the late afternoon and through the evening, one to five millimetres on the model median of three, sixteen degrees at the top. The northwesterly runs at twenty to thirty kilometres per hour and turns westerly at twenty-five to thirty-five through the late evening.

The morning is the calmest stretch. The showers build later rather than land at sunrise, so anyone with a short outdoor errand, a quick walk, a market run before lunch, has a workable window before the weather settles in. The exposed headlands at Cape Schanck and Portsea will carry the wind edge once it builds; Red Hill, Main Ridge, and Merricks sit a little more sheltered.

Sunday holds 80% with two millimetres on the median and the west-northwesterly pushing harder at twenty-five to forty kilometres per hour. Monday opens June at 100% with six to fifteen millimetres, the wettest day of the run. Tuesday and Wednesday stay near a hundred percent. The Peninsula is now properly in its winter rhythm, and it is not lifting until later in the week.

The plan from here is the covered Peninsula. Cellar doors with indoor tasting rooms, winery restaurants that hold their warmth, and Peninsula Hot Springs, where weather is a non-factor. Sunday's Paul Dillon session at the Bath House Café is the weekend anchor, confirmed for noon.
