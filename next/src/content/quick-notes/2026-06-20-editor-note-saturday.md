The winter solstice falls on Sunday 21 June. The festival falls on Saturday. The gap between the two is intentional: the longest night is the warm-up; the actual day of turning is the quiet that follows.

The forecast shifted this week. Seven days ago, Saturday looked like twenty percent chance of rain and a dry foreshore. This morning it reads eighty-three percent, seven millimetres, showers from midday. That is a meaningful revision and worth naming plainly.

The editorial position does not change. The Sorrento Solstice Festival is a fire and lantern event. Its atmosphere is better in low weather than in sunshine. The effigy burn at 6:30pm on the bay is a spectacle that the rain does not diminish. The food trucks are running. The music stages are undercover. The family program is adapted. The village is lit.

If you have tickets, the calculus is straightforward: waterproofs and layers. If you are on the waitlist, it is worth checking Humanitix this morning, as registered holders with wet-weather hesitation sometimes release before the afternoon.

The planning note for the weekend: arrive for the 2pm opening, find your footing in the village, eat early, and be on the foreshore by 6pm. The burn is the hinge of the night and it does not wait.

Sunday is the resolution. Lighter weather, quieter Peninsula, and Peninsula Hot Springs Sunday Sessions from noon if you need the debrief.
