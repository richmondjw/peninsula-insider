The week has a shape worth understanding before committing to plans.

Tuesday is the clearest midweek day on the forecast - 50% shower probability, weighted to evening, with a maximum of 18 degrees under cloud. Easterly winds. The day holds. Wednesday opens with a 50% morning shower risk. Thursday improves to 20%, the second viable day before the pattern deteriorates.

From Saturday, the Peninsula enters a proper wet stretch. Saturday at 80%, Sunday at 90% - with one to eight millimetres possible and westerly winds building. Monday 1 June repeats the 90% pattern, carrying 1 to 8mm and stronger northwesterlies. The first week of June is not the outdoor Peninsula.

What this means for planning: if there is a Peninsula trip with outdoor priority this week, Tuesday is the first choice, Thursday the second. If the trip falls across the weekend, the programme should be built around warmth and shelter rather than landscape exposure. Peninsula Hot Springs' covered and indoor circuit - the Bath House, the Spa Dreaming Centre, clay rituals, fire and ice - is exactly the right frame for that kind of autumn weekend. The Springs were designed for this weather, not despite it.

The autumn Peninsula has its own rhythm. The dry, warm days belong to March. May is an interior month on the Peninsula - morning pools, firelit dining rooms, unhurried cellar doors with no crowd pressure. That version of the Peninsula is available right through the week, whatever the forecast. It just takes a shift in intent.
