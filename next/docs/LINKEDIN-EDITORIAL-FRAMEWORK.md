# Peninsula Insider — LinkedIn Editorial Framework

**Author:** Remy  
**Status:** For James + Emma review

## Strategic Position

LinkedIn is not a distribution channel for Peninsula Insider content.

It is the place where the *thinking behind* Peninsula Insider becomes visible.

The distinction matters. A distribution channel optimises for reach. A thinking channel optimises for trust and authority. Peninsula Insider on LinkedIn should make someone who has never visited the site feel like they have encountered something editorially serious — and want to go deeper.

The through-line: Peninsula Insider exists because good Peninsula planning is harder than it looks. LinkedIn is where that observation gets made, repeatedly, with specificity and calm authority.

---

## 1. Content Pillars

### Pillar 1: Peninsula Observations & Planning Intelligence
**Target mix: 40% of posts**

The core pillar. A real Peninsula observation — something true, specific, useful, and underappreciated — that reveals planning intelligence without explaining itself.

These posts do not begin with "We at Peninsula Insider..." or "Did you know...". They begin with the thing itself.

**Examples of the observation type:**
- "The ocean beaches on the Peninsula behave completely differently from the bay beaches. Knowing which side you're on before booking accommodation is the decision that most affects the weekend."
- "Red Hill and Sorrento are 35 minutes apart. But they are different trips. Most people try to do both in a day. Most people get neither right."
- "The best cellar-door days on the Peninsula start by 10am. By 2pm most tasting rooms are managing groups rather than conversations."
- "Autumn is the Peninsula's most consistent season. The accommodation is available, the cellar doors are unhurried, and the produce markets are at their best. Most visitors still book summer."

**What makes these posts work:**
- Begin with a specific, observable fact — not an opinion dressed as a fact
- The insight follows the observation naturally
- No call to action is needed — the value is self-contained
- PI's editorial position is implied, not stated

---

### Pillar 2: Editorial Guides & Linked Articles
**Target mix: 25% of posts**

Posts that reference a specific Peninsula Insider article or guide — not as a promotional announcement but as a useful extension of a Peninsula thought.

The link is earned by the post, not the other way around.

**Structure:**
The post stands on its own as a Peninsula observation. The article link appears at the end as "the longer version" or "more on this," not as the reason for the post.

**What to link and when:**
- Link when the post genuinely leaves a question unanswered that the article answers
- Link planning guides, area guides, and comparison pieces most frequently
- Do not link every post — standalone observations often perform better and build more authority
- Never lead with "We just published..." — the thinking, not the publishing event, is the hook

---

### Pillar 3: Seasonal & Timely Peninsula Thinking
**Target mix: 20% of posts**

Seasonal and weather-aware Peninsula observations — tied to real Peninsula rhythms, not calendar-marketing moments.

This pillar is where the Notebook / Quick Notes pattern from the site translates into LinkedIn voice.

**Examples:**
- "The Peninsula in May. The vine rows are bare. The cellar doors have space. The long lunches are better than they were in January."
- "Peninsula Hot Springs at 7am in late autumn. The contrast between the water and the air is the experience. This doesn't work in summer."
- "Flinders in winter. This is when the village makes sense — the bakery is warm, the golf course is empty, the ocean is all weather."

**What makes these work:**
- Specific and seasonal — not "the Peninsula is beautiful in autumn" but what specifically is better, and why
- Short. Often the most effective seasonal posts are three to four sentences.
- Present tense — places the reader in the moment, not observing from a distance

---

### Pillar 4: Editorial Thesis Posts
**Target mix: 10% of posts**

Occasional longer-form LinkedIn posts that articulate a Peninsula Insider editorial position. Used sparingly — once or twice a month at most.

These are the posts that explain *how PI thinks* rather than *what PI covers*.

**Examples of thesis topics:**
- Why Peninsula Insider doesn't use star ratings — and what it uses instead
- The difference between a guide that covers everything and one that covers the right things
- Why the Peninsula's strongest experiences are the ones that didn't feel like destinations when they were built
- Planning the Peninsula as a sequence problem, not a checklist problem

**Rules for thesis posts:**
- Never start with the thesis — start with a specific example or observation
- Maximum 300 words on LinkedIn — if it needs more, it belongs on the site
- One idea per post — thesis posts that try to make three points make none
- End without a call to action — the thought is the value

---

### Pillar 5: Behind-the-Editorial Glimpses
**Target mix: 5% of posts**

Selective, restrained glimpses into the publishing and editorial process. Used sparingly — no more than twice a month.

**What works here:**
- How a venue note gets written and verified
- The decision to exclude a place from a guide rather than include it with caveats
- How seasonal relevance affects editorial recommendations
- The difference between what ranks in search and what actually serves the reader

**What doesn't work:**
- Announcing new features ("We just launched...")
- Metrics and milestone posts ("We've reached 10k subscribers!")
- Process content that sounds like startup content ("Here's what we learned building Peninsula Insider")
- Self-congratulatory publishing announcements

## 2. Posting Cadence

**Target: 2 posts per week, occasionally 3**

Consistency matters more than volume. Two posts per week, published to a rhythm, will build more authority than five posts one week and nothing the next.

**Recommended publishing windows:**
- Tuesday: planning intelligence or observation post (pre-midweek, people are thinking about weekends)
- Friday: seasonal or timely post (pre-weekend relevance peak)
- Optional Wednesday: editorial guide link or thesis post

**Never post:**
- Saturday or Sunday (unless responding to something highly timely)
- Late at night
- Multiple posts in a single day

**The quality bar:**
Every post should clear the "would a Peninsula regular nod at this?" test. If it sounds like something you'd read in a tourism brochure, it fails. If it sounds like something a knowledgeable local would say to a friend asking for advice, it passes.

## 3. Post Structure Guidance

### The Peninsula Insider LinkedIn Post Shape

**Opening:** A real, specific Peninsula observation. Present tense. No preamble, no "we at Peninsula Insider," no rhetorical question.

*Good:* "The ocean side and bay side of the Mornington Peninsula are 20 minutes apart. They are completely different trips."  
*Bad:* "Did you know the Peninsula has two very different coastlines? 🌊"  
*Bad:* "At Peninsula Insider, we cover the whole Mornington Peninsula..."

**Development:** The observation earns the insight. One to two short paragraphs that build on the opening rather than pivot away from it. No subheadings. No bullet points (unless the content genuinely calls for it — a short list of 3–4 specifics, not a listicle).

**Close:** Either end naturally (the thought is complete) or place a single specific link. Not a CTA ("Click here to read more!"), not a question designed to generate comments ("What do you think?"), not an emoji parade.

**Length:**
- Observation posts: 80–150 words
- Seasonal/timely posts: 50–100 words
- Editorial guide posts: 100–180 words
- Thesis posts: 200–300 words maximum

**Paragraphing:** Short. Two to three sentences per paragraph. LinkedIn compresses text — rhythm matters.

### What to Avoid in Every Post

| Avoid | Because |
|---|---|
| Starting with "We..." | Makes it about the brand, not the insight |
| Rhetorical questions as openers | Signals engagement bait, not editorial confidence |
| Emoji as structural elements | Undercuts the calm authority tone |
| "🧵 Thread:" or listicle formatting | LinkedIn, not Twitter/X |
| "Thoughts?" or "What do you think?" as closers | Engagement bait |
| "PENINSULA INSIGHT:" or bold headers | Sounds like a press release |
| Hashtag stacks (more than 3) | Visual noise, signals content farming |
| Announcing articles rather than sharing thinking | Distribution, not authority |

## 4. Visual Direction

### Primary visual register: real Peninsula imagery

The strongest PI LinkedIn posts are anchored in a genuine Peninsula photograph — vineyard rows, coastal light, a cellar door in autumn, the pier at Flinders. These images do editorial work that branded graphics cannot.

**When to use real imagery:**
- Seasonal and timely posts (almost always)
- Area guide posts (always)
- Observation posts where the image reinforces the specific observation

**When to use editorial cards:**
- Thesis posts where the focus is on the idea, not the place
- Quote-style observations where the text is the content
- Article announcement posts (if used at all)

### Editorial card design principles

If using branded cards (the beige editorial treatment):
- Text-first, not image-first — the card is for words, not decoration
- One thought per card — not a summary or a bulleted list
- The PI card aesthetic should feel like the cover of a calm print publication, not a social media template
- Avoid overuse — if every post is a card, the real photography loses contrast and value

**Ratio:** Aim for roughly 60% real Peninsula imagery, 40% editorial cards. Seasonal posts should almost always use real imagery.

### What to avoid visually

- Conceptual graphics (lightbulb icons, graph overlays, map pin emojis over photos)
- Stock photography that could be anywhere in Australia
- Text-heavy carousel slides
- Canva templates that look like Canva templates
- Logos at the bottom of every image (once established, trust signals don't need to be repeated every post)

## 5. Linking Strategy

### When to include a link

**Include a link when:**
- The post explicitly sets up a question the article answers
- You are referencing a specific area guide, planning framework, or seasonal guide that extends the thought meaningfully
- The article genuinely adds something the post cannot — depth, specificity, an itinerary, a shortlist

**Post without a link when:**
- The observation is complete — no unanswered question
- The thought is better served by staying in the moment rather than directing away from it
- The article exists but adding it would feel like promotion, not extension

**Rule of thumb:** At least one post per week should have no link at all. The observation is the value. Not everything needs to drive traffic.

### Link placement

Always at the end, after the thought is complete.  
Format: "More on this: [brief description] → peninsulainsider.com.au/[path]"  
Not: "Read the full guide here!" or "Click to discover more 👇"

### What LinkedIn should do for the site

LinkedIn should build the kind of trust that makes someone search for Peninsula Insider specifically rather than clicking through from a link. The goal is authority, not traffic spikes. Traffic from LinkedIn audiences who chose to look up the site is worth more than traffic from a single viral post.

**Metrics that matter:**
- Follower quality (is the audience people who travel, plan, work in the industry?)
- Post saves (signals genuine usefulness)
- Direct navigations from LinkedIn referral

**Metrics that don't:**
- Impressions
- Post likes (engagement bait generates likes; useful editorial generates saves and follows)
- Viral moments

## 6. Editorial Principles

These are the working principles that keep the LinkedIn channel editorially coherent as it scales.

### 1. The observation earns the platform
Every post must begin with something real and specific about the Peninsula. Not a brand message. Not a content announcement. Not a thought leadership preamble. The Peninsula — a real place, a real observation, a real planning insight.

### 2. Restraint is the brand
Peninsula Insider's authority is built on selectivity — what it chooses to cover, how it covers it, what it leaves out. The LinkedIn channel should demonstrate the same restraint. Not every thought needs to be posted. Not every article needs to be promoted. Not every season needs a seasonal post. The curation is the product.

### 3. Useful before interesting, interesting before engaging
Posts should aim to be genuinely useful first. If they are also interesting, that is earned. If they also generate engagement, that is a by-product. Designing posts for engagement first inverts the value proposition and erodes the calm editorial authority that makes the channel worth following.

### 4. The Peninsula speaks for itself
The best posts on this channel will barely mention Peninsula Insider at all. They will make someone who has never heard of it feel like they have been reading it for years. When the Peninsula is described with specificity, accuracy, and genuine local knowledge, the brand is implicit.

### 5. Never explain the philosophy — demonstrate it
No post should say "At Peninsula Insider, our editorial approach is..." or "We believe that good planning matters..." These are claims. The posts themselves are the evidence. If the channel is demonstrating the editorial philosophy correctly, no post ever needs to explain it.

### 6. The voice is calm and confident
Not modest ("We humbly present..."). Not promotional ("This is the ultimate guide..."). Not conversational in the lowest-common-denominator sense ("Hot take: the Peninsula is underrated 🔥"). Calm and confident means: this is what we observe, this is what we know, here it is.

## Sample Posts (by pillar)

### Pillar 1 — Peninsula Observation
> The Mornington Peninsula's hinterland and its coastline are 20 minutes apart. Most visitors try to do both in a day. Most of those days don't work. The ridge rewards slow — a morning at one cellar door, a proper lunch, an afternoon on the same road. The coast rewards scope — the whole front beach arc, the cliff walk, the swim. Combined into a single day itinerary, each dilutes the other.

### Pillar 3 — Seasonal
> May on the Peninsula. The vine rows are bare. The cellar doors have space. The accommodation is available at rates it won't hold in January. The cooks are in the kitchen rather than on the floor. This is when the place actually works.

### Pillar 4 — Thesis (short)
> Peninsula Insider doesn't use star ratings. Not because we think the restaurant was good but not quite five stars. But because the meal that earns five stars at noon is a different meal from the same room at 7pm. The question isn't whether it's good. The question is whether it's right for the specific day you're having.

### Pillar 2 — Article reference
> Choosing where to stay on the Mornington Peninsula before deciding what to do there is the most common planning mistake. The itinerary shapes the accommodation decision, not the reverse. We built a decision framework for this: peninsulainsider.com.au/journal/how-to-plan-a-peninsula-weekend/
