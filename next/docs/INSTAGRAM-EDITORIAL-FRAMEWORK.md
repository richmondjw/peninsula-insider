## Channel Identity

**Instagram = atmospheric Peninsula intelligence**  
**LinkedIn = editorial authority**

These are different jobs. LinkedIn makes the rational case. Instagram makes you feel the place — with enough local intelligence layered in that the feeling is earned, not decorative.

Peninsula Insider on Instagram is not a photography account. It is not an influencer account. It is not a tourism feed. It is the visual expression of the same editorial sensibility that runs the site — calm, specific, locally intelligent, useful — expressed through image and atmosphere rather than planning frameworks and article links.

The goal: someone scrolling past a PI Instagram post should feel momentarily like they are already there, and that the person who took the photo knows something they don't.

---

## What "Atmospheric Peninsula Intelligence" Means in Practice

**Atmospheric** means: the image places the viewer in a specific moment — early morning fog on a vineyard, the particular quality of autumn light on the ridge road, the pier at Flinders before anyone else is there, a half-empty cellar door on a Wednesday afternoon. Not generic Peninsula scenery. A specific, experienced moment.

**Intelligence** means: there is always something to learn or notice. The caption adds one layer of local knowledge — when to be there, what you are looking at, why this moment is better than the obvious version of the same place.

The combination: an image that makes you feel something, and a caption that makes you understand why that feeling is earned.

## 1. Content Pillars

### Pillar 1: The Specific Moment
**Target mix: 40%**

The core Instagram pillar. A real Peninsula photograph that captures a specific, non-obvious moment — the kind of image that requires knowing where to be and when.

Not: postcard Peninsula
Not: golden hour at Sorrento foreshore (everyone has that shot)
Yes: the light on Red Hill vineyard rows at 8am in late April
Yes: the thermal pool at Peninsula Hot Springs before the crowd arrives
Yes: Flinders General Store on a Tuesday
Yes: the back beach at Cape Schanck in mid-winter — no one there, full weather

**Caption approach:**
One to three sentences. Present tense. Names the specific thing in the image rather than describing the mood. Adds the one piece of local knowledge that makes the image more valuable.

*Example caption:*
"Flinders Pier. Low tide, 7am, May. The town is 20 minutes from Sorrento and consistently empty by comparison. The difference between them is the decision about what you're actually there for."

---

### Pillar 2: Seasonal Peninsula Intelligence
**Target mix: 25%**

Seasonal photography that makes the timing legible — showing what the Peninsula is actually like right now, not the idealised version. Honest about what changes season by season and why that affects the experience.

**Examples:**
- Vineyard rows turning in late April → "The Peninsula in autumn. The commercial argument for summer doesn't apply here. This is when the cellar doors relax."
- Cape Schanck in a winter squall → "Bass Strait in June. Cold, completely clear, genuinely dramatic. This is what the back beaches are for."
- A Wednesday market in off-peak → "Mornington on a Wednesday morning. Smaller than Saturday, better produce, no queue."

**What makes these work:**
The image shows something real and specific. The caption names what season does to this particular place — not "autumn is beautiful" but what specifically autumn changes, and why that matters for a visit.

---

### Pillar 3: The Local Angle
**Target mix: 20%**

The shot or subject that visitors routinely miss. The thing locals know that the tourist trail doesn't surface. Presented without smugness — as an invitation, not an in-joke.

**Examples:**
- A cellar door road that isn't on Google Maps
- The particular bathing box colour sequence at Mornington
- A fog moment on Arthurs Seat that only appears between 7am and 9am
- The Balnarring Pub on a Sunday afternoon — what it looks like when it's working correctly
- The approach to Point Nepean from the shuttle bus — the thing you see before the park

**Caption approach:**
Name the specific knowledge. Not "a hidden gem" — name what makes it specific. "The fog on the Red Hill plateau usually clears by 10am. Which means the walk you want to do starts at 8."

---

### Pillar 4: Place Identity Cards
**Target mix: 10%**

Occasional single-image editorial cards — a place name, a one-line description, a visual. Used for: new destination pages launching on the site, area guide features, editorial announcements where the card format suits.

These are the Instagram equivalent of the LinkedIn thesis post — used sparingly and only when the card format genuinely serves the content better than a photograph would.

**Visual standard:** The PI editorial card aesthetic — warm paper tone, Cormorant Garamond, understated. Should feel like the cover of a small literary publication, not a social media template.

**Do not use cards for:** seasonal moments, local angles, specific moments. Those always use real photography.

---

### Pillar 5: Planning Intelligence Moments
**Target mix: 5%**

Very occasional posts that make a planning insight visual — not an infographic, but a photograph that illustrates a planning distinction.

**Example:**
A side-by-side or sequence image showing the bay beach and the ocean beach on the same day — calm and warm on one side, surf and weather on the other. Caption: "Same Peninsula, different trip."

Used sparingly. The planning intelligence lives primarily in the caption, not the image structure. These posts work when the visual genuinely illustrates the insight rather than just accompanying it.

## 2. Posting Cadence

**Target: 4–5 posts per week on the feed. Stories: 3–4 per week.**

Instagram rewards more regular posting than LinkedIn. The platform's algorithm favours consistency. But quality still determines whether the account builds genuine following versus passive scroll-past.

**Recommended feed rhythm:**
- Monday: seasonal or local angle (starts the week with atmosphere)
- Wednesday: specific moment (mid-week, midday post performs well)
- Friday: planning intelligence or local angle (pre-weekend relevance)
- Weekend: one post, photograph-led, lighter caption (people are actually on the Peninsula)

**Stories:**
- More immediate and timely than feed posts
- Can be more casual — a quick weather note, a cellar door visit in real time, a Saturday market morning
- Do not use Stories for content that belongs on the feed — Stories are for the in-the-moment layer

**Reels:**
- Used selectively — Peninsula seasonal moments that benefit from short video (fog clearing, waves at Cape Schanck, market activity)
- No talking heads, no voiceover explanations, no trending audio divorced from the Peninsula context
- If the sound doesn't come from the Peninsula, use no sound or a stripped ambient track

## 3. Caption Structure

### The Peninsula Insider Instagram caption shape

**Opening:** The image earns the opening — no need to describe what's already visible. Begin with what the image *doesn't* show — the timing, the knowledge, the context.

*Don't:* "Beautiful morning at Flinders Pier 🌅"  
*Do:* "Flinders Pier. Low tide, 7am. The town is twenty minutes from Sorrento and quietly a different trip."

**Body (when needed):** One to two additional sentences that add one layer of knowledge. Not a description of the image. Not a travel blog flourish. The specific thing you know about this place or moment.

**Close:** No call to action unless there is a specific article that genuinely extends the thought. No "link in bio" unless the link is genuinely useful. No hashtag clusters that signal content farming.

**Length:**
- Specific moment posts: 1–3 sentences
- Seasonal posts: 2–4 sentences
- Local angle posts: 2–4 sentences
- Planning intelligence posts: up to 6 sentences

**Hashtags:**
- Maximum 5, always at the end, never in the caption body
- Prefer specific over broad: #MorningtonPeninsula #RedHill #PeninsulaInsider over #travel #weekend #Victoria
- Do not use hashtag stacks — they signal volume-content accounts, not editorial ones

## 4. Visual Direction

### Photography standard

Real Peninsula photography. Specific, earned, non-obvious.

**What makes a PI Instagram image:**
- A specific time of day that most people miss (early morning, late afternoon, dusk)
- A specific season expressed visually (not labelled — shown)
- An angle or approach that requires local knowledge (the back road, the non-obvious viewpoint, the moment before the crowd)
- No obvious staging — nothing that looks like a photo has been taken for Instagram

**What to avoid:**
- Golden hour at Sorrento foreshore (everyone has this)
- Wide-angle vineyard shot with no specific character
- Food photography that could be anywhere
- Stock photography
- Drone shots that show the Peninsula from above rather than at human scale
- Overcorrected colours, heavy filters, preset-heavy editing

**Editing direction:**
- Natural, slightly warm, Peninsula-specific palette
- Preserve the specific quality of Peninsula light — the particular blue of Port Phillip, the red of the volcanic soils, the grey-green of winter coast
- No black-and-white unless the image genuinely calls for it (rare)

### The ratio: real photography vs editorial cards

**80% real Peninsula photography, 20% editorial cards maximum**

The card format should feel like an occasional statement, not the default. When every second post is a card, the photography loses contrast and the account starts to feel more designed than observed.

## 5. Linking Strategy

### Instagram's linking constraint is a feature, not a problem

Because Instagram limits link placement (link in bio / Stories link sticker / paid promotions), the channel is naturally less traffic-focused than LinkedIn. This is correct for what Instagram should do for PI.

**Instagram should:**
- Build atmospheric familiarity with the Peninsula that makes people want to visit the site eventually
- Function as the top-of-funnel awareness layer that LinkedIn doesn't reach
- Reinforce brand recognition for people who discover PI through search but confirm it through social

**Instagram should not:**
- Be optimised for traffic in the way that LinkedIn can be
- Use "link in bio" as a constant CTA — it signals distribution-first, not editorial-first

**When to use link in bio:**
- When a genuinely useful article directly extends the post's thought
- For area guide launches, major planning guide features, or timely seasonal content
- Not for every post — leave at least half of posts with no link at all

**Stories for links:**
- The Stories link sticker is lower-friction than "link in bio"
- Use for time-sensitive content: a seasonal guide, a weekend picks feature, a new area guide
- Keep the accompanying Story copy as simple as the framework suggests for captions

## 6. Editorial Principles

### 1. The image is the intelligence, the caption is the layer
On Instagram, the image must carry genuine atmospheric and observational value. The caption adds what the image cannot show — timing, local knowledge, planning context. Neither is decorative.

### 2. Specificity is the voice
"Vineyard fog in May" is not a caption. "The fog on the Red Hill ridge road usually clears by 10am. The hour before that is a different Peninsula." Specificity is what distinguishes editorial intelligence from tourism content.

### 3. Never sell the Peninsula — observe it
Tourist accounts and tourism boards sell the Peninsula. They tell you it's beautiful, that you should visit, that it's worth the drive. PI observes it. It shows you what a specific day actually looks like, from the angle of someone who has been paying attention. The imperative to visit is implicit in the quality of observation.

### 4. Seasonal honesty
The Peninsula does not look the same in every season. The Instagram account should reflect that honestly — winter posts that look like winter, not filtered into warmth. Fog that is actually foggy. A grey sea that is genuinely grey. The honesty is part of the intelligence.

### 5. The account reflects the editorial standard of the site
Every post should clear the same test as a site editorial decision: could this run anywhere else? If it is a generic vineyard photo with a generic autumn caption, it could run on a hundred tourism accounts. If it shows a specific moment from a specific angle with a caption that adds specific local knowledge — that is PI.

### 6. The audience is people who care about visiting well
Not volume. Not reach maximisation. The account is building a small, engaged, quality audience of people who care about how they spend time on the Peninsula. These are the people who will eventually use the site, book based on its recommendations, and share it with others who will do the same.

## Relationship to LinkedIn

| | LinkedIn | Instagram |
|---|---|---|
| **Register** | Editorial authority | Atmospheric intelligence |
| **Primary driver** | Rational / planning | Sensory / experiential |
| **Content** | Thinking and frameworks | Images and moments |
| **Caption length** | 80–300 words | 1–6 sentences |
| **Posting frequency** | 2–3/week | 4–5/week |
| **Links** | Regular, earned | Selective, non-pressured |
| **Engagement goal** | Trust and authority | Familiarity and desire |
| **Tone** | Calm confidence | Quiet observation |

Neither account should try to do the other's job. The strongest PI social presence is the two channels working together as distinct but complementary expressions of the same editorial intelligence.
