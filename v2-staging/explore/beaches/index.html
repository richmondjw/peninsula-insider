Explore 13 beaches · bay and back

The Peninsula's best beaches.

Two coasts. Two entirely different beach experiences. This is the honest ranking — back-beach drama, bay-side calm, and the hidden ones that reward the effort.

Start here

Bay or back. Choose correctly.

The Peninsula has two coastlines, and the difference between them is the single most important beach decision you will make. The bay side — Port Phillip — is calm, shallow, walkable, and reliable. The back beaches — Bass Strait — are open, consequential, often rough, and occasionally extraordinary.

First-time visitors default to the back beaches for drama. Regulars default to the bay for swimming and save the back beach for the photograph. Both are correct. The list below is ordered by character, not by ranking as "best swim" — where a beach needs specific conditions, we say so.

Swim between the flags. Bass Strait is not a forgiving coast.

If you only have one…

For drama: Sorrento Back Beach at low tide.

For swimming: Shelly Beach or Mills Beach.

For a walk: Mornington foreshore either direction from the pier.

For a photograph: Bushrangers Bay (walk in, bring a camera).

The ranking

Thirteen beaches, properly ranked.

  1. 01

    Sorrento Back Beach

    Sorrento · Back beach

    The Peninsula back-beach most visitors remember. Patrolled, limestone cliffs at the western end, strong surf; swim only in patrolled hours and inside the flags. Near Coppins Track for the best clifftop walk on the Peninsula.

  2. 02

    Portsea Back Beach

    Portsea · Back beach

    The big, wild Bass Strait beach. Long, open, consequential — the kind of beach you visit for the drama rather than the swim. Unpatrolled outside peak; respect conditions.

  3. 03

    Bushrangers Bay

    Cape Schanck · Hidden bay

    Reached only on foot (5km return walk from Cape Schanck). Basalt cliffs framing a sandy bay where Main Creek meets the sea. The Peninsula beach experience that most visitors never have. Not for swimming — for standing still.

  4. 04

    Diamond Bay

    Sorrento · Hidden bay

    A clifftop drop-away onto a small sand beach. Access via stairs; not always safe at the bottom depending on surf. The view from the top platform at Coppins Track end is the better experience than the beach itself.

  5. 05

    Shelly Beach

    Portsea · Bay

    Soft, calm, clear water on the Port Phillip side. Sheltered, family-friendly, and one of the most under-visited easy swims on the Peninsula. The path in is short and flat.

  6. 06

    Mills Beach

    Mornington · Bay

    The northern Peninsula's bathing-box beach, 100 metres from Mornington centre. Flat, calm, walkable, and served by the foreshore path either direction. The default beach for a northern Peninsula morning.

  7. 07

    Mornington Main Beach

    Mornington · Bay

    Similar character to Mills but with the pier and Schnapper Point as visual anchors. Sunrise and sunset both work. The foreshore path links it to the main town strip in under ten minutes.

  8. 08

    Balnarring Beach

    Balnarring · Back beach

    Long, flat, Western-Port side. Quieter than the Bass Strait back beaches. Works well for dog-walking sections (check local rules) and for a broader, less dramatic beach day.

  9. 09

    Safety Beach

    Safety Beach · Bay

    Broad, open, sheltered — Port Phillip side, long flat expanse. Better for long walks and casual swims than for the visual drama of the back beaches. Works well with a coffee from Dromana after.

  10. 10

    Rye Front Beach

    Rye · Bay

    A gentler, family-oriented Port Phillip beach. Good for paddleboarding on calm mornings. The pier is a good photographic anchor. Close to supplies in Rye town.

  11. 11

    St Andrews Beach

    St Andrews · Back beach

    Long, quiet, open Bass Strait beach. Often fewer people than Sorrento or Portsea back beaches. Pair with a round at St Andrews Beach Golf Course for a very Peninsula kind of day.

  12. 12

    Gunnamatta Beach

    Fingal · Back beach

    The surfers' back beach. Open Bass Strait. Patrolled in summer at certain hours only; consequences real outside patrols. Not the casual swim beach; the serious-surf beach.

  13. 13

    Shoreham Beach

    Shoreham · Bay

    Western-Port side, long and quiet. The Honeysuckles side of Shoreham is often overlooked. Good for long beach walks and for escaping the southern Peninsula crowds in summer.

Common questions

Beaches, answered.

Which side should I swim? Bay side — Port Phillip — unless you are an experienced ocean swimmer and the back beach is patrolled. Mills, Shelly, and Mornington Main are the best default swim options.

Are back beaches dangerous? Bass Strait is a serious coast with rips and currents. Stick to patrolled beaches in patrolled hours (Sorrento Back, Gunnamatta in summer). Outside that, walk the beach, take the photograph, do not swim.

Dog-friendly beaches? Dogs are banned from Mornington Peninsula National Park. On-lead access is generally available on the Mornington, Mount Martha, Balnarring and Rosebud foreshores. Check council rules — they change seasonally.

Best beach for children? Shelly Beach (Portsea) or Mills Beach (Mornington). Both are shallow, sheltered, and close to supplies.

Bathing boxes? Mornington foreshore for the northern Peninsula's most photogenic row. Mills Beach has them directly in front; the full foreshore walk shows them all.

Sunset beach? Any Port Phillip beach on a clear evening. Safety Beach and Rye Front work especially well when the sky settles behind the Bellarine.
