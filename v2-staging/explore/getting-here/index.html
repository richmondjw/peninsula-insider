Explore Transport

Getting to the Peninsula.

Ninety minutes from Melbourne CBD on a clear day. Forty by ferry from Queenscliff. This is how to arrive — freeway, airport, public transport — without turning the drive into the first problem of the weekend.

From Melbourne

The freeway route.

The standard route is the M3 Eastlink to the M11 Peninsula Link, joining the Mornington Peninsula Freeway (M11) southbound. The freeway serves the Peninsula's spine — exits for Mornington, Mount Martha, Dromana, Rosebud, Rye, Sorrento and Portsea are well-signed. From CBD to Mornington is typically 60 minutes, to Sorrento 95 minutes. Summer Saturday mornings add 30–45 minutes; leave before 9am or after 2pm to avoid the worst.

For Red Hill, Main Ridge, Merricks and Flinders, exit at Dromana and take the Arthurs Seat Road up onto the plateau. The drive up the hill is part of the arrival experience — do not rush it.

From Geelong / Great Ocean Road

The Sorrento–Queenscliff ferry.

The car ferry runs every hour each way between Sorrento and Queenscliff. 40 minutes across Port Phillip Bay. Useful in two ways: as a scenic alternative to driving around Melbourne (saves about 90 minutes from Geelong to Sorrento), and as a way to combine a Peninsula weekend with a Great Ocean Road week. The ferry takes cars, caravans, bikes and foot passengers. Book ahead in peak season. searoad.com.au for schedules.

From Melbourne Airport

Airport to Peninsula.

Melbourne Tullamarine to Mornington is 75 minutes, to Sorrento 110 minutes, via the CityLink and EastLink–Peninsula Link corridor. There is no direct airport bus to the Peninsula — you need a car or a private transfer. SkyBus to Southern Cross Station plus a V/Line coach is possible but slow; for international visitors, renting a car at the airport is the practical choice.

Public transport

Train and coach.

Metro train from Flinders Street Station to Frankston (50 min). From Frankston, V/Line coach services connect to major Peninsula towns — Mornington, Dromana, Rosebud, Rye, Sorrento and Portsea. Services are infrequent compared to Melbourne routes; verify timetable before committing to public transport. The Peninsula is genuinely difficult to navigate without a car once you arrive; public transport works for a focused single-town base (Sorrento is the strongest option), not for a touring weekend.
