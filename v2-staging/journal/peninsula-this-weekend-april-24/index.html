Journal Weekend Picker — 24 April 2026

Peninsula This Weekend - 24 to 26 April

— By [object Object] 5 min read

Peninsula This Weekend is our recurring Friday-useful dispatch: the plan we’d actually make, the booking worth locking in, and the bit of weekend thinking we’d happily do for you.

The ANZAC long weekend has its own register on the Peninsula. The light is softer, the foreshore quieter, and the days run at a tempo that rewards one serious commitment rather than four lighter ones.

For 24 to 26 April, that one commitment is ANZAC Day dawn in Sorrento. Everything else this weekend works best when it sits inside the mood that morning creates.

The lead

Go to the dawn service in Sorrento

If you do one fixed thing on the Peninsula this weekend, make it ANZAC Day Dawn Service in Sorrento.

Sorrento at dawn has the right physical seriousness for the occasion: cold in the air, subdued foreshore light, the quiet arrival of locals and visitors who are there for the right reason. The setting does some of the work, the rhythm does the rest.

Commit to it properly. Stay nearby if you can, get up earlier than you want to, and let the rest of the day stay deliberately under-programmed. Dawn service, coffee, breakfast, a foreshore walk, then a slower afternoon. That is the shape ANZAC Day takes here when it goes well.

The second booking that actually earns its place

Sorrento Writers Festival

If you want a cultural anchor for the rest of the long weekend, Sorrento Writers Festival is the cleanest answer.

It gives Sorrento a more substantial shape than the usual long-weekend drift of coffee and browsing. The town feels better when there is a reason to stay put for a few hours, and a writers festival suits late-autumn Peninsula pacing: thoughtful, low-noise, adult without trying.

Pick one or two sessions with people you actually want to hear from, then leave room for lunch and air around them. The weekend feels cultivated rather than busy when there is some unclaimed space around the booked component.

If Sunday needs shape

Point Nepean half-day

If Saturday belongs to Sorrento, Sunday morning has a clear answer: Point Nepean.

The logic is simple. You are already at the southern tip. Point Nepean is ten minutes from Portsea and does something Cape Schanck cannot on an ANZAC weekend: it ties landscape directly to the occasion. Fort Nepean, the Quarantine Station, the shuttle up the headland, the tunnels - all of it carries weight that makes more sense on this weekend than a generic walk-and-springs formula.

The Parks Victoria shuttle runs from the Visitor Centre. The fort and Quarantine Station infrastructure are open daily. Keep it to the morning. Leave the afternoon deliberately open.

The slower luxury move

Alba Thermal Springs

If the weekend needs one quieter indulgence, Alba Thermal Springs is the cleaner answer this time around.

Alba is the adults-first, sanctuary-grade option - less chaotic than Peninsula Hot Springs on a long weekend, more architecturally considered, and better suited to the reflective register that an ANZAC long weekend already sets.

Book a late afternoon slot. Arrive unhurried. Treat it as the period at the end of the weekend’s sentence, not another item on the activity list.

One local edge

Make Sorrento your base

The weekend makes far more sense geographically if you treat Sorrento as home for the two days.

Dawn service, Writers Festival, Point Nepean - all within ten minutes of each other at the southern tip. Adding Cape Schanck, Red Hill, or Mornington into this mix costs 30 to 45 minutes each way and flattens the weekend’s coherence. The Peninsula rewards a settled base more than a full lap.

If you are not staying in Sorrento, get there early on Saturday and stay south through Sunday before heading home.

The family weather card

Moonlit Sanctuary in hand

If you are travelling with kids and want a card to hold for the day the weather turns or the energy drops, Moonlit Sanctuary Easter School Holiday Program is the most resilient option.

It absorbs mixed ages, uncertain weather, and a group whose patience is running shorter than planned. Pair it with one decent lunch on the drive back and the day still feels intentional rather than salvaged.

A note on rhythm

The Peninsula is at its best this weekend when it is allowed to be thoughtful. Dawn in Sorrento, one second move chosen for the register you actually want — literary, restorative, or low-friction — and unclaimed space around both. That is enough for a very good April weekend here.
