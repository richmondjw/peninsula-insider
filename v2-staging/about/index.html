About · Independent editorial

Independent editorial. Honest, opinionated coverage.

Peninsula Insider is building the editorial guide the Mornington Peninsula doesn't have yet — one that treats readers like adults and venues like subjects, not clients.

The Mission

The Peninsula deserves better than a brochure.

The Mornington Peninsula has over 200 cellar doors, more Good Food Guide–hatted restaurants per square kilometre than anywhere outside inner Melbourne, and a coastline that changes personality four times in 40 minutes of driving. It deserves coverage that takes it seriously.

Most of what exists online is either tourism marketing dressed as editorial, or user-generated review content that tells you a restaurant has 4.3 stars without explaining what the 4.3 actually felt like. Neither helps you build a weekend.

Peninsula Insider is an attempt to fix that. We write opinionated, service-first editorial that answers the questions weekend visitors actually have — and we do it without taking money from the venues we cover.

Editorial standards

Four rules we don't bend.

These aren't aspirations. They're operating constraints. If we break them, the whole thing stops working.

  1. 01
    Editorial first, always

    Every venue, every recommendation, every word - earned, not bought. If a restaurant appears, it's because the editor ate there and had an opinion worth writing.

  2. 02
    We pay our own way

    We pay for our own meals, our own rooms, our own parking. The moment a publication starts taking comps without saying so, the reader has to do the work of figuring out what's real. We'd rather just write it straight.

  3. 03
    Opinions, not stars

    Star ratings are a consensus machine. We write editor notes - a short, honest paragraph about what a place actually feels like when you sit down. Agreement is optional; clarity is not.

  4. 04
    Service journalism

    Every piece answers a real question a weekend visitor actually asks: where to eat, what to do when it rains, which cellar doors are worth the appointment. The Peninsula doesn't need more content. It needs better answers.

The Difference

What Peninsula Insider does that nobody else on the Peninsula bothers with.

Editor notes, not star ratings

Every venue carries a short, opinionated paragraph about what the place actually feels like — the light at 2 pm, the menu's weak spot, whether the wine list lives up to the view. Stars flatten; sentences clarify.

Service journalism that answers real questions

Every Journal piece starts from a question a weekend visitor actually asks: Where do I eat if I can't get a booking? What do I do when it rains? Is the drive to Flinders worth it for one night? If we can't name the question, we don't write the piece.

Weekend-shaped, not list-shaped

Most Peninsula content is a flat list of places. Ours is sequenced: itineraries that put lunch, walk, and bed in the right order; service pieces that tell you when to arrive, not just where.

Seasonal rotation, not stale archives

The homepage, section hubs, and venue surfaces shift with the Southern-Hemisphere calendar. What you see in April is different from what you see in December — because the Peninsula is a different place in every season.

How it's built

Structured content, not a WordPress blog.

Peninsula Insider is built on an atomic content layer. Every venue, place, experience, and event is a structured entity with typed fields — not a blog post pretending to be a database. This means every page is connected: a venue knows which place it belongs to, which articles mention it, and which itineraries route through it.

The architecture is designed for editorial integrity at scale. When we update a venue's editor note, it flows through to every page that references it. When we add a new experience to a place, the place hub updates automatically. The structure does the work so the editors can focus on the writing.

The site is statically generated — no server, no database queries at runtime, no third-party trackers. Fast, private, and permanent. Every page has a real URL that will still work in ten years.

Colophon

A small editorial operation, deliberately.

Peninsula Insider is a one-desk publication. The editorial team lives on the Peninsula, eats on the Peninsula, and walks the same coastline the pieces describe. The coverage is slow, deliberate, and local — because the best regional journalism has always worked that way.

The site publishes a weekly dispatch. Peninsula This Weekend lands weekly, and the newsletter is the only way to get it all in your inbox.

If you're a venue owner who wants coverage, there's nothing to buy — see our contact page for how to reach us. If you're a reader who spotted an error, we fix fast: corrections@peninsulainsider.com.au.
