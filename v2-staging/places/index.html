Places Town hubs — local geography

The Peninsula, town by town.

The Peninsula has twenty distinct localities — from working harbour towns on Western Port to winery hamlets in the hinterland to white-sand ocean villages at the tip. No two towns are alike. This hub organises all of them by subregion so you know which to pair and which to avoid putting in the same day.

— By The Editors 37 towns 140 venues mapped
Why towns matter here

Pick the subregion first. Everything else follows.

Most people come to the Mornington Peninsula for a category — beaches, wine, hot springs, golf — and pick accommodation that looks close to that category. That usually works out fine. But the Peninsula is specific in a way that categories flatten: the beach towns on Port Phillip Bay are calm, family-tilted, and good in summer; the ocean-side towns face Bass Strait and suit people who like dramatic coastline and solitude; the hinterland ridge is where serious food and wine happens; the Western Port fringe is quieter, underrated, and where artists and racehorses live.

Staying in Portsea when you want wineries is a 40-minute drive in the wrong direction. Staying in Red Hill when you want beaches puts you 20 minutes from any coast.

Understanding the subregions changes what you book. Read the subregion intros below, use the town cards to shortlist, then go deep in the individual pages.

Department · I

Hinterland — Red Hill, Main Ridge, Merricks

The elevated central ridge between the bay and the ocean. Cool mornings, fog-prone winters, and volcanic red soils produce some of the best cool-climate Pinot Noir and Chardonnay in Australia. The area is predominantly rural — farmstands, cellar doors on gravel roads, the occasional serious restaurant tucked into a vineyard. There is no beach within five minutes. That's the point.

Department · II

Ocean coast & Western Port

Rye, Cape Schanck, Flinders, Shoreham, Balnarring, and Hastings wrap the peninsula's southern and eastern flanks. Rye is the most relaxed ocean-side town. Cape Schanck is a lighthouse at the edge of a national park. Flinders is a village that thinks of itself as a destination. Shoreham and Balnarring are quiet to the point of deliberate. Hastings is a working port with a marina and a train station.
