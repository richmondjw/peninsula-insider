The Shortlist

Cellar doors worth the appointment.

Five producers we'd send a close friend to this autumn — ranked by the ratio of experience to effort, not the view from the tasting room. Each has been visited at least three times in the last twelve months.

Read the full methodology
  1. 01
    Main Ridge Appointment · 1 hr By seated tasting
    Ten Minutes by Tractor

    Three single-vineyard Pinots poured in sequence, built to teach rather than impress. The cellar door staff know the answer to every question, including the ones you don't think to ask. The benchmark by which every other cellar door should be judged.

    3 hats Restaurant on site Flight 4 wines
  2. 02
    Red Hill Appointment · 90 min Food pairing available
    Montalto

    The most complete Peninsula cellar-door experience — wines that justify the fuss, a sculpture walk that isn't a contrivance, and a kitchen that treats the tasting as part of a meal rather than an obstacle. The pizzeria is the smart hedge for weekends.

    1 hat Sculpture garden Pizzeria adjacent
  3. 03
    Merricks North Appointment · 45 min By appointment only
    Yabby Lake

    Block-specific Pinots from some of the oldest vines on the Peninsula. The quietest of the top tier — no restaurant, no sculpture walk, no hotel. Just wine, made with precision, and the time to talk about it properly. A purist's cellar door.

    Halliday 97 pts Single-vineyard Pinot
  4. 04
    Red Hill South Walk-in friendly Tasting fee waived on purchase
    Port Phillip Estate

    The one to send architecture friends to. A cellar door carved into the hillside, restaurant with a one-shot view down to Western Port Bay, and a lineup that's improved noticeably over the last five vintages. Easy to book, hard to forget.

    Accommodation on site Views to Western Port
  5. 05
    Main Ridge Appointment · 60 min Library releases available
    Paringa Estate

    The cellar door to pair with a long lunch rather than a short visit. The Chardonnay has quietly become some of the best in the country. Ask about the library tastings — they're where the real education is, and the waitlist is shorter than you'd expect.

    1 hat Library tastings Restaurant adjacent

Editor's Letter

On the quiet authority of a good autumn.

Autumn is the season the Peninsula stops performing. The weekend crowds thin, the vintage trucks finish their last runs, and the ridge takes on the colour it has for about six weeks a year. Most places you want to get into, you can actually get into. The light is good. The rooms are warm. The producers, finally, have time.

This issue is built for the six weeks where the Peninsula is at its most itself — before winter settles in and the coast goes quiet.

We've rebuilt the cellar-door shortlist from scratch this season, visited every restaurant on the long-lunch list at least twice, and walked every beach on the ranking. The newsletter, Peninsula This Weekend, will land in your inbox at 6 pm Wednesday with the current issue's picks tightened into a single page.

If this is your first issue with us — welcome. If you're returning — thanks for the patience through the refit.

—
