# Boating the Mornington Peninsula

Two coastlines, two very different tides. Bring your own boat and pick the ramp that suits the water today, or leave the trailer at home and hire one or go out with a skipper.

## Choose your ramp

All 6 are free to launch. The tide decides more than the drive does: 3 work at any tide, the rest need the water up.

### Port Phillip Bay

Calmer water and a small tidal range. Tide station: **Portsea**. [Today's tides for Portsea, on the Bureau of Meteorology site](<http://www.bom.gov.au/australia/tides/>)

### Western Port

Strongly tidal. Time your launch and your return. Tide station: **Stony Point**. [Today's tides for Stony Point, on the Bureau of Meteorology site](<http://www.bom.gov.au/australia/tides/>)

- #### [Hastings](<https://peninsulainsider.com.au/boating/ramps/hastings-boat-ramp/>)

Upgraded in 2024, with an all-abilities hoist on the new southern pontoon. The base for Western Port charters.

**Launch at**

Any tide

**Lanes**

4

**Parking pressure**

Medium

- #### [Warneet](<https://peninsulainsider.com.au/boating/ramps/warneet-boat-ramp/>)

Smaller and quieter, with a central pontoon, for the whiting and flathead grounds of the northern arm.

**Launch at**

Mid to high tide

**Lanes**

2

**Parking pressure**

Medium

## Before you launch

**Respect the Rip.** South and south-westerly swell running into Port Phillip Heads stands up into dangerous waves. Do not cross in unsuitable conditions, whatever the size of your boat or your experience.

- ### Read the forecast twice

Check the [Bureau of Meteorology](<http://www.bom.gov.au/australia/tides/>) marine forecast the night before and again on the morning you go.

- ### Use the right tide station

Portsea for the Peninsula's bay ramps, Stony Point for Western Port. Heading up the bay towards Melbourne, switch to Port Melbourne. [Willyweather](<https://tides.willyweather.com.au/>) and the [Bureau](<http://www.bom.gov.au/australia/tides/>) both publish them.

- ### Know your second ramp

Pick an alternative before you leave home, in case the first is full or the tide turns against you.
