Why we’d go

The Peninsula's most complete estate experience, sculpture, garden, cellar door, and a one-hat kitchen, done at a scale that still feels human rather than corporate.

Drive in and the estate opens up, lake, sculptures, kitchen garden, olive grove, before you've reached the cellar door. This is the place you bring visitors to prove that Victoria does wine country properly. The sculpture trail is serious, not decorative: major works on permanent loan, set into the landscape so they read differently in every season.

Photo · Two Palms Australia, courtesy of Visit Victoria

The restaurant earns its hat honestly. Pasta made daily, produce grown on-site or sourced from neighbours within five kilometres, and a wine list anchored by the estate but ranging thoughtfully. The terrace in summer is as good a lunch setting as the Peninsula has.

Arrive early for a cellar door tasting before lunch. Set aside the afternoon. The estate rewards time.

Editor's verdict

 Montalto is one of the Peninsula's most complete estates: the wines are genuinely good, the restaurant earns its hat, and the kitchen garden is not decorative - it supplies the kitchen. Simon Black's winemaking respects site above all, and the single-vineyard releases make an honest case for what Red Hill South terroir can do. If you only have time for one estate that covers all bases - vines, table, grounds - this is the call.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does Montalto take walk-ins or do you need to book?

Walk-ins are welcome for groups of 1–6 people. Bookings are available for those who prefer a reserved session. The restaurant requires a separate booking.

### Does Montalto have a restaurant?

Yes. The Restaurant at Montalto (1 Hat, Age Good Food Guide 2025) serves Modern Australian cuisine with produce from the estate kitchen garden. The Piazza offers casual dining.

### Are dogs allowed at Montalto?

No - the official site explicitly states no pets allowed onsite at Montalto.
