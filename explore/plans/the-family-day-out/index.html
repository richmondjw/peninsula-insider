The Peninsula Family Day Out

A gondola ride, a brewery lunch and a bay beach, with a coffee stop only if the day runs early.

Follow the route →

Keep the Eagle, brewery lunch and beach as the three anchors. This route works best Thursday to Sunday when the brewery bar and kitchen usually serve lunch; confirm current hours and menu, including public holiday changes. The Eagle can close in extreme weather, and its flexi tickets do not require a reserved time. The final coffee is optional, because Commonfolk Mornington closes at 3 pm on weekends.

Day 1

  1. Morning

    Arthurs Seat Eagle

    Start with a return gondola ride, about 15 minutes each way, and pause at the summit lookout. You can board at the base or summit station; check conditions and choose your starting car park before you travel.

  2. Midday

    Red Hill Brewery

    The deck and beer garden make this a relaxed family lunch. The bar and kitchen are usually open Thursday to Sunday, with food from midday; menus vary, so check today's service before setting out.

  3. Afternoon

    Mount Martha Beach

    Make the beach the afternoon pause. Choose whether to swim after checking the day's conditions, and supervise children near the water.

  4. Afternoon

    Commonfolk Coffee

    Only add this Mornington coffee stop if you can reach it before closing. Commonfolk's weekend cafe service ends at 3 pm, and weekday service at 3:30 pm. Otherwise, let the beach be the finish.

Booking checklist

  1. recommendedCheck Arthurs Seat Eagle operating conditions and ticket options
  2. essentialConfirm Red Hill Brewery is serving lunch on your chosen day
  3. recommendedCheck Commonfolk Mornington's closing time if you want the optional coffee stop

Lookout
Red Hill 1.5 h · Easy

Arthurs Seat Eagle

Arthurs Seat is the Peninsula's highest point and the Eagle is the gondola that runs people up it - about fifteen minutes each way, dangling over eucalypt canopy, with the bay widening out below until Melbourne appears on the northern horizon. As a first move on a Peninsula weekend it is surprisingly effective: you arrive, you go up, and the whole region orients itself around you in a way that makes the rest of the trip easier to plan. The summit has a cafe, a lookout deck, and short walking trails through the scrub. Come at the start of a clear day for the view north across the bay; come at the end of a clear day for the light spreading over the ridge. Go midweek if you can - weekends in school holidays can be busy. Book tickets online if visiting in summer.

Beach
Mount Martha 1 h · Easy

Mount Martha Beach

Mount Martha is the gentlest swim on the Peninsula. The beach curves south in a long, shallow arc of pale sand, protected from the prevailing winds, shallow enough for kids, and deep enough fifty metres out for a real swim. The northern end carries the famous heritage-listed bathing boxes; the southern end runs into the coastal reserve and eventually hits the cliff track south toward Balcombe. Unlike the back beaches at Sorrento, this is a swim-first beach rather than a walk-first one. If you only have one bay-facing afternoon on the Peninsula and you want it to feel genuinely restorative rather than dramatic, come here. Easy parking outside of January. A single beach kiosk. Nothing to plan - which is exactly the point.
