The architects

## Five designers, one peninsula

The Peninsula's concentration of serious course architecture is not accidental. Here is who built it and what they were trying to do.

Tom Doak

St Andrews Beach Golf Course; The National Gunnamatta Course

Doak designed St Andrews Beach and redesigned The National's private Gunnamatta Course in 2019. Both use the existing land to reward strategic shot choices.

Peter Thomson

Moonah Links Open Course

Five-time Open Champion Peter Thomson designed the Moonah Links Open Course. It is the Peninsula's visitor-bookable Thomson layout.

Greg Norman

The National Moonah Course

Greg Norman and Bob Harrison designed The National's private Moonah Course. Victorian nonmembers must be invited and play with a member; interstate and overseas visitors can request tee times directly.

Robert Trent Jones Jr

The National Old Course; RACV Cape Schanck Resort

Robert Trent Jones Jr designed The National's private Old Course and the separate visitor-bookable RACV Cape Schanck course.

Tony Cashmore

The Dunes Golf Links

Tony Cashmore designed The Dunes, an 18-hole public links course built through Rye's dune country.

## When one person plays, what does everyone else do?

The Peninsula solves this better than almost anywhere else in Australia. The non-golfer's day can be as good as the golfer's - if you plan it properly.

- [Peninsula Hot Springs](<https://peninsulainsider.com.au/journal/peninsula-hot-springs-vs-alba/>) - A bathing session can fill the time of a golf round. Check the route from the chosen course before booking both.

- [Cape Schanck boardwalk](<https://peninsulainsider.com.au/explore/cape-schanck-boardwalk/>) - A clifftop walk towards Pulpit Rock with exposed coastal views. Match the walk to the weather and available daylight.

## Planning a golf weekend

A Peninsula golf weekend works when the accommodation is close enough that travel between the course and the stay does not eat the day. The RACV Cape Schanck Resort is the most integrated: on-property accommodation, an 18-hole course, and the spa within walking distance. Moonah Links has its own on-property stay. Both allow two rounds in two days without getting in a car between them.

For those not staying on a golf property, Sorrento and Blairgowrie are useful southern bases for the Cape Schanck cluster and the Sorrento ferry. Check the route for your chosen tee time because Peninsula traffic can change the drive substantially. The full stay-and-play guide pairs accommodation with nearby courses.

Common questions

## FAQ

What is the best golf course on the Mornington Peninsula?

Moonah Links Open Course for championship pedigree and full public access. St Andrews Beach for Tom Doak architecture at a public green fee. The National is a private club at Cape Schanck; eligible visitors should confirm access with the club before making plans.

For a mid-handicapper who wants the best round rather than the most famous address: Moonah Links Open Course is the correct answer. The challenge is real, the conditioning is excellent, and the round does not end with the score card as the main memory.

Can you play golf on the Mornington Peninsula without a club membership?

Yes. Moonah Links, St Andrews Beach, RACV Cape Schanck, Eagle Ridge, The Dunes, Mornington Golf Club, and Rosebud Country Club list visitor green fees; check current tee times with each club. Flinders welcomes visitors seven days in published time windows, and Portsea offers limited visitor green-fee times. The National is private: Victorian nonmembers need an invitation and must play with a member, while interstate and overseas visitors can request tee times directly.

How much does golf cost on the Mornington Peninsula?

Green fees change with season, day and tee time. RACV, Moonah Links and The Dunes publish rates; other clubs may show a live price in their booking calendar. Check visitor eligibility and the current amount directly with each club before committing to a round.

Is there accommodation near the Peninsula golf courses?

RACV Cape Schanck Resort is on-site with direct access to its 18-hole course. Moonah Links has on-property accommodation with stay-and-play packages covering both the Open and Legends courses. For other clubs, choose accommodation near the course and check travel time before booking. The full [stay-and-play guide](<https://peninsulainsider.com.au/explore/plans/mornington-peninsula-golf-stay-and-play/>) maps each option with travel times and booking advice.

How do the Cape Schanck and other Peninsula courses differ?

The Cape Schanck / Fingal cluster brings together St Andrews Beach, Moonah Links, and three of The National's four courses (Old, Moonah, Gunnamatta). The fourth National course, Long Island, is at Frankston rather than Cape Schanck. The southern courses sit in coastal duneland exposed to Bass Strait weather, with links-style characteristics and a strong concentration of recognised course architecture.

The other clubs are spread across Flinders, Boneo, Rye, Mornington and southern bayside towns. They vary in visitor access, terrain and travel time; check the club rules and route that suit your trip.

How far is Mornington Peninsula golf from Melbourne?

Drive times from Melbourne vary materially by the course, departure point and Peninsula traffic. Mornington is the northernmost venue in this guide; the Rye, Fingal and Cape Schanck courses require travelling farther south.

Check a live route for the exact tee time before booking. Allow extra margin on Friday afternoons, weekends, summer dates and school holidays, when Peninsula roads can be busier.

What is the best time of year for golf on the Mornington Peninsula?

Autumn (March–May) and spring (September–November) are the strongest seasons - stable weather, firm course conditions, and shoulder-season pricing on green fees and accommodation. The coastal light in autumn is genuinely extraordinary on the Cape Schanck courses.

Summer (December–February) is busy and can be windy at coastal courses. Winter is playable on dry days but expect some course closures after heavy rain. Links-style courses like St Andrews Beach and The Dunes drain faster than parkland courses and often stay open when inland layouts close.
