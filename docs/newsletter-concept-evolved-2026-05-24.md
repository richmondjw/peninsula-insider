## 1. What we're building

Not a newsletter. A recurring Peninsula habit.

The Insider is a weekly dispatch from someone who pays close attention to the Mornington Peninsula — its rhythms, its seasons, its quiet shifts — and has something worth saying about it. It helps readers decide, plan, and feel connected to the Peninsula between visits.

The positioning in one sentence:

> Each week, the one thing worth knowing, one place worth going, and one opinion worth having about the Mornington Peninsula.

This is a **trusted local dispatch** — not a content roundup, not a tourism bulletin, not an events calendar. The curation and restraint are the product.

---

## 2. The shift from where we were

**Old frame:** here are our latest articles.  
**New frame:** here's what's worth knowing this week.

**Old frame:** what we published.  
**New frame:** what we noticed, what changed, what we'd do this weekend.

**Old frame:** content delivery mechanism.  
**New frame:** seasonal intelligence layer — a planning companion readers come back to.

This is not a structural change. It is a voice and intent change. The sections can stay similar. What changes is the *reason* someone would open it.

---

## 3. What the newsletter is for (revised priority order)

1. **Help the reader decide.** This weekend. This season. This kind of trip. Turn vague Peninsula intent into a concrete plan.
2. **Earn trust through editorial judgement.** The reader should feel that whoever wrote this actually considered whether it was worth their time — and skipped anything that wasn't.
3. **Keep the Peninsula present between visits.** Most subscribers visit 4–10 times a year. The newsletter is the thread that keeps the Peninsula alive in their minds the rest of the time.
4. **Build the habit of asking PI.** Over time, readers should think of Peninsula Insider as the place to ask questions and plan experiences — not just read articles. The newsletter plants that seed.

---

## 4. Voice and tone (evolved)

The register that differentiates PI:
- Observational, seasonal, slightly opinionated
- Confident without being smug
- Specific without being exhaustive
- Human without being chatty
- Calm without being bland

What this sounds like in practice:
- "The Peninsula is quieter this week."
- "Rain changes the Peninsula properly."
- "The best Peninsula Saturdays usually involve one booking."
- "This is the autumn the locals keep to themselves."

**What to avoid:**
- Sales energy ("don't miss this!")
- Tourism brochure language ("world-class," "stunning," "acclaimed")
- Generic listicle framing ("Top 5 reasons to...")
- Corporate distance
- Clutter and over-explanation
- Any framing that sounds like a press release

The house rules from the PI style guide apply in full. No em-dashes. No exclamation marks. No star reviews. Specificity over adjectives.

---

## 5. Cadence

**Weekly. Thursdays, 7:00 AM AEST.**

Thursday morning is the Melbourne weekend-decision window. Most weekend bookings happen Thursday–Friday. A 7am Thursday email lands exactly when the reader is already thinking "what are we doing this weekend?"

"When it's worth sending" is the long-term editorial ideal — but weekly is the structural discipline that makes the brand real. The cadence is the product. Keep 2 issues in the bank at all times.

---

## 6. Content architecture — six recurring modules

Keep it to six modules. Tight enough to scan in 90 seconds. Named sections readers come back for. Not a document — a letter.

### Module 1 — Peninsula This Week (the opener)
2–4 sentences. First-person. What the Peninsula feels like *right now*. Weather, season, vibe, mood. What has shifted or is shifting.

No links required. This is the hook — the reason someone reads a human voice rather than a directory.

Examples:
- "The Peninsula is quieter this week. Autumn hit the vine rows overnight and the cellar doors have their tables back."
- "Rain changes the Peninsula properly. The coast gets its colour back, the roads empty out, and the hot springs make sense again."

This block should never feel like a weather report. It should feel like someone who was just there.

### Module 2 — One Thing Worth Doing
Single recommendation. Specific and opinionated. Not "things to do this weekend" — one thing, with a sentence on *why this weekend specifically*.

Format: place name, one sentence of why, one sentence of context (timing, what to book, who it suits), a link.

This is the utility core. If only this block gets read, the newsletter has done its job.

### Module 3 — Worth Knowing (2–3 notes)
Practical intelligence. New opening, seasonal change, chef change, booking tip, event worth flagging, quiet spot to know about. One line each. Factual. Link-heavy.

No opinion here — just information the reader couldn't easily find themselves. Max 3 items.

### Module 4 — From the Journal
One article. The best piece from the week, or the one that fits the seasonal moment. Headline, 2-sentence dek, link.

Not a list of links. One link. Written as a genuine introduction, not a headline dump.

### Module 5 — Ask The Insider (rotating, from month 2)
Seed this with a question PI actually gets asked, or one worth planting. Answer it in 3–5 sentences, in voice.

Initially: plant questions to build the habit. Later: reader-submitted. This section trains readers to think of PI as the place to ask planning questions — not just read articles. It also seeds the long-term "Ask" product.

### Module 6 — The Local Note (sign-off)
A short, specific, human observation to close on. Something noticed this week — a seasonal shift, a quiet moment, something only someone paying close attention would catch. 2–4 sentences. No agenda.

This is the leave-behind — the thing that makes the newsletter feel like it was written by someone who was actually there, not assembled from a content brief.

Examples:
- "The vines on Mornington–Flinders Road turned properly this week. Worth a drive just for that."
- "Sorrento is noticeably quieter than it was a fortnight ago. The good tables are available again."

**Total length target: 600–900 words.** Over 1,000 and we're wasting the reader's time.

## 7. Seasonal and planning framing (evolved)

The newsletter should actively reflect how the Peninsula changes — and how that changes what's worth doing.

Seasonal prompts to keep in rotation:
- "This is the right time of year to..."
- "The Peninsula is [slower / busier / at its best] right now because..."
- "What changes this week is..."
- "If you've been waiting for a good weekend to [X], this is it."
- "Rainy-day Peninsula: here's what we'd do."

This seasonal layer is what makes the newsletter feel timely, not just regular. It should feel like someone who's paying attention — not someone working through a content calendar.

---

## 8. Balance rules (what we won't become)

These are the failure modes to actively protect against:

- **Not an events calendar.** Events get mentioned when they are genuinely worth flagging, not to pad Worth Knowing.
- **Not a content roundup.** One Journal link per issue. Not three, not five.
- **Not sales-forward.** No "exclusive offers," no commercial partner promotion in the editorial voice.
- **Not a tourism brochure.** The restraint and opinion are the product. A newsletter that recommends everything is recommending nothing.
- **Not cluttered.** Six modules. That's the ceiling, not the target.
- **Not generic.** If it could run in any lifestyle newsletter, it shouldn't run here.

---

## 9. How this connects to the broader PI system

The newsletter is one node in the PI intelligence layer — it should drive toward:
- **Ask** (readers asking PI for planning help)
- **Plans** (curated Peninsula day shapes)
- **What's On** (seasonal events and experiences)
- **Future membership/community** (the reader relationship the newsletter builds)

Each module should, where natural, point toward one of these destinations. Not forcefully — as a genuine next step for a reader who wants more.

## 11. Production checklist per issue

Before any issue ships, confirm:

- [ ] Peninsula This Week has a specific, seasonal observation — not a generic opener
- [ ] One Thing Worth Doing is genuinely specific to *this* week — not a perennial pick
- [ ] Worth Knowing has 2–3 items that feel genuinely useful, not padding
- [ ] From the Journal has one link, not several — with a real introduction
- [ ] The Local Note is specific and observational — not generic, not a filler sentence
- [ ] Total word count is under 900
- [ ] No press-release language anywhere
- [ ] Subject line is conversational and specific — not a headline or a generic prompt
- [ ] Preview text is a second hook, not a repeat of the subject line
