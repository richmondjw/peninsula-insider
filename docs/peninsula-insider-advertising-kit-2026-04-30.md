## 1. About Peninsula Insider

Peninsula Insider is a modern newsroom for the Mornington Peninsula.

Local first. Editor-led. Published every Thursday. Every claim verified. Every commercial relationship disclosed. Built for digital, not converted to it.

We cover the Peninsula across five pillars: Eat & Drink, Stay, Do, Plan, and the Insider's Journal. The Journal carries a weekly cover story, longform features, interviews with the chefs and winemakers shaping the region, and seasonal guides that go deeper than the destination-marketing copy currently on the internet for this place.

Two products carry the brand. The publication itself, at peninsulainsider.com.au. And The Insider's Letter, a weekly five-minute read sent every Thursday morning, written and signed by the editor.

The publication is founded and edited by **James and Emma Richmond**, with a structured editorial desk handling commissioning, research, drafting, fact-checking, copy, and scheduling.

## 2. The firewall

Peninsula Insider takes commercial partnerships. We are open about that. The publication is funded in part by the businesses who appear on it. What we do not do is let those businesses influence the editorial.

Three claims hold the line:

**Curated commercial.** Only Peninsula businesses appear on PI. No programmatic, no off-region brands, no Booking.com retargeting filling our sidebar. If a reader sees a partner story or a sponsor placement here, the venue is one we know.

**Editor-led.** James and Emma sign the editorial. They pick the cover. They never appear at advertiser-only events. The masthead is theirs. The rate card is the publisher's. The two do not meet.

**Disclosed by default.** Every paid item carries a Partner Content eyebrow. Every founding partner is publicly listed. Every conflict is declared in the article that touches it. Once a year we publish a one-page firewall audit, naming what we ran, what we declined, and where the line strained.

The differentiator is not "we don't sell." Visit Mornington Peninsula sells. Broadsheet sells. The differentiator is we tell you who is paying, and the editorial does not bend.

### What we will not run

- Programmatic display advertising
- Off-region brands
- Off-tone advertisers, including any business whose offer would embarrass the publication or its readers
- Sponsored editorial that contradicts our published view of the same venue or category
- Any commercial item that fails our editor's reader-benefit test

We say no more often than we say yes. That is the point.

## 3. The newsroom

PI is built as a structured publication, not a one-person blog.

Editorial commissioning runs through a desk model: research, drafting, fact-checking, copy, scheduling, editorial sign-off, each handled as separate functions with structured handoffs. Every factual claim in a feature is logged with a primary source, a confidence rating, and an expiry date before draft. Every cover passes editor sign-off before publish.

The result is a five-to-seven-day production cycle on a feature that would take most regional publishers five weeks, at the editorial standard of a national title.

For a partner, that means the difference between commissioning a story in autumn and seeing it published in time for the season you wanted to capture, versus waiting until the season has passed.

## 6. The Charter Story

The flagship public product. A full editorial feature, written by our desk, photographed, fact-checked, edited, scheduled, and published to PI standard.

**What you get:**

- A 1,200-word feature article, drafted by the editorial desk, signed off by the editor before publish
- Original photography commissioned for the piece, including a hero image and gallery
- One quarter of priority placement on the section hub most relevant to your venue
- One mention in The Insider's Letter in the issue the story drops
- Permanent hosting and indexing, refreshed annually
- A clear "Partner Content" eyebrow on the article

**What you supply:**

- A short brief, 200 to 400 words, on what you would like the story to cover
- Access to the venue, the people, and any background information our desk asks for
- A signed agreement that the published piece must pass our editor's reader-benefit test before invoice

**Production cycle:** Five to seven working days from brief acceptance.

**Capacity:** Twelve Charter Stories across calendar 2026. Once full, the next window opens in January 2027.

**Investment:** $4,500 AUD ex GST.

## 11. How to apply

Every partnership begins with a conversation.

1. **Submit an enquiry.** Visit peninsulainsider.com.au/partners and complete the commercial enquiry form. Tell us about your venue, what you would like to do, and any timing constraints.
2. **Editorial review.** The editorial team reviews every submission. We do not accept every venue, and we do not guarantee coverage. If your venue fits the PI editorial brief, we move forward. If it does not, we tell you straight.
3. **Conversation.** A 20 to 30 minute call or coffee, depending on what you are exploring. James or Emma will join, depending on category.
4. **Agreement and brief.** Once you confirm a tier and product, we send a one-page agreement, a brief template, and a production schedule.
5. **Production.** Our desk takes it from there.

Editorial submissions, as opposed to commercial enquiries, are reviewed under our standard editorial intake. There is no charge for editorial coverage, and there is no guarantee of it.

## 13. Standards we hold

**Editorial firewall.** No advertiser influences our editorial recommendations or rankings. The default test for any commercial item is reader benefit, with the burden on the commercial side to show it.

**Disclosure.** Every paid item is labelled. Every Founders' Circle partner is named publicly. Every conflict is declared in the article that touches it.

**Annual audit.** Once a year, we publish a one-page firewall audit naming what we ran, what we declined, and where the line strained. The first audit publishes November 2026.

**No fake bylines.** Every PI piece carries either a James Richmond byline, an Emma Richmond byline, the byline of a named human contributor, or "The Peninsula Insider" house byline. We do not invent writers.

**No surprise pricing.** Everything in this kit is the price you will pay. No hidden production fees, no rate-card-versus-actual gap.
