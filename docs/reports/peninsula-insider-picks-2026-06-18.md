## The picks (3, in priority order)

### 1. The closing window — MPRG Autumn Exhibition

**Why this pick, why now:** The Autumn Exhibition at Mornington Peninsula Regional Gallery closes **30 June**. That is twelve days from publication. The show covers contemporary Australian painting, a rotating photography wall, and a Peninsula-focused historical exhibition in the side gallery. Free entry, no booking, five minutes from Main Street.

The "why now" is unassailable: this is the final clear week before the exhibition closes. After the solstice weekend noise subsides, the gallery is at its quietest and best — warm, properly lit, and genuinely rewarding on a grey Saturday afternoon.

**Editorial call:** Deliberately held back from the Sunday dispatch, which is fully committed to the solstice weekend. The dispatch covers the festival; this pick covers the cultural counter-move for readers who want something quieter and indoors.

**Source:** events/mprg-autumn-exhibition.json
**Details:** Mornington Peninsula Regional Gallery, 2/350 Dunns Road (Civic Reserve), Mornington. Closes 30 June 2026. Free entry. Open most days; check mprg.mornpen.vic.gov.au for hours. Allow 45–60 minutes. Pair with lunch on Main Street.

### 2. The outdoor move for this weather — Cape Schanck Lighthouse Walk

**Why this pick, why now:** The Cape Schanck Lighthouse walk is one of the most dramatic short coastal walks in Victoria, and mid-winter is when it is at its most theatrical. The 4-kilometre return track runs from the 1859 working lighthouse down to Pulpit Rock via a boardwalk that sits above sheer rock with ocean on both sides. In grey light and wind, the Bass Strait side of the Peninsula feels wilder and more remote than it does in any other season.

The editorial contrast from the May 20 pick (Greens Bush) is intentional: Greens Bush was the hinterland interior; this is the exposed coast. Both are winter-best, but they serve different moods. Greens Bush is stillness and canopy. Cape Schanck is wind and vertigo.

**Source:** experiences/cape-schanck-lighthouse-walk.json
**Details:** Cape Schanck Road, Cape Schanck. 90 minutes return, easy difficulty (mostly boardwalk and wide gravel). Lighthouse tours available on selected days. Check wind before the boardwalk; the cape is exposed. Go early morning or late afternoon for the best light. Pair with lunch in Flinders or a warm stop at the Flinders Hotel.

---

### 3. The food/stay call — Barragunda Dining, Cape Schanck

**Why this pick, why now:** Barragunda is the Peninsula's most genuinely farm-to-table dining experience, and winter is when chef Simone Watts's kitchen makes the most sense. The 1000-acre regenerative estate at Cape Schanck supplies most of what appears on the plate, the menu changes weekly based on what the farm has produced, and the room is quiet, intimate, and deliberately under-publicised.

The previous Insider Picks (May 20) led with Tedesca Osteria. Barragunda is the natural winter follow-on: different terroir (Cape Schanck rather than Red Hill), different format (farm dining rather than wood-oven Italian), and a different register of quietness. Where Tedesca is warm and convivial, Barragunda is restrained and immersive.

**Editorial call:** This is the pick for readers who have already done the headline Peninsula restaurants and want the one that locals talk about in winter. The booking pressure is real: limited seats, no walk-ins, and word has been spreading.

**Source:** venues/barragunda-dining.json
**Details:** 165 Boneo Road, Cape Schanck. $$$$. Book well in advance at barragundadining.com.au. The menu is produce-driven and changes weekly. Lower-intervention wine list. Allow three hours. Pair with a stay at RACV Cape Schanck Resort or a spa session at One Spa.

## Editorial notes

- Three picks this week rather than four. The solstice weekend dominates the editorial calendar, and the Sunday dispatch is already saturated. Adding a fourth pick would mean either duplicating the dispatch or forcing a weaker angle. Three strong picks beats four diluted ones.
