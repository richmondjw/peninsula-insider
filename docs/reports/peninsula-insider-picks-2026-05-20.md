### 2. The outdoor move for this week — Greens Bush (Two Bays section)

**Why this pick, why now:** The forecast has shifted. This is no longer the "last clean autumn afternoon in open coastal light" editorial frame that drove the Bushrangers Bay pick in Week 20. That window is closed. The current conditions — overcast, 12°C, some chance of rain — suit a **hinterland walk** better than a coastal clifftop. Greens Bush is the largest remnant bushland on the Peninsula: 8.9km through old grasstrees (some 200+ years), fern gullies, tea-tree and open eucalypt. Genuinely beautiful in this kind of still, grey light. Autumn/winter best.

The editorial contrast from last week is intentional: last week was "use the light while it holds." This week the light has gone; the call changes accordingly.

**Source:** experiences/greens-bush-two-bays-section.json
**Details:** Greens Bush Carpark off Baldrys Road, Merricks North. 4 hours, moderate, return circuit or car-shuffle one-way. Best combined with a Red Hill or Merricks lunch.

### 3. Closing cultural window — Tall Poppy at Kelli Lundberg Art, Moorooduc

**Why this pick, why now:** The Tall Poppy show closes 7 June. This week is the clearest window before King's Birthday noise starts crowding the editorial calendar. The claim is that this is the **first Melbourne Design Week exhibition programmed on the Mornington Peninsula** — the NGV Melbourne Design Week 2026 catalogue corroborates it. Fifteen Australian designers and makers: furniture, ceramics, textiles, design objects, curated by Tiffany Jade. Free entry, no booking.

The editorial pull: it is a regional first, the precinct is a converted 1920s grocery with 20+ working maker tenants (not a white-cube gallery), and it pairs cleanly with a Red Hill lunch and a Moorooduc wine stop. Two weeks of exhibition life remaining after this weekend.

**Source:** events/tall-poppy-melbourne-design-week-exhibition.json
**Details:** Kelli Lundberg Art @ The Studio & Co., Moorooduc. Closes 7 June. Free entry. Open most days 10am–4pm (10am–3pm Sundays, closed Tuesday).

### 4. The food/stay call — Tedesca Osteria for winter's first serious dinner

**Why this pick, why now:** Tedesca's register has shifted decisively now. In April and early May, the fireplace and set menu felt like something you'd book *around* a clear-sky weekend. Now, cool and overcast, it becomes the anchor. Brigitte Hafner's wood-oven kitchen is at its peak relevance in late May and June — the set-menu format works with winter produce, the dining room is warm, and the rooms book three to four weeks ahead for King's Birthday.

This is the week for a first winter dinner before it competes with the King's Birthday occasion premium. Not a celebration — just the best dinner on the Peninsula right now.

**Source:** venues/tedesca-osteria.json
**Details:** 96 Junction Road, Red Hill. 2-hat. Set menu format. Book ahead; the room is small. Check current availability at tedescasteria.com.au.
