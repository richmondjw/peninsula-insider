## The picks (4, in priority order)

---

### 1. The anchor event — Mornington Winter Music Festival, King's Birthday Weekend

**Why this pick, why now:** The dispatch mentions the Mornington Winter Music Festival as a footnote. It earns its own treatment. Twelve years running. Twenty-plus venues across Mornington town on the King's Birthday weekend: jazz, blues, soul, R&B, street entertainment on Main Street. Free to $25 per gig, mostly walk-in. The festival runs across all four days (5–8 June), so it functions as the fallback plan for every hour the Winter Wine Festival crowds become too much, or for the reader arriving Sunday or Monday. It is the only thing on the Peninsula this weekend that gives you somewhere to be at 9pm on a cold June night.

**Editorial call:** This is the complementary cultural layer to the wine-heavy dispatch. For readers who've already seen the Wine Festival coverage, the Music Festival is the dimension the dispatch deliberately left at a single line. It covers a different time of day, a different part of the Peninsula, and a different kind of long-weekend day.

**Source:** events/mornington-winter-music-festival-2026.json
**Details:** Fri 5 – Mon 8 June. Main Street Mornington and 20+ venues across town. Free to $25 per gig. Schedule at morningtonmusicfestival.com.au.

---

### 2. The outdoor move (all-weather) — Truffle Hunt, Red Hill Truffles

**Why this pick, why now:** Red Hill Truffles season opened 30 May. This is the peak early-season booking window. The hunts run on farm terrain with waterproof-appropriate gear — they run in rain as well. This is the single active, hands-on, outdoor thing on the Peninsula right now that the weather doesn't compromise. The editorial shape is strong: you book early in the season, the truffle yield is highest, and the format (hunt plus meal at Red Hill Estate Restaurant or pizza on the farm) runs as a complete half-day plan. The dispatch covered the truffle angle only as a footnote for readers locked out of the Wine Festival. This edition leads with it properly.

**Booking pressure:** Multiple sessions run daily throughout the season, but King's Birthday weekend tends to fill quickly. The window this week is to book while weekend spots are still available.

**Editorial contrast from previous picks:** The May 20 edition mentioned Red Hill Truffles as the upcoming launch; the May 27 run noted it as the Week 22 lead. By this edition, the hunt season is confirmed running, farmgate days are operating, and the booking call is urgent-but-not-panicked — normal booking horizon for the next free weekend.

**Source:** events/red-hill-truffles-winter-truffle-hunt-season.json, experiences/red-hill-truffles.json
**Details:** Red Hill Truffles, 1180 Mornington-Flinders Road, Main Ridge. From $189pp (pizza hunt). Perigord black truffle season May–August. Book via redhilltruffles.com.

---

### 3. The warm indoor move — Peninsula Hot Springs, Bathe-in Cinema (Thursday)

**Why this pick, why now:** With Tall Poppy closing Sunday and the dispatch covering the Wine Festival and Stonier, there is a gap in the coverage of the "what do you do when the crowds clear after the long weekend" question. Bathe-in Cinema at Peninsula Hot Springs runs every Thursday through to 31 July. A bathing session ticket is required; the film lineup posts weekly. The editorial angle is the shape of the plan: King's Birthday Monday is the decompression day, but Thursday is the recovery move — thermal water, cold air on your face, a film you didn't plan, and the springs quieter than any weekend.

**Timing note:** This is a recurring event, but the winter context — dark by 5:30pm, temperature dropping — makes the combination of outdoor thermal bathing and a film work in a way it doesn't in summer. The editorial frame is seasonal specificity, not novelty.

**Source:** events/peninsula-hot-springs-bathe-in-cinema-thursdays.json
**Details:** Peninsula Hot Springs, Fingal Road, Fingal (near Rye). Bathing session required. Film lineup posted weekly at peninsulahotsprings.com. Runs Thursdays to 31 July.

---

### 4. The forward-look booking — Sorrento Solstice Festival, 20–21 June

**Why this pick, why now:** King's Birthday is the obvious handle this week. Sorrento Solstice is two weekends out — close enough to earn a planning note. Free entry, foreshore event, fire performers, a 6-metre burning effigy on the bay at 6:30pm Saturday, lanterns, food trucks, sunrise swim Sunday. The editorial point is that winter on the Peninsula builds toward two festivals: this long weekend is the first; Solstice is the second. Naming the second now gives the reader a reason to stay connected and gives the edition's curation some forward momentum.

**Editorial call:** This is the "what's next" marker, not a full pick with booking pressure. It earns a shorter treatment. For readers who've already locked in King's Birthday plans, this is the sentence that makes them bookmark the edition.

**Source:** events/sorrento-solstice-festival-2026.json
**Details:** 20–21 June, Sorrento Village and Foreshore. Free. Free ticketing recommended (Humanitix). 2pm–9pm Saturday, sunrise swim Sunday.
