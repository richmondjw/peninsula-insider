# Peninsula Insider — Social Media & Publishing Strategy
*Established: May 2026 | Owner: Emma Richmond + Remy*

---

## Core Principle

Peninsula Insider's timing and cadence strategy is not built on generic engagement data.

It is built on matching:
- Platform behaviour
- Peninsula planning behaviour
- Emotional mindset
- Trip timing

The content is decision-making, aspirational planning, and regional editorial guidance.
Not breaking news. Not memes. Not influencer content.

**Quality of observation always outperforms perfect timing.**
A highly specific post ("Bass Strait after a cold front") will outperform generic content at the "perfect" time.

## Publishing Rhythm — The Week

### Wednesday
**Newsletter dispatch**
The weekly planning dispatch. Mid-week is the sweet spot — people are settled into the week, not yet in end-of-week wind-down. Wednesday morning launch.

### Thursday 8:30am
**LinkedIn #1 — Newsletter echo**
Catches morning commute and desk settle-in. Professional planning mindset. Aspirational escape energy. Auto-posted.

### Thursday 7:00–9:00pm ⭐ Priority slot
**Instagram #1 — Weekend anticipation**
The single most important Instagram window.

Why: Thursday evening is when people mentally begin leaving Melbourne. The Peninsula becomes psychologically available. Content arriving here isn't just a post — it's arriving at the precise moment the decision is forming.

Best content for this slot:
- Shape of the Day
- Weather/timing observations
- The quieter version (where most people won't go)
- One Good Stop framing
- Atmospheric photography

### Friday or Saturday 8:00–10:00am
**Instagram #2 — Live Peninsula atmosphere**
Weekend is beginning. People are planning or already there. Real-time energy.

Best content for this slot:
- Market mornings
- Weather shifts
- Ferry movement
- Swimmers, café energy, long lunches
- "Right now on the Peninsula" observations

### Tuesday 8:30am or Thursday 4:00pm
**LinkedIn #2 — Journal article**
Editorial depth. Positions Peninsula Insider as considered regional intelligence, not a listings account. Auto-posted from journal archive. Thursday 4pm secondary spike catches people mentally starting their weekend transition.

## Channel Strategy

### Newsletter (Weekly — Wednesday)
The editorial heartbeat. Full weekend planning dispatch. Content from here feeds all other channels.

Natural CTA path: Instagram Thursday teaser → newsletter subscribers → repeat.

### Instagram (2x/week — manual, Emma)
**Cadence:** Wednesday newsletter publishes; Instagram posts Thursday evening and Saturday morning.

**Tone rules:**
- Short, natural, confident
- Use "we" — never "we recommend"
- 2–4 lines max. Observation first, context second
- No hashtag dumps in caption — 3–5 tight hashtags in first comment
- First line must hold on its own. If it doesn't, the post doesn't go up

**Content pillars (rotate):**
1. Place/landscape — the Peninsula in its current season
2. Human atmosphere — market, ferry, café, swimmer, movement (not posed)
3. Weather or light observation — always specific, never generic
4. The quieter version — something most people drive past

**The people note:** Over time the feed may benefit from small human moments — not influencer portraits, not posed content. Movement, atmosphere, café energy, ferry loading, market mornings, swimmers, long lunches in weather shifts. Otherwise the Peninsula can feel slightly too empty/editorialised.

**Expansion note:** 4x/week is the natural next level when photography quality and content depth is consistently there. Start at 2x, expand when it earns it.

### LinkedIn (2x/week — automated)
**Cadence:** Thursday morning + Tuesday morning or Thursday afternoon.

**Audience:** People planning weekends during work breaks, thinking aspirationally midweek, looking for intelligent content rather than entertainment.

**Best windows:**
- Primary: Tue–Thu 8:00–10:00am (commute scroll, morning settle-in, aspirational escape mindset)
- Secondary: Wed–Thu 3:00–5:00pm (weekend-planning psychology, lighter editorial reads)

**Avoid:** Friday afternoons, weekends, Monday mornings.

**Content:** Newsletter echo + journal articles. Journals do what Instagram can't — they show the editorial intelligence behind the brand. Someone who finds PI through a journal piece reads it differently than an Instagram scroll. Different audience, longer attention, higher intent.

### Email — Ad Hoc (Monthly or Seasonal)
Beyond the weekly dispatch: targeted sends to subscribers for significant moments, seasonal shifts, or events. Not a broadcast blast — considered, occasion-driven.

### Outreach — Peninsula People (Ongoing)
Community building layer, separate from broadcast. Local venue owners, growers, community figures, contributors. People who should know PI exists and who might contribute, share, or become part of the editorial fabric over time.

Not a distribution list. A relationship layer.

## Editorial Refinements & Standing Principles

### 1. Cadence — watch 4–5 posts/week carefully
The strongest long-term cadence may settle at 3–4 excellent posts/week as photography quality rises and captions deepen. Fewer highly observed posts beats filling cadence.

### 2. "The Quieter Version" must never become superiority signalling
Quiet confidence — not "you tourists don't know this."

The tone must remain welcoming, not exclusionary. "Quieter alternative" content can drift into insider smugness, anti-tourist energy, and performative localism. The current examples handle this correctly. Protect it carefully.

### 3. Weather framework — stay specific, avoid repetition
"Weather changes everything" can become overused philosophy.

The observation must always stay concrete.

✅ "Bass Strait in a front."
❌ "The weather changes the Peninsula."

Specificity is what makes it feel insightful rather than a recurring motif.

### 4. People — the missing layer (long-term)
The framework is landscape/place/flow dominant. That fits the brand well. But over time: small human moments add warmth and prevent the Peninsula feeling too empty or editorialised.

Not influencer portraits. Movement, atmosphere, energy.
