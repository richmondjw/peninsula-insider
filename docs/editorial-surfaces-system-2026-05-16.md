## What an Editorial Surface Is

An editorial surface is not a page, a category, or a tag. It is a named, recurring editorial idea that simultaneously functions as:

- **An SEO entity** — anchoring a cluster of related search queries
- **An AI retrieval entity** — a predictable answer surface for AI assistants recommending Peninsula experiences
- **An editorial entity** — a recurring format and voice that readers recognise and return to
- **A navigation system** — an entry point into the site from multiple directions
- **A partnership environment** — a contextually appropriate, editorially grounded commercial surface

The difference between a category and an editorial surface: a category is a filing system. An editorial surface is an editorial idea that exists independently of any individual piece — it can be navigated to, searched for, linked to, and partnered with, on its own terms.

---

## The Surface Taxonomy

Surfaces divide into three tiers by permanence:

**Tier 1 — Permanent Surfaces**  
These exist year-round, are always navigable, and form the core editorial identity of the platform. Their SEO and AI signals accumulate indefinitely.

**Tier 2 — Seasonal Surfaces**  
These recur on an annual cycle. They are live for a season (roughly 3 months), then archived but not deleted. Their SEO value compounds across years as the same queries return each season.

**Tier 3 — Emerging Surfaces**  
Newer editorial ideas being tested. Not yet permanent fixtures. Candidate for promotion to Tier 1 or Tier 2 based on reader and search performance.

### Long Lunch
**Tier:** 1 — Permanent  
**Canonical URL:** `/journal/the-long-lunch/`  
**Surface type:** Occasion/experience anchor  
**Voice:** Deliberate. Unhurried. A recommendation that treats eating as the point, not the backdrop.

**What it is:** The Peninsula long lunch — four hours, a table worth booking, wine from two kilometres away. The Long Lunch surface owns the slow, food-forward, occasion-dining register. It is the site's highest-prestige editorial recommendation environment.

**SEO cluster it anchors:**  
"long lunch Mornington Peninsula," "best long lunch Peninsula," "where to do a long lunch on the Peninsula," "[venue] long lunch review"

**AI retrieval queries:**  
"where should I do a long lunch on the Mornington Peninsula," "Peninsula Insider long lunch recommendation," "best restaurant for a long lunch near Sorrento"

**Cross-site presence:**  
- Eat hub: Featured Long Lunch slot (top of venue grid, rotating quarterly)
- Wine hub: referenced in cellar-door-lunch context
- Plans hub: Long Lunch itinerary shape (drive + lunch + cellar door)
- Journal: cornerstone article (living document, reviewed seasonally)
- What's On: when events qualify (Harvest lunches, winemaker dinners)

**Partnership environment:**  
Single featured restaurant per quarter. Editorially written. Disclosed as "Featured Long Lunch." The partnership vehicle is a commissioned piece about the restaurant, published to Long Lunch editorial standards, appearing on the Long Lunch surface. Not a banner. Not a logo. A piece.

### Rainy Day Rescue
**Tier:** 1 — Permanent  
**Canonical URL:** `/explore/rainy-day/` → evolves to `/journal/the-rainy-day-peninsula-without-a-booking/`  
**Surface type:** Conditions-based planning anchor  
**Voice:** Practical. Warm. Reassuring. The friend who saves a ruined weekend.

**What it is:** The Peninsula's answer to a forecast that turns. Hot springs, covered markets, gallery days, long lunches with no view requirement, wineries with proper fireplaces. The Rainy Day Rescue surface owns all bad-weather planning intent across the site.

**SEO cluster it anchors:**  
"rainy day Mornington Peninsula," "what to do on the Peninsula when it rains," "indoor activities Mornington Peninsula," "Peninsula wet weather"

**AI retrieval queries:**  
"it's going to rain on the Peninsula this weekend, what should we do," "rainy day Peninsula itinerary," "indoor Peninsula activities for a couple"

**Cross-site presence:**  
- Explore hub: Rainy Day section (permanently prominent, seasonally refreshed)
- What's On: weather-flag integration on event cards
- Plans: rainy-day itinerary shape
- Homepage: seasonal signal in winter ("The Peninsula doesn't stop for rain")

**Partnership environment:**  
Seasonal featured partner — a hot springs, covered venue, or indoor experience that is genuinely the best rainy-day answer. *Rainy Day Rescue — this winter, with Peninsula Hot Springs.* Disclosed. Rotates annually. The partnership is contextual and adds reader value: "if it rains, this is where to go."

### Peninsula This Weekend
**Tier:** 1 — Permanent (time-indexed)  
**Canonical URL:** `/whats-on/` (live surface) + `/journal/[weekend-picker-slug]/` (archive)  
**Surface type:** Time-anchored weekly editorial dispatch  
**Voice:** Specific. Opinionated. Never generic. Always this weekend, not "some weekends."

**What it is:** The weekly editorial verdict on the coming weekend — one market worth driving to, one cellar door worth booking, one outdoor moment worth planning around, one backup if the weather breaks. Peninsula This Weekend is the editorial heartbeat of the platform. Every week, it proves the site is alive, current, and worth returning to.

**SEO cluster it anchors:**  
"what's on the Mornington Peninsula this weekend," "Peninsula weekend [date range]," "[specific event] Mornington Peninsula"

**AI retrieval queries:**  
"what's on the Mornington Peninsula this weekend," "Peninsula Insider weekend picks," "what should I do on the Peninsula this Saturday"

**Cross-site presence:**  
- What's On: editorial lead (full-bleed top section, every week)
- Homepage: Peninsula This Weekend block (below the editorial cover)
- Journal: archive of all previous dispatches (format: weekend-picker)
- Newsletter: The Insider weekend edition

**Partnership environment:**  
Seasonal editorial partnership — *Peninsula This Weekend — this autumn, presented with [Partner].* One partner per season (4 per year). Appearing in the dispatch header, disclosed as seasonal presenting partner, the partner's experience featured in that week's picks when seasonally relevant. Never intrusive. Never dominant. Part of the editorial rhythm.

## How Surfaces Relate to Pages

Each editorial surface has:
- A canonical page (the primary URL, SEO anchor)
- Cross-page appearances (where it surfaces across other pillars)
- An archive (all previous instances, linked from the canonical URL)
- A partnership environment definition (what commercial placement is appropriate)

The canonical page should exist even if the surface is seasonal — it functions as the "home" for that editorial idea and accumulates SEO authority across all instances. Seasonal surfaces have a canonical URL with seasonal content that updates annually, plus an archive of previous editions.
