# Peninsula Insider — Content Architecture UX Model

## Product call
The UX should make Peninsula Insider feel easier to use, not simply fuller.

The new experience should help readers move through three modes:
- discover options
- choose a plan
- act on timely recommendations

## Navigation behaviour
Top-level navigation:
- Eat
- Stay
- Wine
- Explore
- Plans
- What's On
- Journal

### Navigation logic
- **Eat / Stay / Wine / Explore** = browse and discover
- **Plans** = decide quickly
- **What's On** = check now
- **Journal** = read and deepen trust

### Priority treatment
- Plans and What’s On should receive equal or greater visual emphasis than Journal in primary nav.
- What’s On should have a visible freshness signal.
- Plans should feel like a premium product layer, not just another category archive.

## Homepage hierarchy
The homepage should stop behaving like a mixed article feed.

### Recommended order
1. **Hero utility block:** What’s On This Weekend
2. **Featured Plan:** one clear recommended Peninsula day/weekend
3. **Seasonal signal block:** what matters right now
4. **Discovery pathways:** Eat / Stay / Wine / Explore cards
5. **Major editorial story:** one flagship Journal piece
6. **Curated live picks:** openings, events, live music, markets
7. **Plan shelf:** additional Plans by intent
8. **Trust / newsletter block:** subscribe for Peninsula picks

### Why this works
- immediately useful
- lower cognitive load
- clearly differentiated
- supports repeat visits
- lets editorial pieces earn attention instead of competing with utility noise

## Plans landing page pattern
Plans should feel like a decision engine.

### Above the fold
- confident section statement
- quick filters by context
- featured current plan

### Primary filters
- one day
- weekend
- family
- couples
- food & wine
- rainy day
- summer
- winter
- group getaway

### Core modules
1. Featured Plan
2. Seasonal Plans
3. Quick-answer Plans ("Just here for today? Start here")
4. By audience / group type
5. By weather / mood
6. By duration

### UX rule
Plans should compress overwhelm. Do not flood the page with dozens of near-identical itinerary cards.

## What's On landing page pattern
What’s On should feel current, selective, and alive.

### Above the fold
- headline with editorial positioning
- current date context
- immediate jump links

### Section structure
1. This Weekend
2. This Month
3. Seasonal Highlights
4. Major Events
5. Local Picks

### Supporting shelves
- Markets
- Live Music
- Food & Wine Events
- Openings
- School Holidays

### Card design logic
Each card should answer quickly:
- what it is
- why it matters
- when it matters
- who it suits

### UX rule
This is not a giant calendar. If an item is not worth a PI recommendation, it should not be surfaced here.

## Section page principles

### Eat / Stay / Wine / Explore
- cleaner taxonomy navigation
- stronger category pages
- less magazine-style clutter
- fast path to best options and dependable reference info

### Journal
- fewer but stronger story surfaces
- emphasis on voice, authority, and editorial framing
- less pressure to compete with live utility blocks

## Internal linking model
- reference pages link upward to Plans and What’s On where relevant
- Plans link out to reference pages for venues and places
- What’s On links to both Plans and reference pages when helpful
- Journal links contextually, not mechanically

This creates a real topic ecosystem rather than a pile of disconnected pages.

## Newsletter mapping
The architecture naturally creates three repeatable newsletter lanes:

### 1. What’s On lane
- weekend roundups
- openings
- timely events
- live picks

### 2. Plans lane
- save-this itinerary
- themed escape
- weather-based recommendation
- seasonal getaway

### 3. Journal lane
- deeper feature
- editor’s perspective
- comparison / local insight

Recommended mix per issue:
- 1 lead What’s On utility block
- 1 featured Plan
- 1 editorial story
- 3–5 quick supporting recommendations

## Social mapping
Social should follow defined content lanes instead of random promotion.

### Core lanes
- what’s on now
- save this plan
- local editorial take
- seasonal Peninsula recommendation

### Execution logic
- What’s On = urgency + relevance
- Plans = save/share value
- Journal = authority + voice
- Eat / Stay / Wine / Explore = evergreen discovery

## Key UX risks to avoid
1. Recreating the old overlap inside new labels
2. Making What’s On too broad and directory-like
3. Making Plans too list-heavy and not decisive enough
4. Letting the homepage slide back into a generic article feed
5. Overloading users with too many parallel entry points

## UX success condition
A new visitor should understand within seconds:
- where to browse
- where to get a recommendation
- where to check what’s happening now
- where to read PI’s editorial perspective

That clarity is the product improvement.
