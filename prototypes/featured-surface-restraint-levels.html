Level 1

Editorial Card

  • Creates a clear pause before the grid
  • Image gives visual weight comparable to editorial features
  • Verdict reads at length — enough space for PI voice
  • Two-column layout differentiates from venue cards below
  • Risk: could read as promoted content if the editorial quality dips

Best for: Long Lunch, Featured Cellar Door — surfaces where the venue warrants the editorial real estate and the verdict is the product.

Level 2

Editorial Strip

  • Horizontal band sits naturally between sections
  • Warm background distinguishes from hero without competing
  • Thumbnail image present but not dominant
  • Compact enough to feel like a note, not a feature
  • Works cleanly on mobile as a stacked card

Best for: Rainy Day Rescue, Slow Weekend, Plans surfaces — where the surface is guiding behaviour rather than profiling a single venue.

Level 3

Editorial Annotation

  • Maximum restraint — almost indistinguishable from editorial pagination
  • Trust signal: nothing is being promoted, just noted
  • Works best when the venue name has independent authority
  • Weakest commercial evolution path — too quiet to anchor a partner placement
  • Risk: may be missed by readers scanning quickly

Best for: What's On editorial signals, Journal crosslinks, or as a secondary surface within a page that already carries a Level 1 or 2 surface elsewhere.

Recommendation: Level 2 (Editorial Strip) as the primary system pattern — it has enough visual presence to shape reader behaviour, uses restraint appropriate to an editorial publication, and has a clean commercial evolution path (the warm band becomes the frame for partner context without redesigning the structure). Level 1 is reserved for flagship surfaces (Long Lunch, Cellar Door) where a single venue warrants the editorial real estate. Level 3 works as a secondary annotation layer, not a primary surface. The three levels can coexist: a hub page carries one Level 1 or Level 2 surface; editorial content elsewhere on the same page may carry Level 3 contextual annotations.
