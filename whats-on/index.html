The editorial promise

What we'd actually book, and what we'd flag early

Every other Peninsula calendar lists everything. Ours adds a point of view and practical context. Two lanes below shape every recommendation on this page.

Worth booking

Three lenses, one editorial standard.

Weekend Pick
What the editor would book this weekend if she could only book one. Under three picks per week, usually one.
Worth The Drive
The events worth the 90-minute return from Melbourne without any other reason. Genuine destination programming.
Family Saturday
A-graded kids days, no pre-show queues, no parking gymnastics, no padded lunch break. We've been to all of them.
Locals Know
Under-published picks. The recurring sessions, market stalls, and producer days the visitor feeds miss every time.
Free
Council-run walks, ranger programmes, and free public events that are genuinely worth the time, not filler.
Worth knowing

Short planning notes for the events that need context.

What we flag
Events that suit a narrow audience, get crowded quickly, or work best when you know the trade-offs before booking.
How we decide
A named editor attends or attended in person, or has direct reporting from a trusted Peninsula source. We do not add planning notes on hearsay.
Why we publish
Because readers need context, not just listings. Timing, access, and fit are often the difference between a good Peninsula day and a messy one.
Frequency
Only when the context materially changes how we would plan the day.
Recourse
If you run an event and think the context has changed, the contact form is open. We revise when the facts move.

Frequently asked

Four answers about how this calendar works

When does the Peninsula This Weekend dispatch publish?

Weekly, for the upcoming weekend (Saturday and Sunday). One pick, one weather-proof backup, and one useful planning note. Subscribers receive it by email; the latest is always pinned at the top of this page.

Are the kids-grade A/B/C ratings genuine?

Yes. We attend or have attended every family programme we grade. A means we'd return without hesitation. B means it works but has a flaw. C means we wouldn't go back. Grades are visible on every event with the family-saturday or school-holidays lens.

Why do some events carry a planning note?

Because the useful part of an events guide is context. When an event is likely to be crowded, logistically awkward, or a better fit for a different kind of visitor, we say so briefly and plainly so readers can plan around it.

Do you cover events outside the Peninsula?

No. Mornington Peninsula only, defined as Mount Martha down to Portsea, plus Hastings and the Western Port fringe. We don't cover Phillip Island, the Bellarine, or Frankston. The constraint is what makes the recommendations sharp.
