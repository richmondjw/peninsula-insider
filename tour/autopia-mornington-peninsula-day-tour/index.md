## What happens on this tour

Pickup from Melbourne CBD hotels and inner-city points, with multiple pickup locations meaning individual boarding times differ from the general departure time. The guide uses the transit to the Peninsula to introduce the wine regions, the Red Hill escarpment's role in cool-climate viticulture, and the day's itinerary. Three cellar door stops are the standard format, with the day structured around well-regarded producers that can manage the group size without tasting room bottlenecks. Lunch is at guest expense at a cellar door restaurant, with Autopia's scale allowing pre-arranged tables. A scenic stop at Arthurs Seat or a coastal viewpoint is typically included. Total duration is six to ten hours depending on the specific tour variant.

## Who this suits

International visitors staying across multiple Melbourne hotel precincts who need a reliable daily departure. Guests booking on shorter notice than the boutique operators can accommodate. Those who want polished logistics, consistent guiding, and a broad introduction to the Peninsula wine region without requiring a personalised itinerary.

## Frequently asked questions

### How does Autopia compare to a private tour at a similar price point?

At a comparable per-person price, private tour options such as Rick's Wine Tours offer an exclusive vehicle and guide at the cost of smaller operational infrastructure. Autopia offers polished logistics, daily departures, and one of the largest guide teams on the Peninsula circuit. The trade-off is reliability and frequency versus exclusivity and personalisation.

### Is this tour suitable for non-drinkers?

Non-drinkers can participate in the cellar door visits and enjoy the scenic stops, but the tour's core value proposition is wine tasting. The Peninsula format typically includes a scenic stop at Arthurs Seat or the coast, which has independent appeal. Whether the scenic and food components alone justify the fare is a question each guest should assess against their priorities.
