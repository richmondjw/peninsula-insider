## 1. Opening mood and feel

**The post-festival exhale.**

The three weekends before this one carried a programme. The June 6–8 long weekend. The Mornington Winter Music Festival. The Sorrento Solstice Festival on June 20–21. Each one gave visitors an anchor, a reason, a packed itinerary. This weekend has none of that.

That is the point.

June 27–28 is the Peninsula's first genuinely unscheduled winter weekend in over a month. No marquee event. No sold-out main session. No festival pressure on the cafes and cellar doors. This is the weekend where the winemaker may actually be behind the counter, where you get a table without a booking, where the walk down Main Ridge Road has no queue at the gate.

The editorial register: **calm usefulness.** This weekend is not about doing more. It is about doing two things well on a Peninsula that has quieted.

**Structural note for the draft:** Lead with the feeling, not with a list. One short paragraph establishes that the solstice weekend has passed and something different has opened up. Then move directly into the anchor. Do not list every option.

## 2. Anchor booking

**Peninsula Hot Springs Sunday Sessions — the final session**

- **When:** Sunday 28 June, live acoustic from noon (Bath House Café)
- **Access:** Included with any bathing session booking
- **Status:** Booking required (Peninsula Hot Springs website)
- **Why now:** This is the last Sunday Sessions of the winter run. The series has run every Sunday through June; June 28 is the close. After this, live music with bathing doesn't return until next season.

**The case for this as anchor:**

The urgency is real, not manufactured — there is a confirmed end date and no carryover. The format (live acoustic music, outdoor and semi-indoor Bath House Café, natural hot spring pools) is one of the most distinctly Peninsula experiences that runs across an afternoon. You don't need to rearrange a day around it: book the afternoon bathing session, arrive at the pools mid-morning, stay for the noon set, drift out at 2pm.

The emotional hook: this is the last chance to experience this version of a Peninsula Sunday in 2026.

## 3. Slower companion move

**MPRG Winter Exhibition — first full public weekend**

- **What:** Helen Britton: The Story So Far (nationally touring, Australian Design Centre "Living Treasures: Masters of Australian Craft" program) + Natalia Milosz-Piekarska: Sifted Light (Peninsula-inspired jewellery and sculpture)
- **When:** Saturday 27 or Sunday 28 June, 11:00am–4:00pm (Tue–Sun, closed Mon)
- **Where:** Mornington Peninsula Regional Gallery, Mornington
- **Cost:** Free
- **Status:** Walk-in, no booking required

**The case for this as companion move:**

The exhibition opened June 20 with a Friends of the Gallery launch. June 27–28 is the first full public weekend — no opening buzz, no preview crowd, just the work. Helen Britton is one of Australia's most significant working jewellers; this is a nationally touring show stopping at a regional gallery that almost never gets this calibre of work. Natalia Milosz-Piekarska's pieces are made from Peninsula-sourced materials. Free, two rooms, ninety minutes.

The natural companion loop: gallery (11am) + lunch on Mornington Main Street + walk to the foreshore. No bookings required for any of it. A 2.5–3 hour arc that composes into a full Saturday or Sunday morning.

**Structural note for the draft:** Present this as the "what to do if you're in Mornington" strand. Don't over-explain the exhibitions. One sentence each on Britton and Milosz-Piekarska — enough to tell the reader what they're walking into, not a review. The appeal is the loop, not the art criticism.

## 4. Local edge

**Bass & Flinders Gin Masterclass — the Peninsula's working distillery**

- **When:** Saturday 27 June (check website for session times)
- **Where:** Bass & Flinders Distillery, Dromana
- **Cost:** $175pp
- **Duration:** ~3 hours
- **Format:** Tour the still, learn the botanicals, blend your own gin, leave with a personalised 500ml bottle
- **Status:** Booking required (Bass & Flinders website)

**The case for this as local edge:**

Bass & Flinders is a working distillery, not a cellar door. The distinction matters: you are watching spirit being made, not tasting finished product in a tasting room. The masterclass format (hands-on, three hours, you leave with something you made) is one of a kind on the Peninsula. No equivalent experience exists in the wine trail format.

It almost never appears in Peninsula dispatches. It's not an event — it runs weekly — so it never surfaces in event-based calendars. For the food-and-drink reader who is not a wine drinker, or the reader who has done the cellar door circuit and wants something genuinely different, this is the answer.

Dromana context: combine with Dromana Community Market (Saturday morning, 9am–1pm, foreshore) for a complete Saturday without going over the hill.

**Structural note for the draft:** Keep this tight — two to three sentences. Name what it is (working distillery, not cellar door). Note the practical hook (you leave with something you made). One sentence on the Dromana pairing if space allows. No superlatives.
