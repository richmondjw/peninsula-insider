**Title:** Peninsula This Weekend — 23 to 24 May
**Seasonal position:** Late autumn. The deliberate lull between Mother's Day and King's Birthday. One week before truffle season opens.

**Organising idea for the draft:**
This is a quiet weekend in the best sense — not thin, but unhurried. The market circuit is dark (no Red Hill, no Boneo, no Emu Plains). The truffle season hasn't arrived yet. What's left is real: the mushroom season at its final peak, a gallery with two weeks of life left in it, heated water and live music, a chocolaterie in Flinders doing its last structured Sunday. ## Opening mood

Late May on the Peninsula: cool and overcast, probably 13-14°C, a chance of light rain on either day. The morning light is low and flat. The regular Saturday markets are dark this weekend — no Red Hill, no Boneo, no Emu Plains. The truffle season (Red Hill Truffles opens May 30) is sitting just past the edge of this weekend, close enough to feel.

What's active is seasonal in a more specific way. Wild mushrooms are at genuine peak. The Peninsula's gallery circuit has two weeks of closing pressure. Peninsula Hot Springs has live music in the water on both evenings. Flinders Chocolaterie has its final structured Sunday tasting.

This is a weekend that suits a deliberate pace. Not every option needs an entry ticket. Not every move needs a booking. But one does — and that one is worth securing before Friday.

## The anchor booking — lock this in

**Wild Mushroom Forage & Lunch with The Kitchen**
Saturday 23 May | 11:00–13:30 | $85pp | Bookings required | Max 15

The Kitchen runs guided mushroom foraging through May across the Peninsula. This Saturday is the penultimate session — one week before the season ends. Capacity is fifteen people; sessions have been running close to full.

The format: guided foraging in the field, then lunch built around what's found. Hands-on, seasonal, specific to this part of autumn. Not a cooking class. Not a farm tour. Something a little harder to categorise, which is part of the appeal.

If this session is sold out, the Mush-Meander (independent operator, Humanitix, 9:30am Saturday) is the active alternative — pricing and location to be confirmed in phase 3. But The Kitchen's forage-to-table format is the more complete experience.

**Book before Friday.** The final session (May 30) is more exposed to late bookings. This one, quieter in the calendar, may have a few spots left — or none.

## The companion move — slower, no booking needed

**New Wave 26 + Michael Vale at Mornington Peninsula Regional Gallery**
Saturday 23 or Sunday 24 May | 11:00–16:00 | Free | No booking

Both exhibitions close 31 May. This is the second-to-last weekend.

New Wave 26 is MPRG's annual survey of graduate-level contemporary art from Peninsula-adjacent institutions. Michael Vale's solo show runs alongside it. Together they make a deliberate gallery loop — not landmark exhibition territory, but genuinely curated and free, which removes a decision barrier.

The natural shape: arrive late morning, spend an hour through both shows, walk down Main Street Mornington for lunch or coffee, then either head south toward the Hot Springs or back through Balnarring toward Merricks. No timeline pressure. No crowd on a late May Saturday. The gallery is one of the Peninsula's most underused assets in the dispatch context — this closing window is the right moment to surface it.

Works across both days. Works as a rainy-day fallback. Works for two people who don't want to plan much beyond mid-morning.

## The local edge — one thing most visitors won't know

**Rocky Road Festival — Final Sunday Tasting Sessions at Flinders Chocolaterie**
Sunday 24 May | Sessions: 11:00, 12:00, 13:30 | $28pp + booking fee | Bookings required

The Rocky Road Festival's free general entry runs until 31 May — but the structured tasting sessions (sample eight flavour variations, make your own rocky road) run Sundays only through May. May 24 is the last one.

Flinders Chocolaterie is a short drive south of Red Hill, and Flinders itself is underused as a Peninsula editorial destination. The tasting session is the experience worth booking; the free festival drop-in is fine but thinner. On the last Sunday before the format wraps, this is a natural reason to be in Flinders — and Flinders Sourdough (a short walk) is worth a stop before or after.

This works particularly well if Sunday is the more flexible day — no mushroom forage, no gallery urgency. Families, couples, anyone who wants a reason to be in Flinders rather than Red Hill.

## Evening texture

**Saturday: Chloe Gill at Peninsula Hot Springs**
From 19:00 | Complimentary with bathing | Amphitheatre pool, Fingal

PHS has been quietly running live music through late autumn. Saturday evening is a newer format — contemporary folk, in the water. Entry cost folds into bathing, so there's no separate ticket barrier. Works as a natural close to a Saturday that's already been in the paddock or the gallery.

This is an under-published strand of what PHS is doing this season. Worth naming without over-weighting it — it's texture for Saturday evening, not the lead reason to go.

**Sunday: Peninsula Hot Springs Sunday Sessions**
14:00–17:00 | Complimentary with bathing | Amphitheatre pool

Same format, Sunday afternoon. The Sunday Sessions series runs May-June. If the mushroom forage is Saturday, this slots cleanly into Sunday afternoon as a slower close.

## What's not happening — worth one line

The Peninsula's Saturday market circuit is dark this weekend: no Red Hill, no Boneo, no Emu Plains, no Pearcedale. The one market running is Tootgarook Primary School Market (07:30–12:30, 250 stalls, community-scale). For visitors building a market-then-winery Saturday, the usual shape doesn't apply. The dispatch should acknowledge this without apology — the quieter weekend has its own logic.
