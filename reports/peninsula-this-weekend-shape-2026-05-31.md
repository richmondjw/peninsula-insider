## Weekend mood and opening frame

This is the loudest editorial weekend of the Peninsula winter, and the dispatch's job is to refuse to amplify it. King's Birthday is when the cellars are open at once, the music festival is running across twenty Mornington venues, the truffle season is in full swing on both ridges, and the long weekend brings down Melbourne. The instinct of a generic round-up is to list everything. The instinct of the Peninsula is to pick two things and protect the gaps between them.

Weather is true early-June: 13–14°C by day, 6–8°C overnight, real chance of rain across all three days. None of it matters. Everything worth doing this weekend is built for fires, indoors, pavilions, or thermal water. The contingency is volume, not weather.

The opening register: confident, observational, slightly contrarian. A weekend that knows what it is, and a dispatch that knows the reader is being pulled in twenty directions. The move is to narrow, not widen.

## Four-slot dispatch structure

### 1. Opening feel — Pick two, not ten

The Peninsula has its single biggest winter weekend ahead. Saturday is Winter Wine Festival at Red Hill Showgrounds; the same three days carry the 12th Mornington Winter Music Festival, the truffle season at Red Hill and Flinders, Stonier's Pies & Pinot under fire, Crittenden's King's Birthday tastings, and forty-plus cellar doors open across both days. The mistake — and most readers will be tempted into it — is treating the weekend like a checklist.

The frame is the opposite. Lock in one anchor. Add one quieter companion move. Leave the rest of the weekend open. The Peninsula handles long weekends best when the visitor plans loosely, not tightly. Saturday will be crowded; Sunday is where the weekend actually settles.

**Tone guide:** Calm, slightly editorial. Don't announce the festival as if the reader hasn't heard. Assume they know it's a long weekend. Write to a reader who has the calendar already open and needs a reason to close it.

**What:** Stonier Pies & Pinot — annual King's Birthday weekend, Merricks
**When:** Sunday 7 June, 11:00–17:00 (also Sat + Mon)
**Price:** Free entry. Pies $12–18, wines by glass and bottle
**Booking:** None. Walk-in. Family-friendly, pet-friendly.
**Source:** `stonier-pies-pinot-king-s-birthday-weekend.json` + `stonier-vineyard-tours-new-release-pinot-tasting.json`

**Why it's the companion move:** This is the weekend's calmest hour and the dispatch's contrarian read. Sunday at Merricks is when the festival pressure releases, the fires are settled, the lawn is open, the pies come out of the oven warm. Crucially, Sunday is also the first weekend the new-release 2025 Pinot Noir is poured publicly — winemakers themselves running the tastings across the long weekend. For the reader who'd rather have one hour with a glass than four hours with a crowd, this is the move.

The play: Showgrounds on Saturday, Stonier on Sunday. The festival gives the breadth; Stonier on the slower day gives the depth and the new vintage. Most visitors will only do Saturday and call the weekend done. The local read is to keep Sunday for Merricks.

**Tone guide:** Understated, plain-spoken. Don't oversell. "Free entry, fires lit, the new Pinot is out" is enough. The reader fills in the rest.
